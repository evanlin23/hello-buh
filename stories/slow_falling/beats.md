# slow_falling: beat sheet, clue map, writer's checks (r1)

r1 (2026-11-10) applies the critic's fix for parked pitch #4 ("fails why?: if she climbs up, why not down?"): the
only way up is Grandpa's "fountain", a soul-sand bubble column that only lifts, shown in S01 and named in L04; Grandpa
brews her slow falling every night at a brewing stand in the cellar (the narrator's "night tea" at the "tea machine");
the kid catches it with his own hands (takes one, rides up, drinks it, steps off) and the game names it.

## The ending first
The reveal frame: a wide over Grandpa's shoulder on the market bench, looking up at Grandma and the kid floating down
side by side past the glass column, swirls on both, the windmill on the cliff above. Just before it, the game says what
the "tea" is: "Potion of Slow Falling" over the hotbar, the effect icon, the slow drop with no damage.
The first frame it re-explains: Grandma at the gap in the windmill's balcony rail, lowering a peach bottle and stepping
off. She isn't brave or magic: every one of those 11,000 steps was a bottle Grandpa brewed the night before.

## Spine
- **Every day,** Grandma rides Grandpa's fountain to the top of the windmill, drinks the juice he packed her, steps off
  and floats down to the market, sure she's light as a feather; every night Grandpa sits at his stinky tea machine.
- **One day,** on his birthday, the kid takes one forbidden tea while Grandpa dozes, and next morning rides up behind
  Grandma because he wants to fly too.
- **Because of that,** he's stuck at the top of a fountain that only goes up, and sees her juice is the same color as
  his tea.
- **Until finally,** he drinks it and steps off, the game names it (Potion of Slow Falling), he floats down beside
  Grandma, she beams that he got it from her, and he tells us who really gave her wings.

## "Why?" interrogation
- **Why does Grandma go up every morning?** She loves to fly. It's her morning, for thirty years.
- **Why does she jump instead of climbing down?** There's no way down: the fountain is a bubble column and only lifts
  (L04, S04), and the windmill has no ladder or stairs (set: the piece's inside ladder is removed).
- **Why did Grandpa build only up?** "She was born to fly" (L05): he gave her a reason to fly every day.
- **Why doesn't she die?** The juice is slow falling (the twist). Without it the drop is 23 blocks: lethal.
- **Why doesn't Grandma know?** It comes as juice from Grandpa every morning; she never skipped one. She believes she's
  light as a feather (L02) and that it runs in the family (L12).
- **Why does Grandpa keep it secret?** So it stays true for her. The bible's backstory; the script lets L05 carry it.
- **Why brew every night if a stand makes three?** He keeps a shelf of spares (S06 shows it) and has never let it run
  low. Every night is his habit. (Also: why the kid's theft doesn't leave Grandma without one.)
- **Why does the kid take the tea?** It's his birthday and it's the one thing he's not allowed (L06-L07). A treat, not
  a plan: he doesn't connect it to flying yet.
- **Why does he ride up?** He wants to fly too (L08), and nobody's watching: Grandpa is at the bakery stall with his
  back turned (bible), Grandma is above him, facing up.
- **Why doesn't Grandma see him in the column?** She's above him, facing up, and the ride takes about 2 s.
- **Why does he step off a 23-block drop?** He's stuck (L09) and he has just worked out the juice and his tea are the
  same (L10), and then watched her float. The "So" says it.
- **Why doesn't Grandma stop him?** She has already stepped off; she sees him only in the air (S12).
- **Why is Grandpa on the bench at the end?** He watches her land every morning (S05).

## The out-of-context read (each line, 3 s, a stranger)
- L01-L05: a funny grandma story. L06 "I'm not allowed any" (tea): fine. L07 "he fell asleep at the tea machine. I took
  ONE.": a kid sneaking a tea; it says "tea machine" in the same breath, so it never reads as alcohol. No adult hands
  the kid anything in secret (r1 dropped a "don't tell your grandma" bribe beat for exactly this reason). L11 "So I
  drank mine and stepped off too": played bright, mid-air with Grandma in the next frame; never "jumped" from a kid.
- No hushed lines anywhere: every direction says full volume.
