# goat_horn: beat sheet, clue map, writer's checks (r1)

r1 (2026-11-10) applies the critic's pitch fixes (writers' room rank 3, "develop, just"):
- why Dad must be callable is planted twice: the packed donkey and the map (S06), "the far MINES, ALL winter" (L06),
  and Dad's "can't go without ONE thing" (L07), misread as a grudge ("REVENGE").
- Dad's plan is shown, not explained: he tries to step aside at the last second so the goat hits the oak, and he is
  too slow (L03, S03).
- the horn rule has a fair plant in the picture and a line: Ida's goat horn in an item frame by her door, and "a goat
  MISSED her once" (L05, S05), misread as a fib ("Goats don't MISS").
- the turn is the kid's own act (he takes Dad's spot and steps on the goat's tell), "GOAT HORN" is a big caption over
  the pickup, and the last beat is the horn blown and Dad turning on the far road.

## The ending first
The reveal frame (S13, the payoff): on a lakeside road far from home, a man and a packed donkey walking toward us stop.
A goat horn's call carries down the valley. The man turns his back to us to face a tiny cliff with a big oak on top,
125 blocks away, and waves. The first frame it re-explains: S01, a goat knocking that same man off that cliff. 212
knocks were 212 tries at the horn that just called him back.

## Spine
- **Every day,** a goat rams Dad off the cliff into the lake, and he climbs back up and stands in front of it again,
  one step in front of the big oak, trying to dodge at the last second. He's too slow.
- **One day,** his donkey is packed for the far mines, all winter, and he says he can't go without one thing. I figure
  it's revenge. This morning he leaves anyway.
- **Because of that,** I take his spot for him. I've watched it 213 times: when the goat drops its head, I step.
- **Until finally,** the goat hits the oak, out pops a goat horn, I blow it, and way down the road Dad turns around.

## "Why?" interrogation
- **Why does Dad stand in front of the goat?** He needs it to charge so he can step aside and let it hit the oak. A horn
  only comes from a ram into a solid block (vanilla), and the oak is the only block on the goat's shelf.
- **Why the cliff edge?** The oak grows on the lip, and the goat lives on the shelf. The ram line runs to the oak, so
  the drop is right behind him. He picked the spot for the tree, not the fall.
- **Why doesn't he get hurt?** He's a player and lands in deep water (no fall damage). S02 shows the splash and him
  swimming to the stairs.
- **Why is he too slow?** He steps after the goat lowers its head and starts the charge. The kid steps *on* the tell.
  S03 shows Dad's step a beat late.
- **Why not kill the goat, or lead it to a wall on a lead?** A dead goat drops no horn. A lead: Dad does things the hard
  way, and the shelf is reached only by the narrow cliff stairs (no room to lead a goat down). Bible note only.
- **Why doesn't Dad just say what it's for?** It's a surprise for his kid. "One thing" is all he gives away.
- **Why does Dad leave without it?** The mines won't wait. The donkey is packed (S06).
- **Why does the kid take his spot?** He thinks Dad's leaving sad over a grudge, so he finishes it "FOR him". If he
  fails he lands in the lake like Dad.
- **Why can the kid dodge when Dad can't?** He has watched 213 tries from below and knows the tell.
- **Why does Dad turn?** A goat horn is heard 256 blocks away. He's ~125 blocks off (S13), and nobody else in the
  valley has a horn but Ida, whose horn hangs on her wall.
- **Why does Ida have a horn?** A goat once rammed her wall instead of her (she says so). Villagers don't blow horns: it
  hangs in a frame.
