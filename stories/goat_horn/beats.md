r2 (2026-11-10) applies the r1 critic's and continuity's must-fixes (reviews/r1-response.md). The main changes:
- L03 no longer gives away the plan: "He tries to DODGE. Too SLOW." The oak behind him is in the picture only.

- L12-L13 make the ending explain itself: "It was for ME. To CALL him." / "I BLASTED it. Down the ROAD, Dad turned and

- The count is 213 everywhere. The kid can dodge because he has watched all 213 tries (L10).

## The ending first
The reveal frame (S13, the payoff): on a lakeside road far from home, a man and a packed donkey walk toward us. A goat
horn's call carries down the valley. He stops, turns his back to us to face a cliff with a big oak on top, 125 blocks
away (centred behind him), and waves. The first frame it re-explains is S01, where a goat knocks that same man off that
cliff. The 213 knocks were 213 tries at the horn that just called him.

## Spine
- **Every day,** a goat rams Dad off the cliff into the lake. He climbs back up and stands in front of it again, one
  step in front of the big oak, trying to dodge at the last second. He's too slow.
- **One day,** his donkey is packed for the mines, all winter, and he says he can't go till he gets me one thing. I
  figure he means a win. This morning he leaves anyway.
- **Because of that,** I take his spot. I've watched 213 times: when the goat drops its head, I step.
- **Until finally,** the goat hits the oak and out pops a goat horn. It was for me, to call him. I blow it, and down
  the road Dad turns and waves.

## "Why?" interrogation
- **Why does Dad stand in front of the goat?** He needs it to charge so he can step aside and let it hit the oak. A
  horn only comes from a ram into a solid block (vanilla), and the oak is the only block on the goat's shelf.
- **Why the cliff edge?** The oak grows on the lip and the goat lives on the shelf. The ram line runs into the oak, so
  the drop is right behind him. He picked the spot for the tree, not the fall.
- **Why doesn't he get hurt?** He's a player and lands in deep water (no fall damage). S02 shows the splash.
- **Why is he too slow?** He steps after the charge starts. The kid steps on the tell (the head going down). S03 shows
  Dad's step a beat late.
- **Why can the kid dodge when Dad can't?** "I'd watched 213 TIMES" (L10). He knows the tell.
- **Why not kill the goat, or lead it to a wall on a lead?** A dead goat drops no horn. A lead: the shelf is reached
  only by the narrow cliff stairs, with no room to lead a goat down. Bible note only.
- **Why doesn't Dad say what it's for?** It's a present. "ONE thing" is all he gives away.
- **Why does Dad leave without it?** The mines won't wait. The donkey is packed (S06).
- **Why does the kid take his spot?** To get Dad the win he thinks Dad wanted. If he fails, he lands in the lake like
  Dad.
- **Why does Dad turn?** A goat horn is heard 256 blocks away, and he's ~125 off (S13). Ida's horn hangs on her wall,
  so only one horn in the valley gets blown.
- **Why does Ida have a horn?** A goat once rammed her wall instead of her (she says so). Villagers don't blow horns, so
  it hangs in a frame.

## The out-of-context read
Here is each line as a stranger would hear it for 3 s. L01-L03 are a goat and a cliff, and L04 is a score. L05 is
gossip about a horn, L06 a trip, and L07 "gets me one thing / a win" is plainly a present. "He left anyway" is sad,
not intimate, and "took his spot" sounds like a game. "Dropped its head / I stepped" is action and "BONK" is comic.
"It was for me. To call him." has the horn in frame. "I blasted it" refers to a horn on screen ("blew it" was cut for
this test). "Dad turned and waved" is plain. Nothing reads as intimate, obedient or innuendo. Nothing is hushed: Pip's
recipe direction says "not whispery, breathy or soft".
