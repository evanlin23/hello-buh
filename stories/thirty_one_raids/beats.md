# thirty_one_raids: beat sheet and clue map

**Logline.** A boy's grandpa has saved their village from thirty-one raids, and every one started the night he came
home; when Grandpa is too tired to fight, the boy drinks his medicine to be brave like him.

**The reveal frame (written first).** At the village gate at dusk, the boy has just drunk the grey bottle. The raid
horn sounds; over the ridge come torches, white flags, pillagers and a ravager. He turns: Grandpa is behind him,
holding out his crossbow. "Now they'll love YOU too, kid."

**The first frame it re-explains.** Villagers throwing bread at Grandpa as he crosses the square at dawn: "Grandpa has
saved our village from THIRTY-ONE RAIDS." After the twist: he started all thirty-one, because being their saviour is
the only way anyone is glad to see him. And the boy is about to start number thirty-two.

**Spine.**
1. Every raid, Grandpa saves the village, and every raid starts the night he comes home.
2. One day Grandpa is too old and tired to fight, and the raiders "always come" when he's home.
3. Because of that, the boy drinks Grandpa's medicine at the gate, to be brave like him.
4. Until finally the raid horn sounds the moment he drinks, and Grandpa hands him the crossbow.

**The vanilla rules it runs on:**
- A raid captain (the pillager with the banner, at outposts and on patrols) drops an **ominous bottle**. Drinking it
  gives **Bad Omen**; walking into a village with Bad Omen turns it into Raid Omen, and 30 seconds later **a raid
  starts** with a horn.
- Win a raid and you get **Hero of the Village**: villagers throw you gifts (farmers throw bread) and give discounts.
- Evokers come in raids and drop **totems of undying**.

**Where the mystery lives.** A Minecraft player suspects Bad Omen early (L02, by design: like runaway's hissing, the
viewer is ahead of the narrator). The question for them becomes *does Grandpa know?* The clues answer it step by step
(a whole chest of bottles, he drinks at the gate, he knows when they're coming, he's lonely), and the last line adds
the turn nobody saw: he's passing it on. For a viewer who doesn't know the bottle, L12 to S13 is the reveal, shown by
the game (drink, horn, raid).

## Twist checklist (docs/story_craft.md section 3)

- [x] **Changes line 1.** "Grandpa has saved our village from 31 raids": he caused all 31.
- [x] **Fair play.** The bottle, the chest, the gate, the timing and the motive are all on screen before S13. The reveal
  adds no fact: the boy does what Grandpa does, and we see the result.
- [x] **Planted twice, one in the picture.** The bottle (S08 picture, L08-L09 lines, S09 picture); the tower (S02, S07);
  the empty frame (S05).
- [x] **Misread, not hidden.** The boy reads every clue as heroism or grief.
- [x] **Someone gets ahead.** Players at L02 (mechanism) and L08-L09 (intent); everyone at L12.
- [x] **The game says it.** Drink, horn, a raid over the ridge: vanilla, readable at phone size (an EWS of torches and
  banners, plus the horn).
- [x] **One step.** The medicine brings the raids. Grandpa's line adds intent in five words; it doesn't explain the
  mechanism.
- [x] **Readable at 300 px.** S13 is a sky-and-ridge shot full of moving torches and flags; the horn carries it without
  the picture.
- [x] **Visible causes.** No accidents: every raid has a cause we see (the bottle). The boy chooses to drink.
- [x] **A rewatch plays differently.** L01 (a confession), L04 ("They know who BEATS them"), L06 (the motive), L10
  ("They're COMING": not a guess), S05's empty frame.

**Risks for the table read.** (1) Tone: Grandpa endangers the village on purpose. Keep it quiet and human (loneliness),
never villainous; nobody dies on screen; L01's smoke is the only damage shown. (2) Players may call the mechanism at
L02: that's intended (dramatic irony), so the critic should score the intent turn, not the bottle. (3) The ominous
banner draws white without its pattern (renderer): the bible calls it "white flags" so the picture and the words match.
