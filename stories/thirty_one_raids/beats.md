# thirty_one_raids: beat sheet and clue map (r2)

r2 (2026-11-10) applies the r1 critic's must-fixes: the hook is the totem wall (31 totems and one empty frame) and the
bread moves to its own beat (L05); the bottle is "his COURAGE" (L08, L12), so the boy drinks it to fight, not to be
like him; "Tonight he came home too TIRED to fight" (L11) keeps the rule that raids come when he's home; the raid
comes close (pillagers and a ravager up the road, 15-25 blocks, the horn; no torches, no banners); Grandpa is a
default-skin player actor; and in the last shot Grandpa stands beside the boy loading his own crossbow. Should-fixes:
the motive line (L10) now comes after "They're COMING", and the final night is visibly different (the bench, the
undrunk bottle). Lines are renumbered in the new order.

**Logline.** A boy's grandpa has saved their village from thirty-one raids, and every one started the night he came
home; the night Grandpa comes home too tired to fight, the boy drinks Grandpa's "courage" himself.

**The reveal frame (written first).** At the village gate at dusk, the boy has just drunk the grey bottle. The raid
horn; up the road come five pillagers and a ravager. He turns: Grandpa is beside him at the gate post, loading his
own crossbow. "Now they'll love YOU too, kid."

**The first frame it re-explains.** Grandpa hanging the 31st totem on a wall of frames, one frame left empty:
"Grandpa has saved our village from THIRTY-ONE RAIDS." After the twist: he started all thirty-one, because being their
saviour is the only time anyone is glad to see him; the empty frame is the 32nd, and it's the boy's raid.

**Spine.**
1. Every raid, Grandpa saves the village, and every raid starts the night he comes home.
2. One night Grandpa comes home too tired to fight, and the boy is sure the raid will come anyway.
3. Because of that, the boy drinks Grandpa's courage at the gate, so somebody can fight.
4. Until finally the raid horn sounds, and Grandpa gets up and stands beside him, loading his crossbow, glad.

**The vanilla rules it runs on:**
- Pillager captains (at outposts and on patrols) drop an **ominous bottle**. Drinking it gives **Bad Omen**; a player
  with Bad Omen in a village gets Raid Omen, and 30 s later **a raid starts** with the horn.
- Win a raid and you get **Hero of the Village**: villagers throw you gifts (farmers throw bread).
- Evokers come in raids and drop **totems of undying**.

**Where the mystery lives.** A Minecraft player suspects Bad Omen early (L02 and the tower; like runaway's hissing, the
viewer is ahead of the narrator). The question for them becomes *does Grandpa know?* The clues answer it step by step
(a whole chest of bottles, he drinks at the gate, he knows when they're coming, why he'd want to), and the last line
adds the turn: he's glad the boy did it, and he fights beside him. For a viewer who doesn't know the bottle, L12 to S13
is the reveal, shown by the game (drink, horn, raiders).

## Twist checklist (docs/story_craft.md section 3)

- [x] **Changes line 1.** "Grandpa has saved our village from 31 raids": he caused all 31, and the empty frame is next.
- [x] **Fair play.** The bottle, the chest, the gate, the timing and the motive are all on screen before S13. The reveal
  adds no fact: the boy does what Grandpa does, and we see the result.
- [x] **Planted twice, one in the picture.** The bottle (S07 and S08 pictures, L07-L08 lines, S11 undrunk); the tower
  (S02, S06); the empty frame (S01).
- [x] **Misread, not hidden.** The boy reads every clue as heroism, courage or grief.
- [x] **Someone gets ahead.** Players at L02 (mechanism) and L07-L08 (intent); everyone at L12.
- [x] **The game says it.** Drink, horn, raiders up the road: vanilla, readable at phone size (a ravager and five
  pillagers at 15-25 blocks, the horn).
- [x] **One step.** The courage brings the raids. Grandpa's line adds intent in five words; it doesn't explain the
  mechanism.
- [x] **Readable at 300 px.** S13 closes from 25 to 15 blocks so the ravager is a clear shape by its last frame; the
  horn carries it without the picture.
- [x] **Visible causes.** No accidents: every raid has a cause we see (the bottle). The boy chooses to drink, for a
  reason he says ("Somebody had to FIGHT").
- [x] **A rewatch plays differently.** L01 (a confession), L04 ("They know who BEATS them"), L08 ("his COURAGE"),
  L09 ("They're COMING": not a guess), L10 (the motive), L11 (he chose not to drink), S01's empty frame.

## "Why?" interrogation
- Why do raids start the night he comes home? He drinks the bottle at the gate (L08).
- Why would he? L10: since Grandma died, a raid is the only time anyone is glad to see him.
- Why call it courage? It's what he drinks before every fight; true, and a lie.
- Why does the boy drink it? Raids always come the night Grandpa comes home (L02); tonight he came home (L11) and
  can't fight; so a raid is coming and "Somebody had to FIGHT" (L12). The courage is what Grandpa drinks before a fight.
- Why didn't Grandpa drink it tonight? Too tired: no drink, no raid. The bottle sits undrunk in his hand (S11); that's
  where the boy gets it.
- Why is Grandpa at the gate in S14? The horn woke him; he won't let the boy fight alone, and he's glad (L13).
- Why does Grandpa know the boy drank it? The horn: he's heard it thirty-one times.

## Delivery
- Pauses: before the number (L01), before "it's the only time" (L10), before "Somebody had to FIGHT" (L12), silence
  under the horn, a breath before "kid" (L13).
- Quotable line: "Now they'll love YOU too, kid."
- Tone: Grandpa is lonely, not a villain: no deaths on screen; the smoking roof (S05) is the only damage shown.
