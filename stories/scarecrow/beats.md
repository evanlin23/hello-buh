# scarecrow: beat sheet, clue map, writer's checks (r2)

r2 (2026-11-10) applies the r1 critic's and continuity's must-fixes (reviews/r1-response.md lists each one). The main
changes:
- The white body stays out of frame until Mom's line names it.
- L06 is now "out of SNOW".
- The voice uses Pip's recipe exactly.
- "GONE" gets its own lit full shot (S10b).
- The reveal starts in the POV, looking down at the zombies he never knew were there, and then pulls out to the golem.

Narrator only (Pip's voice, cast `narrator`). Mom is quoted with "said" and has no voiced line.

**The one deviation from the r0 pitch fix, kept on purpose:** vanilla snowballs leave no mark on blocks, so there are
no splats on the wall. The stalker misread comes from what the game does leave behind:
- the trampled patch where the golem stands and throws,
- the rotten flesh from zombies that burned at sunrise,
- the groans.

## The ending first
**The reveal (S11, night).** The shot starts in the boy's eye at the open window, looking straight down at three
zombies at the foot of his wall, arms up, faces up at him. These are what he heard every night, and they were never in
the field. Then it pulls out and up:
- his window, with four shutters open and his head in it,
- the zombies under it,
- and 8 blocks out on its trampled white patch, the snow golem with its pumpkin head, throwing snowballs.

Every hit is a white puff, and each zombie is shoved along the wall away from the window. The line over it: "It
wasn't STARING at me. It was keeping an EYE OUT for me."

**The first frame it re-explains (S01).** A morning push from behind a pumpkin in the wheat (no body in frame), down a
white trail across green grass to a shuttered window. The stalker's path was a guard's beat.

## Spine
- **Every day,** for 63 mornings, a trail of snow runs from Grandpa's scarecrow to the boy's window on a hill farm where it
  never snows. He shovels it, it comes back, and it ends where someone seems to stand all night staring at him.
- **One day,** after the groaning he never looks at and the rotten flesh "gifts", he remembers two things. Mom said
  Grandpa built the scarecrow out of snow the week before he died. And Grandpa always said he'd keep an eye on him.
- **Because of that,** he stays up to catch his stalker, and when the groaning comes he throws the window open.
- **Until finally,** the scarecrow is gone from the wheat. He looks down to find zombies under his window and the
  snow golem pelting them off his wall. It was never staring at him. It was keeping an eye out for him.

## Why the reveal is a surprise
The viewer has been waiting for the stalker at the window, and so has he. S10 shows nothing at the window. S10b shows
nothing in the field. S11's first frame then shows the real thing, which was right under him all along: three zombies
looking up. A player who guessed "snow golem" at S08 still meets the zombies for the first time here. One more half-second
pulls out to the golem, and the stalker turns into the guard.

## The "why?" interrogation
- **Why snow, where it never snows?**
  - A snow golem places a snow layer at its feet as it walks, wherever the biome is cooler than 0.8.
  - The farm is on a hill at y 90+, where plains cools to about 0.77 (temperature falls with height above y 80): cool
    enough for a golem's snow, far too warm for snowfall (under 0.15).
  - It leaves no snow on farmland or in the wheat, so the trail starts at the field's edge.
- **Why does the trail end 8 blocks from the window?** The golem walks until it is within 10 blocks of its target and
  can see it, then stops and throws.
- **Why do the zombies come to my wall?**
  - Zombies hunt players within 35 blocks, and I'm the player.
  - They crowd the outside wall nearest me.
  - My window starts 3 blocks above the ground outside, so they can't get in.
- **Why don't the snowballs kill them?** Snowballs only knock zombies back. The sun burns them at dawn, which is where
  the rotten flesh comes from.
- **Why did he never say it's made of snow?** He does, through Mom (L06). He reads it as a snowman scarecrow that
  walks, which is scarier, not safer.
- **Why does Mom not know it's a golem?** She knows Grandpa built a snowman scarecrow in his field. Nobody has gone
  into that field since he died, and from the porch it's a pumpkin over the wheat.
- **Why did Grandpa build it?** He knew he was dying, and that zombies came for the boy's wall at night.
- **Why does the boy wait 63 nights, and why open the window?**
  - He was scared ("I don't LOOK").
  - The wooden shutters can't be seen through.
  - He decides to catch it, and opening them is his choice.
- **Why is the trail there again after he shoveled it?** He shovels all of it each morning (S03 shows two blocks of
  that). The golem lays it fresh on its walk the next night.
- **Why is the golem back in the wheat by morning?**
  - Vanilla gives it no reason to go home.
  - Once the last zombie burns, it wanders. The yard and the field are the open ground on that side of the hill.
  - The story shows one morning (the 63rd) with it standing at its spot. It never claims it returns there every day.
  - The bible logs this as a known stretch for the minecraft-audit.

## The out-of-context read (3 s, sound on, a stranger)
- "Like someone STOOD there, STARING at me." Creepy, about a window.
- "He always SAID he'd keep an EYE on me. Kind of CREEPY now." A kid's joke.
- "It wasn't STARING at me. It was keeping an EYE OUT for me." Warm.

Nothing reads as intimate, obedient or ASMR. Every direction says full volume, including L11 ("warm and bright, a
smile in it, full volume, never soft, whispered or breathy").
