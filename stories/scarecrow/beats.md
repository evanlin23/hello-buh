# scarecrow: beat sheet, clue map, writer's checks (r1)

r1 (2026-11-10): written from the critic's parked pitch 5 ("Scarecrow") with its fix applied: the opening reads as a
stalker (line 1, the trail that ends where "someone stood all night staring at my window", the groaning, the "gift"),
and the reveal turns the stalker into a guard. Narrator only (Pip's voice, cast `narrator`); Mom is quoted with "said"
and has no voiced line.

**One deviation from the critic's fix, on purpose.** The fix asked for snowball splats on my window and wall every
morning. Vanilla snowballs leave no mark on blocks (a snowball breaks into 8 item particles that last under a second),
so a splat on the wall would be un-vanilla. The same misread is carried by things the game does leave: the snow
trail that ends in a trampled patch facing my window (a snow golem stands and shuffles where it shoots from), the
rotten flesh under the window (zombies that burned at sunrise), and the groans at night. The snowballs themselves are
seen once, at the reveal, as white puffs on the zombies.

## The ending first
The reveal frame (S11, night, wide, pulling back): our cottage's back wall with the shutters open and Milo's head in
the window; three zombies at the foot of the wall under it, arms up; eight blocks out, on its trampled white patch, a
snow golem with a carved pumpkin head throwing snowballs at them, white puffs on every hit, each zombie shoved back
along the wall; its snow trail runs from its feet back to the empty wheat. The line over it: "Grandpa was STILL keeping
an EYE on me."

The first frame it re-explains (S01): the back of the scarecrow's pumpkin in the wheat, and a white trail of snow
across green grass, running from it to my window, in the morning sun. After the reveal the trail is a guard's beat.

## Spine
- **Every day,** for 63 mornings, a trail of snow runs from Grandpa's scarecrow to the boy's window in a meadow where it
  never snows; he shovels it, it comes back, it ends where someone seems to stand all night staring at his window.
- **One day,** after the groaning he never looks at and the rotten flesh "gifts", he remembers Mom said Grandpa built
  the scarecrow the week before he died, and that Grandpa always said he'd keep an eye on him.
- **Because of that,** he stays up to catch his stalker, and when the groaning comes he opens the window.
- **Until finally,** the scarecrow is gone from the wheat, and the wide shows it at his wall: a snow golem pelting the
  zombies off his window. Grandpa was still keeping an eye on him.

## The "why?" interrogation
- **Why snow, where it never snows?** A snow golem places a snow layer under itself as it walks wherever the biome is
  cooler than 0.8; the farm sits in a meadow (0.5), where rain falls but snow never does (snowfall needs under 0.15).
  It leaves none on the farmland or in the wheat (snow needs a full block top and an empty cell), so the trail starts
  at the field's edge.
- **Why does the trail end by my window and not at the wall?** The golem walks until it is within 10 blocks of its
  target and can see it, then stops and throws. The zombies stand under my window; the golem stops 8 blocks out and
  shuffles there all night: the trampled patch.
- **Why do the zombies come to my wall?** Zombies hunt players within 35 blocks. I'm the player (Milo, a player kid in
  a villager family); they crowd the outside wall nearest to me, under my bedroom window. My window's opening starts 3
  blocks above the ground outside (the house steps down the slope), so they can't climb in.
- **Why don't the zombies just die from the snowballs?** Snowballs do no damage to zombies, only knockback. The golem
  keeps shoving them off until sunrise, and the sun burns them: the rotten flesh.
- **Why doesn't the golem melt or wander off?** It's a snow golem in a cool biome; it wanders in the daytime and the
  story only ever shows it in the wheat in the morning (staged at its spot). Rain would hurt it: the story's weather is
  clear (see the bible's risk note).
- **Why did Grandpa build it, and why didn't he say?** He knew he was dying and that zombies come for the boy's wall
  every night; he kept his promise the way a farmer would: something that stands in the field. He built it the week
  before he died; there was no time to explain, and Mom only knows "he built the scarecrow".
- **Why doesn't Mom know it's a golem?** Nobody has gone into Grandpa's field since he died (the wheat stands ripe and
  unharvested); from the porch it's a pumpkin over the wheat.
- **Why did the boy not look for 63 nights?** He was scared ("I don't LOOK").
- **Why does he stay up last night?** Sixty-three mornings of it, and the creepy "gifts": he decides to catch it (his
  choice, "So").
- **Why open the window rather than peek?** The shutters are wood: you can't see through them. He chooses to.
- **Why does he shovel it?** To see if it comes back (it's how he knows the count is 63).

## The out-of-context read (3 s, sound on, a stranger)
- "Like someone STOOD there all night, STARING at it." Creepy, not intimate (it's about a window).
- "He always SAID he'd keep an EYE on me. NOT helping." A kid's joke about a dead grandpa's promise; not obedient or
  intimate.
- "I don't LOOK." A scared kid.
- "Grandpa was STILL keeping an EYE on me." Warm. Nothing reads as innuendo, ASMR or obedience. Every direction says
  full volume; the one scared beat (L10's end) is real fear and still voiced.
