# nine_thousand_torches: beat sheet, clue map, writer's checks (r1)

r1 (2026-11-10) answers the critic's park note ("Great frame, but the clue and twist are wall_grows's"; "drop the
trading hall for an answer that isn't a cage"): the torches are not a cage and not a defence against something coming.
They are a goodbye. The village's player is leaving the world for good and spent his last night making sure no zombie
can ever spawn in it again.

## The ending first
**The reveal frame:** sunrise. Through a villager kid's low eyes, Milo (Steve, his name tag over his head) stands on
the kid's doorstep, mid-wave, a fresh torch burning at his feet. On the next frame he is gone, the doorstep is empty,
the last torch still burns, and the chat reads, in yellow, **Milo left the game**. Behind the house, every block of the
village carries a torch, and the sun clears the hill.

**The first frame it re-explains:** a dawn crane over a whole village studded with thousands of flames. Before the
reveal it reads as a fortress against something coming; after, it reads as a promise left behind: nothing will spawn
here once Milo's gone.

## Spine
- **Every day,** Milo, the player who lives in the village, plays with his best friend, a villager kid, and every
  night says "see you tomorrow" and logs off.
- **One day,** Milo says his mom made him promise, one last night, and the next morning there is a torch on every
  block of the village, nine thousand of them, and the kid decides something big must be coming.
- **Because of that,** he finds all of Milo's things in his family's chest and Milo's house gone, a square of torches
  where it stood, and he follows the one row that is still growing.
- **Until finally,** the row ends at his own door, where Milo places the last torch, waves, and leaves the game
  without saying "see you tomorrow".

## The second the viewer gets ahead
~26 s (L11, 71%). By then the viewer holds "one last night" (L07), his whole inventory, bed included, in our chest
(S09), and his house taken down (S11). A Minecraft player knows what it looks like when a friend gives away their stuff
and takes their base down: they're quitting. The narrator is still on "something's coming". L12-L14 are suspense: the
viewer knows where the row ends and what Milo is about to do.

## The twist checklist (docs/story_craft.md section 3)
- [x] **It changes what line 1 means.** Before: "someone" fortified the village against something coming. After: my
  best friend lit my village so it stays safe after he's gone. Same words, a goodbye instead of a threat.
- [x] **Fair play.** Every fact is on screen or said before L15: the spawning rule (L03), Milo is the player (L05), the
  "see you tomorrow" ritual (L06), "one last night" (L07), the gifts (S09), the house gone (S11), the row to my door
  (S13). The reveal adds nothing new: it is a vanilla message about a player we've met.
- [x] **Planted at least twice, at least once in the picture.** Five plants: two in lines (L06, L07), three in the
  picture (S09 the chest screen 2 s, S11 the plot 2.6 s, S13 the row to my door 1.7 s), each on screen over 1 s.
- [x] **Misread, not hidden.** L04/L10 "something's coming / he's hiding it", L08 "grounded a lot". The house gone
  (L11) is left unexplained by the narrator, which is honest: he has no theory left that fits.
- [x] **Someone gets ahead.** ~26 s (71%), see above.
- [x] **The game says it.** A player quitting a server: the entity vanishes and the server prints "Milo left the
  game" in yellow chat. No spoken "he quit forever"; L15 is the narrator noticing what wasn't said, on the reveal.
- [x] **One step.** "Milo left the game" + "he didn't say see you tomorrow" = he's not coming back. Everything else
  (the torches, the chest, the house) clicks from that one thought.
- [x] **Readable at 300 px.** The chat line is small (GUI scale 3, ~24 px text), so the caption track repeats "Milo
  left the game" in yellow during the hold, and the picture says it too: he is there, then he isn't.
- [x] **Visible causes.** Every torch has a placer (Milo, seen with torches in S07-S08, placing in S12 (an arm at the
  corner) and S14). His vanishing is a quit, which the game shows by its own rule. No accidents in this story.
- [x] **A rewatch plays differently.** L01 "Someone put nine thousand torches": a friend's last night. L04 "Something
  BIG must be COMING": something big was going. L06 "Every night, he says see you tomorrow": the setup for the line he
  doesn't say. L08 "He's grounded a LOT": in a way, forever. L10 "He's HIDING it": he's giving it.

## The "why?" interrogation

| action | why? | answered where |
|---|---|---|
| Milo lights the village at all | Once he's gone, nobody fights the zombies at night. Zombies can't spawn on lit blocks, so if every block is lit, nothing spawns in the village ever again. | L03 says the rule; the twist supplies the motive |
| and every block, even fences and hay | He won't be back to find the one dark spot he missed, so he doesn't leave one to miss. Fences and hay too: it's his last night and he's not taking chances (the kid's "So why EVERY block?" is the viewer's question, and the answer is "because he can't come back") | L03 asks; the reveal answers |
| and not the roads and fields? | Dirt paths and farmland aren't full blocks, so zombies can't spawn on them and torches can't stand on them: Milo knows the rule (vanilla-true; a player will notice the paths stay dark and nod) | the picture (S01); bible |
| and why tonight | His mom's rule: one last night (L07). He's leaving the world for good tomorrow. | L07 |
| all night, alone, while the kid sleeps | Villagers sleep at night (vanilla); a player doesn't have to. That's why the kid wakes to it: "last night". | L01; bible |
| Milo takes his own house down | A house nobody lives in is a dark room, and a dark room is a spawn spot. And he had nothing left to keep in it. | S11 (picture), bible; the attentive viewer works it out |
| Milo puts all his stuff in our chest | He's leaving and can't take it; it goes to his best friend. A villager can't use a sword: it's a keepsake, not a tool. | S09; L10's misread |
| saves our door for last | The last torch is the goodbye; he ends where his best friend is. | S13-S14 |
| Milo doesn't say goodbye in words | Every night he says "see you tomorrow", and tonight it isn't true. He won't lie to his friend, and "goodbye" is too hard; the nine thousand torches say it. The kid gets it from what's missing. | L06 + L15 |
| why not tell the kid days ago | A kid friend who'd have begged him to stay; and "Mom made me promise" was the telling (L07), the kid just heard "grounded". | L07-L08 |
| The kid thinks something's coming | It's the only reason he knows for lighting a village; Milo taught him the spawn rule for defence. | L03-L04 |
| The kid follows the row | It's the only part of the village still changing, it's daylight, and his best friend is probably at the end of it. | L12-L13 |
| The kid opens the chest first | He woke up at home; the chest is in his house (S09 is inside, S10 looks out the window). | S09-S10 |
| A villager narrates, quotes Milo, opens a chest | House style: a villager kid who talks (story_craft section 4 allows villagers to speak as a style choice); the chest screen is the one way the game can show the inventory. Flag for minecraft-audit as a style licence, like the talking villagers in light_sleeper. | |
| Zombies could still walk in from outside | They could: that's the iron golem's job (seen in S03). Milo made sure none can be born inside. | S03; bible |

## The out-of-context read (3 s, a stranger)
- L01 "Someone put nine thousand torches on my village last night": absurd, fine.
- L05 "My best friend Milo is our player": a kid talking about a game friend; nothing intimate.
- L06 "Every night, he says see you tomorrow and logs off": friends gaming, plain.
- L07 'Milo said, "Mom made me promise. One last night."': a kid's curfew; with the kid's bright read and L08's joke
  right after, it can't be heard as anything else.
- L14 "Milo put the last one by my door. And waved." / L15 "He didn't say, see you tomorrow.": friendship, read at full
  volume, plain and steady, no hush (directions say so). No line about waiting, obeying or loving.
