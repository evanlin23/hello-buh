**The reveal frame (written first).** First light behind the giant stone face. Mom, her cap off, has her hand on the
stone. A sparkle trail runs from the stone to her face (S02's trail, running backwards), one dark wrinkle row goes
from the stone, and a second grey streak comes into her hair. Then, at the bench at the statue's foot, Dad has been
waiting. She sits by him: his grey head and her grey streaks side by side.

**The first line it re-explains.** "Nobody gets old, except the statue. And it's getting younger." The statue is
getting younger because Mom is taking her years back, one each morning. Now two people in the village get old.

## The twist checklist
- One sentence: Mom is taking back the years she gave the statue, one each morning, so she can grow old with Dad.
- Said in one plain line by the kid, what she sees (L10: a wrinkle slid off the stone onto her face); the why (Dad) is in the next picture (Dad waiting on the bench) and the sting (L11); no game knowledge needed.
- Planted at least twice where a rewatch catches it: the pale stripes and the seven clear dawns (S01, S03, S04 "rain"),
  Mom's walks (L06/L07, S06 pre-dawn), Dad's choice (L05), the grey hair (L08), the trail's direction (S02 vs S10).
- The rule turned against itself: the gift that keeps you young is taken back to get old.
- Someone loved at stake: Mom, going grey and hiding it; the kid goes to cheer her up and finds out.
- Agency: the kid decides to follow her and says why (L09: "Where does she even WALK?").
- Naivety (0a): every clue gets an innocent read; the kid is never scared and never hints.
- Loop: L11 "Nobody gets old in my village. Except Mom. And Dad." runs into L01.
- Takeaway: a warm "aww": she gave up staying young to grow old with the person she loves.
