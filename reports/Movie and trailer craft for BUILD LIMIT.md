# Rebuild Build Limit around its ceiling

Keep the current premise, a tower city whose sixteen-year-olds jump once into a one-block pool while one jumper a year is secretly made to miss, but it only reaches the bar of The Prestige or The Truman Show once its title becomes its big idea. Its human core is already strong enough for that bar: a promise across a gulf ("I'll hear it from the top"), a mother who named her son so she could keep her daughter, and a girl named by her own grandfather. Its five chapter breaks also already sit where the eight closed-society films put their turns, at about 20, 40, 60 and 80 percent. What holds it back is machinery. It asks the viewer to hold **twelve rules**. Its trick has **four moving parts**, yet one line calls it "the whole trick". Older characters hand Buh most of the answers. The Below is so easy to survive that it risks The Village's "crummy secret". And the title names a ceiling while the secret lives at the bottom of the tower. One move pulls all of this together: make "build limit" the system's lie. The Highs justify the Count with the one rule in this world nobody made ("There is no forty-one"), while their own law, Nothing past the Edge, creates the shortage and half of Forty stands empty. Buh then beats a man-made rule with a real one, the one-tick clutch. The teaser has a measured teaser's shape: the title lands at **79%**, a **12-second** tail follows it, there are **seven lines** and a **0.7-second** silence before the reveal. But it shows all three images the research marks as answers. Its cards run **5 to 9 words**, against **1 to 6** in the trailers measured. **44%** of it is black. And it never says why it is called Build Limit. Of the three alternatives pitched below, only "The Sky Is Built" could beat the reworked original. The notes hold no screenplay sources on Interstellar, Arrival or Children of Men, and on Inception only its teaser sound and one exposition scene. Where those films come up, it is the writer's judgement.

## Five chapter breaks already sit on the closed-society beat grid

Across the eight closed-society films, the turns cluster tightly. The ritual or system is on screen by about 12% of the runtime. The hero leaves home or commits between 15 and 25%. The midpoint is a physical threshold at 40 to 50%. The worst loss is a death or a horror the system did deliberately, at 65 to 77%. The full explanation comes at 67 to 95%, and the ending is a literal exit or opening. The research notes measured these positions from shooting scripts and transcripts: Truman, Snowpiercer, The Hunger Games and The Maze Runner from scripts, The Village, The Giver, Divergent and City of Ember from transcripts. The release film of The Hunger Games turns at minute 28 (the train into the Capitol), 67 (the tube into the arena), 98 (Rue's death) and 106 (the rule change) ([Save the Cat](https://savethecat.com/beat-sheets/the-hunger-games-bookmovie-beat-sheet-comparison)). BUILD LIMIT's draft 2.1 lands on nearly the same marks.

| Beat | Where the films put it | BUILD LIMIT draft 2.1 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ritual shown as procedure | By ~12%: Snowpiercer's head count at 4%, the Reaping at 10% | Cold open, minute 0: the pat-down, the census, two buckets | Matches |
| Leave home or commit | 15–25%; Truman's homeless "father" at 23% | Ch1 out (~19%): the name tag "with an eight", a message from someone presumed dead | Matches |

The table shows the structure is not BUILD LIMIT's problem. Two of its beats are, on content. The first is the worst loss. In the films it is a death or a horror the system commits on purpose. **Rue is speared at 69%**, and the District 11 uprising follows ([Save the Cat](https://savethecat.com/beat-sheets/the-hunger-games-bookmovie-beat-sheet-comparison)). Lucius is stabbed at about 58% ([IMSDb transcript](https://imsdb.com/scripts/Village,-The.html)). The Giver's release footage plays at about 66% ([Scripts.com](https://www.scripts.com/script/the_giver_20316)). Curtis confesses to cannibalism at 77% ([Snowpiercer script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/snowpiercer-2013.pdf)). BUILD LIMIT's Chapter 4 loss is a broken plan: a man taken, a trapdoor without power, a message that can't be answered. The arc already planted the right horror and never paid it off. In Chapter 3 Ash says that if the Highkeeper learns of the lake, "he finds the lake and fills it". The Chapter 4 loss should be that threat coming true: Keepers pouring gravel down the Needle's shaft on the eve of the Drop, burying the only way out for thirty people. Gravel is a vanilla falling block, but that mechanic should be confirmed with the minecraft-audit before scripting. That gives the system a deliberate horror and starts a clock, the way Rue's death leads straight into the rule change.

The second is how the explainers are staged. The four films whose reveals land never stage them as a neutral lecture. The Truman Show explains its world to the audience on a talk show inside the film. The show keeps Truman's image on screen throughout, and a hostile caller ("He's not a performer. He's a prisoner.") turns out to be Sylvia ([Truman script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/the-truman-show-1998.pdf), pdf p.74–83). Wilford cooks steak for a starving man while he talks, and the speech is cross-cut with a fight whose outcome is uncertain ([Snowpiercer script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/snowpiercer-2013.pdf), p.94–105). The Village's blind heroine learns the truth by touching a costume. The explanation then comes as a volley of her one-line questions, each answered in one line ("The screams? From the woods?" / "We created those sounds."), and it ends in grief and a mission rather than logic ([IMSDb](https://imsdb.com/scripts/Village,-The.html)). Reveals delivered as a congratulations speech after the climax are the ones critics call exposition. Brian Eggert wrote that The Maze Runner "delves into outright exposition and ends abruptly with all but flashing 'to be continued'" ([Deep Focus Review](https://deepfocusreview.com/reviews/the-maze-runner/)). BUILD LIMIT's mechanism scene, Ash building a cobblestone model at the camp, has none of the three working parts. Nobody's body is at stake, the explainer wants nothing from the listener, and no hostile voice interrupts. Wren's statement of the Cradle rule is a plain statement in a safe place. Orlo's last speech on the Plank is a confession, not a temptation. Christof ends his explanation by wanting something: "in my world you have nothing to fear" ([Truman script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/the-truman-show-1998.pdf), p.106–108). Wilford ends his with an offer: "I want you to take my station" ([Snowpiercer script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/snowpiercer-2013.pdf)). Orlo's Plank scene should open the same way, with an offer: Forty, for Buh's mother and sister, if he takes the bucket and jumps. Only then comes the credo.

The arc's openings and endings already follow the films that worked. Divergent, The Giver and City of Ember open on narration that lists the rules. James Berardinelli wrote that Divergent's "opening voiceover gives us the basics" and that the film "suffers from a weak final half-hour" ([Reelviews](https://www.reelviews.net/reelviews/divergent)). Eggert wrote that in City of Ember "when you know the inevitable conclusion from the outset… the mystery is rendered pointless" ([Deep Focus Review](https://www.deepfocusreview.com/reviews/city-of-ember/)). Truman, The Village and Snowpiercer show their rules through behaviour first: the two-way mirror, the buried red flower, the head count. BUILD LIMIT has no narrator. Its law chant is a ritual the characters perform, like Ember's oath or Divergent's "Faction before blood" ([Scripts.com](https://www.scripts.com/script/divergent_7003)), so it is on the right side of that line. The series button, other towers ringing twice, is safe from the sequel-bait complaint for the same reason Divergent's "And then we'll jump" is: Chapter 5 resolves its own conflict on screen first.

## Fair clues come from the camera, so Buh must earn the answers

The fair twists in the mystery set follow one pattern. The key clue is a picture or an action, shown plainly and early, while the audience is busy with something else. In Glass Onion, Miles tucks Duke's gun into his waistband while Birdie stands in front of him, and Duke's phone sticks out of Miles's back pocket while he pretends to search for it. Rian Johnson says the first half plays "really, really fair" and that the film was designed for a second viewing ([Slashfilm](https://slashfilm.com/1172779/glass-onion-was-designed-to-be-watched-a-second-time)). The Sixth Sense keeps red out of its palette except near the dead, and Malcolm's red basement doorknob is always locked ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/570758/scariest-scene-in-the-sixth-sense); [Looper](https://www.looper.com/619299/the-big-clue-everyone-missed-early-in-the-sixth-sense)). The Prestige performs its whole twist in miniature in the opening: a crushed birdcage and a second bird, and a child asking where the bird's brother is. Meanwhile the film steers the audience toward Tesla's machine instead of the simple answer, twins ([SoapCentral](https://www.soapcentral.com/entertainment/did-know-big-twist-ending-the-prestige-actually-spoiled-beginning-movie)). Johnson's fairness test is that **the first thing you are told turns out to be true**. His companion rule is that characters may lie and the camera may not ([Scriptnotes 579](https://johnaugust.com/2023/scriptnotes-episode-579-rian-johnson-returns-transcript)). BUILD LIMIT's cold open passes both. The Highkeeper keeps the full bucket and hands Ash the empty one; the second toll sounds; the Well blinks. That is a birdcage, the whole trick shown before anyone knows it is a trick. Fen's "Echo" is a character's lie, so it is allowed.

The next pattern is the rule of three. A rule is set up once, tested once in the middle, and turned at the climax. Marta can't lie without vomiting. She is set up giving true but partial answers, and at the end she lies on purpose to draw out Ransom's confession ([Wikipedia: Knives Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knives_Out)). Zootopia's carrot pen records Nick, then Judy, then Bellwether ([Save the Cat](https://savethecat.com/?p=12988); [Movie Mistakes](https://moviemistakes.com/film11430/questions)). BUILD LIMIT's clutch has its three uses: the stone MLG on Floor 6, "From Forty you get one tick. Sometimes none," and the one-tick clutch from Forty. The double toll and the bucket have three or more each. "Forty-one", the phrase carrying the title, has only two: "Then I'll jump from forty-one" in Chapter 1 and "Told you there was a forty-one" at the end. It needs a middle beat, which the next section supplies.

The arc breaks these films' discipline in its mechanism. Knox's 1929 rules ban devices that need a long scientific explanation and allow at most one secret passage ([OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/agatha-christie-and-the-golden-age-detective-fiction/content-section-5.1)). StudioBinder points to the scene in Inception where Cobb explains the dream rules to Ariadne as a case of over-explaining ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/exposition-examples-in-film/)). That is the only point on Inception's screenplay anywhere in the notes. BUILD LIMIT's trick has four parts. The jumper is handed an empty bucket. A dispenser empties the Well. A sticky piston drops the stone under it. A second dispenser pours a new Well one block over. Ash then says, "Two buckets. One empty, one full. That's the whole trick." That isn't true, and the owner has twice been unable to read the trick on screen. Both edit-list notes record it: "i dont understand the opening dialog", and "it doesnt make sense that theres no water in the water bucket" (`episodes/trailer/edl.json`). The Prestige's answer is a single fact under elaborate misdirection, and BUILD LIMIT's should be too. In the writer's judgement, the trick should collapse to two parts a single shot can show: the swap, and one hole. The jumper gets the empty bucket. Lever two opens the floor under the Well, the water and the jumper fall through into the shaft and the lake, and the floor closes and refills in place. The "new Well one block over" exists only to hide the hole afterwards, and it can go. Two costs come with this. The existing reels render the Well sliding, so those shots would be re-recorded. And Rook's Chapter 5 line becomes "It opened!" instead of "It moved!"

The rule load should shrink for the same reason, and not to make it easier for a young audience. At the bar of The Prestige, every rule should be one face of a single idea. The arc's twelve rules are the Drop, "the higher, the harder", one bucket, the fog as the void, Nothing past the Edge, Two is the Count, the Cradle's price, the bell, the rig, the Below, and a three-part reason the ones stay hidden. That last one is three reasons at once: their family must name another, the Highkeeper fills the lake, and nobody would see them come out of the fog. Each rule that doesn't serve "one in, one out at build limit" should fold into one that does.

Who explains also matters more than the arc allows. Van Dine's 1928 rules require the culprit to be found by logic, not by luck or an unprompted confession ([UMSL](https://www.umsl.edu/~gradyf/film/vandinerules.htm)). Fleur Bradley's craft advice is to spread clues as milestones the detective must work to reach, never handed over by an adult. It was written for children's mysteries, but the technique is general ([SCBWI](https://scbwi.blogspot.com/2024/05/3-tips-for-writing-mystery-for-kids.html)). In draft 2.1, Ash supplies the compass, the name tag, the model and the reason the ones stay hidden. Fen supplies the double toll, Wren the rule, and Tilly the torn page. Buh's own deduction is a single line: "Named. It's not a miss. Somebody picks." Buh should build the model himself, from what he has gathered: the tally marks, the seventeenth bucket, the lever room's two levers, the dispensers on the cap. Ash only confirms it, in The Village's one-line volley: "Two levers." / "Two tolls." / "The empty bucket?" / "So you can't clutch." That turns a lecture into a brother checking his brother's work. It also gives the slowed replay of the cold open the trigger StudioBinder says a flashback needs, a character's own memory ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/exposition-examples-in-film/)). Coraline offers the model for Wren's rule. The ghost children explain the Beldam to Coraline while she is trapped behind the mirror, and the explanation comes with a quest attached ([Wikipedia: Coraline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coraline_(film))). Wren should say the Cradle rule during the mob attack at the wall, not at a quiet camp.

Dialogue meant to work on rewatch already runs through the arc. It should keep obeying the rule the sources show. Rewatch lines are ordinary social phrases that are true on both readings, like Sarah's "Not today" in The Prestige ([Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/The_Prestige_(film))), the Sixth Sense anniversary dinner, or the "My House, My Rules, My Coffee!!" mug in Knives Out ([Looper](https://www.looper.com/619299/the-big-clue-everyone-missed-early-in-the-sixth-sense); [Wikipedia: Knives Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knives_Out)). In BUILD LIMIT, "How many in your family?", "There's always room on Forty", "Three, son" and "I'll hear it from the top" all qualify, as long as the first reading is played warm. Kindly villains in these films give things. Their threat is built into the offer, and they reveal themselves in one short credo. The Other Mother feeds Coraline and then offers her buttons ([Wikipedia: Coraline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coraline_(film))). Bellwether hands the heroes the traffic cameras ([Wikipedia: Zootopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zootopia)), and her worldview fits in three words, "Fear always works" (via a search summary of [Looper](https://looper.com/778393/the-bellwether-detail-in-zootopia-that-means-more-than-you-think)). Orlo needs one real kindness in Chapter 2 that later turns out to serve the system. For example, he grants Buh a practice permit for the high floors, and the permit is how Hollis tracks him. He also needs one credo, as late as possible.

On how dark the Cradle rule should be (owner choice 4), the owner's bar settles it: keep it, and show it once, with restraint. The set's most devastating reveal is the plainest. Jonas's father talks baby talk to a newborn twin while he kills it: "Almost, almost... Done. That wasn't so bad now, was it?" The Giver's only comment is flat: "That's death" ([Scripts.com](https://www.scripts.com/script/the_giver_20316)). BUILD LIMIT's equivalent is the naming night three years ago, which Mom remembers during the Chapter 4 fight. Orlo kneels by the stall, asks gently how many are in the family, and writes. Nothing cruel is shown, only the warmth of the census against what it costs. That scene earns the rest of the film's restraint.

One reveal still shrinks the stakes, and it is the one Roger Ebert punished. He called The Village's twist "a crummy secret, about one step up the ladder of narrative originality from 'It was all a dream'". His complaint was a reveal that makes past danger fake ([Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-2004-movie-so-bad-it-insulted-roger-ebert/)). BUILD LIMIT's "the fog is not the void" does the same thing to every past Drop Day. The arc's answer, the Cradle and "Mom named me", raises the moral stakes but leaves the Below itself nearly free: green, sunlit, with a lake and campfires. Snowpiercer shows the taken coming back changed. Gerald returns with a one-string violin, and Tim is found "completely inserted into the Engine" ([Snowpiercer script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/snowpiercer-2013.pdf), p.73, p.105). Curtis's confession works as a story told with a single gesture, an open palm (p.87). The Below needs that kind of scar. Wren should tell, with one gesture, of the year one of the ones climbed back, and of the brother their house named in that one's place. That turns the hardest of the three "why they stay" reasons into a memory that still bleeds.

## Minecraft rewards one game rule made final, so make build limit the lie

The Minecraft sources agree on what makes a story feel native. Parkour Civilization took one vanilla verb, jumping, and made it the only law, the only currency and the only class marker. It played that absurd premise "always… straight" ([The Brandeis Hoot](https://brandeishoot.com/?p=27284)), and its hero fell into the void on his first big jump, which "introduced failure into the story" ([Critical Video Game Studies](https://criticalvideogamestudies.com/shes-really-good-parkour-civilization/)). Its first compiled film runs **114 minutes** and drew about **28 million views** by October 2024 ([The Catalyst](https://thecatalystnews.com/2024/10/10/movie-of-the-block-minecraft-but-i-survive-in-parkour-civilization/); [Rolling Stone via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/minecraft-cinema-fans-parkour-civilization-181650791.html)). Dream SMP layered "canon lives" over vanilla respawn: only deaths at important events count. Dream described the server as Minecraft used as a storytelling medium rather than a game ([Wikipedia: Dream SMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_SMP)). A Minecraft Movie's most-shouted moment was an item used as a lifeline. "Water bucket, release!" became a call-and-response in cinemas ([Soap Central](https://charlie-www.soapcentral.com/humor/a-minecraft-movie-fan-yells-water-bucket-release-throwing-popcorn-someone-fans-roast)). The official wiki then corrected the film's version: in the game the bucket is placed just before landing, and the film's Steve fires water at the ground from a distance ([Minecraft Wiki: Movie:Bucket](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Movie:Bucket)). Fans nitpicked piglins safe in the Overworld, ghasts used as transport, howling wolves and a chained bucket that doesn't exist ([Sportskeeda](https://opera.sportskeeda.com/minecraft/5-differences-minecraft-game-movie)), yet Parkour Civilization's invented social laws drew praise for being "detailed, consistent, well thought out" ([The Brandeis Hoot](https://brandeishoot.com/?p=27284)). The line fans draw is clear. **Physics, items and mobs must behave like the game. Social rules are free.**

BUILD LIMIT is already grounded to that standard, more than any machinima in the notes. The story turns on real mechanics. Build limit is y 320. The clutch has a one-tick window at almost 190 blocks, and the arc's production notes say about one fall in five has no valid tick at all. The allay delivers to a played note block, a lodestone compass points to the Needle, and a name tag carries a message. Bells carry about 32 blocks, which is why they are wired one every five floors. Tripwire can be cut with shears without setting it off. A redstone torch powers an iron trapdoor. Mushrooms grow below the clouds and carrots above them, and the Keepers are people in iron armour because iron golems can't hold items. Two risks remain. One is the clutch itself. It must be shown as the game does it: placed in the last tick, with the empty bucket visible in the hotbar afterwards. That is the version players will read as real ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Movie:Bucket)). The other is closeness to the genre's biggest hit. In Parkour Civilization's late reveal, "a player's very first jump on Earth determines their place" ([The Catalyst](https://thecatalystnews.com/2024/10/10/movie-of-the-block-minecraft-but-i-survive-in-parkour-civilization/)), and BUILD LIMIT's whole premise is that the floor you jump from is your family's forever. The notes treat the likeness as "a known, loved pattern rather than a copy". At a prestige bar, though, BUILD LIMIT's own idea has to live somewhere Parkour Civilization doesn't go.

That place is the title. Glass Onion's title promises the method: Johnson chose it for something hidden in plain sight ([Wikipedia: Glass Onion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_Onion:_A_Knives_Out_Mystery)). In draft 2.1, build limit is a location and a joke. It is where the Plank stands, and the point of Rook's "There is no forty-one. That's build limit, genius." The secret, meanwhile, is at the bottom, in the Needle. Yet the arc already holds every piece needed to make the title the secret. The Count needs a reason, and the obvious official one is that the tower has reached the top of the world, so there is no room. "Half of Forty's empty" exposes that as a lie. Nothing past the Edge is the law that keeps the shortage real, because building out would end it. And the ending, "We build out now. Not up.", is the answer. Build limit is the only rule in this world nobody made, and the Highs dress their own law in its authority. That turns the Minecraft grounding into the film's moral spine. The game's rules are real and fair: the one tick, the bucket, the limit. The society's rules are lies wearing the game's clothes. The Truman Show states this thesis outright: "We accept the reality of the world with which we're presented" ([Truman script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/the-truman-show-1998.pdf), p.81). Snowpiercer's villain uses the same excuse: "this train is a closed eco-system. We must always strive for balance" ([Snowpiercer script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/snowpiercer-2013.pdf)).

The phrase then follows The Giver's pattern for a euphemism. A child uses it innocently early ("Can I be 'Released to Elsewhere,' Mother?"), and the hero later says the plain word ([Scripts.com](https://www.scripts.com/script/the_giver_20316)). Rook says it as a taunt in Chapter 1, and it closes the law chant. In Chapter 2 it gets the missing middle use: Buh, on Forty's roof, tries to place a block above and the game refuses. Orlo says it as his credo on the Plank in Chapter 5, the Bellwether slot, and Buh answers it at the end. The climax becomes the argument made physical. Buh beats a man-made limit with a real one, a clutch the game itself allows him one tick to make.

The owner named Interstellar as the bar, "a father's promise against time". The notes contain no source on its screenplay, so this comparison is the writer's judgement. BUILD LIMIT already owns that shape: a promise made across a gulf, kept in a way the person who made it could not foresee. Ash's "Play it every night and I'll hear it from the top" becomes "I heard it every night. From the bottom". Messages cross the gulf through a played instrument and a creature that answers it, the note block and the allay. That is the arc's emotional core. The machinery's only job is to serve it.

The genre evidence also settles three production questions. First, voices. In August 2023, the fan animation "Scooby-Doo Springtrapped" lost goodwill once viewers learned four of its seven characters used AI voices of working performers ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/the-scooby-doo-springtrapped-ai-voice-controversy-explained)). A 2025 paper documents AI-generated children's videos on YouTube that closely imitate popular stories ([AoIR, "Slop for Kids"](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/download/15158/12307/93918)), and vendors sell Minecraft story pipelines with stock AI voices ([Clippie](https://clippie.ai/ai-video-styles/minecraft)). The teaser's voices are Gemini TTS, and one voice, Achird, plays both brothers (`episodes/trailer/voices.json`). At the owner's bar, the movie needs real, directed performances, and distinct voices for Ash and Buh. Second, narration. Parkour Civilization's most-criticised element was its hero's "perpetually excited" YouTuber narration ([Critical Video Game Studies](https://criticalvideogamestudies.com/shes-really-good-parkour-civilization/); [The Catalyst](https://thecatalystnews.com/2024/10/10/movie-of-the-block-minecraft-but-i-survive-in-parkour-civilization/)), and BUILD LIMIT should never add a narrator. Third, the compiled movie. Parkour Civilization's version "often jumps back into recaps right after cliffhangers" ([The Catalyst](https://thecatalystnews.com/2024/10/10/movie-of-the-block-minecraft-but-i-survive-in-parkour-civilization/)), so the movie cut should strip chapter-opening recaps. Each chapter's cold open should still work as a standalone clip, because Parkour Civilization went viral from reposts of its first ninety seconds, not from the film ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/parkour-civilization)). One reviewer also found that "many important characters go unnamed" ([The Brandeis Hoot](https://brandeishoot.com/?p=27284)). BUILD LIMIT still has "a kid", "a Keeper", "the crier" and "Hollis's small boy", and each needs a name.

## The teaser has a teaser's shape but shows the answer

The seven trailers measured for the notes split cleanly by form. Full trailers of about 2:15 to 2:30 land the title at **94 to 96%** of their length, with little after it. The two teaser-shaped edits, the Minecraft teaser and The Village's Trailer #1, land it at about **75%** and spend the last quarter on a gag, a closing line or a tagline. The cut rate peaks in montages in the last third, and the quietest moments are deliberate drop-outs just before a reveal ([Minecraft teaser](https://archive.org/details/youtube-PE2YZhcC4NY); [The Village](https://archive.org/details/youtube-sTGyhwvdY6k); [Knives Out](https://archive.org/details/dailymotion-x7cscee)). The BUILD LIMIT edit list adds up to 58.15 seconds, and most of its proportions match.

| Answer images | Withheld: The Hunger Games "shows nothing of the actual 'game'" | The Well's slow-motion slide (6.1 s), green under the fog, "Took you long enough, Buh" |

The table's last row is the biggest problem. The trailer writer who identified The Hunger Games' music said the first trailer "shows nothing of the actual 'game', withholding just enough information to leave the viewer wanting to see more" ([Trailer Music News](https://trailermusicnews.com/2012/02/zerokilled-music-hunger-games-trailer/)). mOcean, which cut trailers for Stranger Things, says "the old template telling people how they should interpret what they're watching is less effective" ([FLOOD](https://floodmagazine.com/40518/coming-attractions-the-makings-of-the-modern-movie-trailer)). The notes name the three answer images for this story: the world under the fog, Ash alive, and the Well moving. The current teaser shows all three. The slide plays from 32.45 to 38.55 seconds in slow motion. The ladder shot glimpses something green. Ash's voice says "Took you long enough, Buh". The slide is the mechanism, the Chapter 3 model, in picture. The other two are the Chapter 2 cliffhanger, 40% into the story. The Hunger Games trailer reportedly ran as far as a tribute rising into the arena ([Neon Tommy](https://www.neontommy.com/news/2011/11/hunger-games-trailer-released-video.html)). But BUILD LIMIT releases chapter by chapter, so its teaser only has to sell Chapter 1's question. The breach can be shown without the mechanism, and Fen's "It rings twice... for the one" has already set it up. The second toll heard over the fall into the fog is the reveal, carried by sound. The empty bucket held for a beat is the birdcage image: shown, not explained. Ash's line belongs in the full movie trailer.

The cards are too long for the form. Every card measured runs 1 to 6 words, often one sentence split across two or three cards. The Minecraft teaser's 2-3-2 run, "NEXT YEAR / LET YOUR IMAGINATION / RUN WILD", shows each card for about 1.25 seconds, alternating with shots of about one second. Knives Out uses the same small-word-over-big-word layout ("comes a / Whodunnit") ([Minecraft teaser](https://archive.org/details/youtube-PE2YZhcC4NY); [Knives Out](https://archive.org/details/dailymotion-x7cscee)). BUILD LIMIT's cards hold 8, 9, 8, 7 and 5 words on black. The rule can be restated as the same kind of run: "ONE TOWER" over the tower push, "ONE JUMP", "ONE BLOCK OF WATER". The breach then takes Minecraft's 2-3-2 shape exactly: "EVERY YEAR / ONE OF US / NEVER LANDS". "IT WAS NEVER / AN ACCIDENT" already fits. Putting the cards over picture, or between one-second shots, also solves the black problem. Black works in the measured trailers under a voice: Arcane opens with ten seconds of near-black under Vi's memory ([Arcane](https://archive.org/details/youtube-OrTyvmQ0KA8)). BUILD LIMIT's four card beats are 9.4 seconds of black carrying nothing but text. The documented failure of the Minecraft teaser was spectacle without a premise. Reviewers said it told "little about the plot" ([The Crimson White](https://thecrimsonwhite.com/115701/culture/culture-pick-its-game-over-for-the-minecraft-movie-following-disastrous-teaser-trailer/)), and it drew 1.4 million dislikes against 626,000 likes in its first week ([The Slate](https://theslateonline.com/article/2024/09/a-minecraft-movie-teaser-is-a-disappointing-mess)). BUILD LIMIT's teaser risks the opposite: a clear premise in a world barely seen. A 190-block fall past a stone tower into a sea of fog is the image that sells this film, and it gets less screen time than black does.

The teaser also never earns its title. The measured trailers put a rule sentence before the title, then the title. Minecraft's trailer has "As long as what you imagine… can be built out of blocks." The Village has "We do not stray into their woods. They do not come into our village." ([Minecraft trailer](https://archive.org/details/youtube-gbNzxvz2_H4); [The Village](https://archive.org/details/youtube-sTGyhwvdY6k)). The Minecraft teaser drops its music for about a second between "I…" and "am Steve." ([Minecraft teaser](https://archive.org/details/youtube-PE2YZhcC4NY)). BUILD LIMIT's 0.7-second hush is the same device, but it sits before the slide, which is to be cut. Moved, it should fall before the one line that explains the title, "There is no forty-one.", and the title should smash in on that line. "That's not an echo." stays as the button after the title, in the slot The Village gives "THE TRUCE IS ENDING". Over the two far bells, it reframes everything the way the Maze Runner trailer's "You don't get it. We're already dead." does.

The score is the teaser's strongest asset, but its spine borrows generic trailer devices where the film's own sounds would serve better. Every element is made from the game: the four-note song on a real note block, the game's bell given a sine body, drones, braams, risers and a hard silence (`scripts/trailer.py`). It follows the structure the composer guides describe: a sparse intro, a build with pulses, holes left for dialogue, a break just before the end, and a signature sound introduced early and recalled at the end ([Master the Score](https://www.masterthescore.com/article/how-to-write-trailer-music-a-step-by-step-guide); [Richard Pryn](https://richardpryn.com/how-to-structure-trailer-music/)). But the build runs on clock ticks and a heartbeat, the generic devices of the form. Mark Woollen builds rhythm from a film's own diegetic sound. In A Serious Man, a head thudding on a chalkboard "became kind of the baseline for the trailer and kind of set the trailer's rhythm" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2015/02/23/388481464/how-the-man-behind-the-trailers-sparks-an-urge-to-see-a-movie)). He has also cut to a locomotive horn and to DiCaprio's breathing ([FLOOD](https://floodmagazine.com/40518/coming-attractions-the-makings-of-the-modern-movie-trailer)). Inception's first teaser was scored from subway foley recordings ([The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/braaams-beginners-how-a-horn-793220)). The Knives Out trailer is built on "a single piano", and the Glass Onion trailer starts on the same note ([Trailaurality](https://trailaurality.squarespace.com/blog/2022/11/6/knives-out-glass-onion)). BUILD LIMIT's bell is that sound. Its montage should cut on tolls, one per jump and one per cut, so that when the toll comes twice the audience hears the reveal before any card states it. Derek Lieu's rule fits this: "a line of dialogue is followed immediately by a sound effect or big beat in the music cue" ([Derek Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2017/12/3/the-inception-braaaaaaam-sound)).

Last, the teaser needs one anchor image. The Village reuses its red slash as the wipe that reveals the tagline ([The Village](https://archive.org/details/youtube-sTGyhwvdY6k)). BUILD LIMIT's equivalent is the Well seen from Forty: one block of water, "a dot" almost 190 blocks down. It should open the fall and come back under the title. Across the best-received trailers in the sample, the notes find a shared pattern: one premise the viewer can repeat in a sentence, one question the trailer refuses to answer, and one recurring image or sound. With the rule cards, "How many in your family?" left unanswered, and the bell, BUILD LIMIT has the first two and half of the third.

## Three other premises, and the one that could win

The owner asked for a judgement first. On the research, the reworked original remains the strongest vehicle for this production. It holds the most mature human core of any candidate, sits on the closed-society beat grid, and has a cold open the reels already prove. The rework is the title-as-lie change above, plus subtraction. Three alternatives aim at the owner's bar. Each must also earn "Build Limit" and keep the game's rules as the world's rules. Mechanics marked as vanilla behaviour in the pitches come from the writer's knowledge of the game, not from the notes, and each needs a pass with the minecraft-audit skill before scripting.

### Pitch A: "The Sky Is Built"

**Hook.** A city that has never seen rain learns that the build limit its founder taught it is a ceiling he built, and the founder is the heroine's father.

**The first minute.** Noon under a flat white sky. A funeral on the top terrace for a roofer "taken by the Limit". The heroine holds her dead mother's clock; at noon its dial shows the moon. A zombie crosses the bright market square and doesn't burn, and people step around it like a puddle. Her father, Keeper of the Limit, lays the last stone on the grave and says the creed: "Nothing is built to the Limit." She looks up. He says, without looking: "Don't." Title.

**Why it fits.** It is The Truman Show's design. The first anomaly falls from the sky at 3% of the script ([Truman script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/the-truman-show-1998.pdf), p.4), the final explanation is a temptation, and the last image is a door in the sky. The clock does what The Prestige's birdcage does, showing the whole trick in minute one ([SoapCentral](https://www.soapcentral.com/entertainment/did-know-big-twist-ending-the-prestige-actually-spoiled-beginning-movie)). The unmoving sun and the zombie that won't burn work like The Sixth Sense's colour code: plain, early, and misread. The heroine solves it from game rules every player in the audience knows, so the audience can check its own guess. The title promises the method, as Glass Onion's does. The human core is a father who built a sky to keep his daughter from what killed her mother. That is The Village's grief, held to Ebert's warning. What is above must be genuinely dangerous, so the reveal enlarges the world instead of shrinking it ([Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-2004-movie-so-bad-it-insulted-roger-ebert/)).

**How it earns the title.** "The Limit" is the city's word for its ceiling. The last act climbs past the false limit to the real one. In the final image she places the last block the world allows, and the game refuses the next: the only limit nobody built.

**Grounding.** In vanilla, a clock tracks the real sun anywhere in the Overworld; zombies burn only under open sky; rain needs open sky; and light blocks don't move. All four need confirming with the minecraft-audit.

**Risk.** A new set, and comparison with The Truman Show.

### Pitch B: "Topping Out"

**Hook.** The night the greatest builder alive places the last block his world allows, he falls from it, and the chat names the granddaughter he raised.

**The first minute.** Fireworks at build limit. The old builder places the final block, tries the one above, and the game refuses. He laughs: "That's as high as it goes." He hands her his water bucket ("You'll need it more than me."), a line built to read differently on rewatch. Cut to dawn. His items lie scattered on the plaza three hundred blocks down, on a despawn timer nobody knows is running. In the hall, the family reads the death message in chat. It names her. She knows she took his bucket.

**Why it fits.** This is the Knives Out move: show the audience early that the heroine believes she did it, and the mystery becomes suspense over whether she'll be caught ([Wikipedia: Knives Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knives_Out)). Johnson: "The least interesting part of a whodunnit is whodunnit" ([Salon](https://www.salon.com/2019/11/26/knives-out-rian-johnson-murder-mystery-agatha-christie)). The chat is Glass Onion's camera that cannot lie, while every character can. Every clue is a game rule the audience already knows, which satisfies Van Dine's requirement that the reader gets the same clues as the detective ([UMSL](https://www.umsl.edu/~gradyf/film/vandinerules.htm)). The build limit breaks the one alibi nobody can test: "I was building above him." The theme is mature: inheritance, and the ceiling on what one life can make.

**Grounding.** The death message's exact wording and the time window it uses to name a killer must be checked against vanilla 1.21.

**Risk.** It shares Knives Out's spine: a family, a dead patriarch, and the person closest to him who believes she killed him. It must resolve somewhere Knives Out doesn't. It also has no ritual society.

### Pitch C: "The Climb"

**Hook.** Floor 1 has never had a window. Tonight the Lows climb to build limit, floor by floor, led by messages from someone at the top.

**The first minute.** The head count on Floor 1. Keepers call numbers, and a mother refuses to sit. Like Curtis, she is counting the seconds between gates ([Snowpiercer script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/snowpiercer-2013.pdf), p.4). A Keeper takes a child "up to Forty". That night an allay drifts down the stairwell and drops a book: "Floor 12. Midnight."

**Why it fits.** Snowpiercer's car-by-car structure, "each transition marked by a bloody battle", is a ready-made chapter engine ([Slate](https://www.slate.com/articles/arts/movies/2014/06/snowpiercer_directed_by_bong_joon_ho_reviewed.html)). Its red-letter capsules arrive at about 8, 25, 37, 65 and 91%, and the last one flips: the villain wrote them ("I just wrote it. For you."). At the top waits Wilford's offer of his station. It reuses the existing tower, and each band of floors becomes a vanilla economy set by light.

**How it earns the title.** The top is both the prize and the trap; at build limit the climb ends, and the only way left is out.

**Risk.** It is the least original of the four. Slate's reviewer felt "a slight letdown" once Snowpiercer reached the engine. Vertical class towers have precedents outside the notes, such as The Platform and High-Rise (the writer's knowledge).

## Conclusion

The research's deepest lesson for BUILD LIMIT is that Minecraft grounding is not set dressing but the film's moral argument. In every story the notes praise, the game's rules are the one honest authority: the void in Parkour Civilization, canon death in Dream SMP, the bucket placed in the last tick. Every lie is something people built on top of them. A premise where the villains pass their own law off as the world's physics, and the hero wins with a real game rule they forgot to fear, earns the title "Build Limit", answers the Parkour Civilization comparison, and gives a prestige-level idea to a story whose heart is already a promise across a gulf. The structure needs no new beats; the work is subtraction and reassignment. Cut the trick to what one shot can show. Let Buh earn what Ash, Fen and Wren now hand him. Let the Below keep a scar. Make the worst loss a deliberate horror.

For the teaser, the finding runs against instinct. Its proportions are already right. The fixes are about withholding the answer and showing more world: shorter cards over picture, less black, the bell as the rhythm, and one line that tells the audience why the film is called what it is. The notes can't say how prestige features such as Interstellar or Arrival handle these choices, because they contain no sources on those screenplays. Testing the reworked Chapter 1 against them would take a separate pass.
