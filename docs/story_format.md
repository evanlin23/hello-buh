# The Buh Stories format: the Roblox story Short, in Minecraft

Owner, 2026-11-11 (after a friend watched Gold for the Portal: "im confsued bro, feel like i just wasted 10 seconds of
my life bro"): "We should be following the format, the tried and true format that already works for the Roblox
stories, but now we're doing Minecraft stories instead." Source: docs/story_research_mystery.md (the 2-7M-view rule
Shorts) and docs/research/story_craft_research.md 12 (blocky's 27-hit study). Every Buh Stories script follows this.

## The premise (owner, 2026-11-12)
A crazy Roblox-story premise: "In my town, nobody...", "In my world, everyone...", "My twin...", "This man adopted 10
kids and...". A strange rule or impossible fact about people, a family or a town. Minecraft is the look (the world,
the characters, the places), not the twist: the twist does NOT need to be a game mechanic. blocky-stories (the
owner's Roblox-style channel) is a premise source.

## The beats (time is a guide at Pip's pace)
1. **0-3 s, the genre opener + the impossible fact + the question, said out loud.** Open with "In my village..."
   (a local rule), "In my world..." (a rule for everyone) or "My twin / my grandpa / my best friend..." (owner, 2026-11-12). Line 1 is a strange, concrete, countable fact that
   touches everyone, and it makes the "why?" explicit: "This man adopted 10 kids, and not a single one came out alive."
   "In this town, nobody is allowed to speak." Ours: "In my village, every sheep is BLUE. And if one turns RED, Dad says RUN." The fact makes you ask why; never a stock "nobody will tell me why" tag (owner, 2026-11-12).
   The picture shows the fact by 2 s.
2. **3-15 s, the rule in action: 3-5 quick examples**, a cut every 1-2 s, each one a clue that reads two ways.
3. **The narrator is the exception**: the one who doesn't know, wants to know, and says the viewer's question for
   them ("I had to find out WHY.").
4. **Someone they love is at stake** (a lamb, a grandpa, a friend, a dog).
5. **The climax turns the rule against itself**: the narrator breaks the rule or goes to look.
6. **The reveal is SAID, in one plain sentence a casual player understands with no game knowledge**, over the picture
   that proves it: "Every kid he brought home was already dying." Our naive narrator says what they SEE, not what it
   means: "It was wearing Grandpa's HELMET." The viewer makes the one jump. Never a wordless reveal; never a reveal that
   needs a mechanic the viewer wasn't shown.
7. **One beat, then cut.** At most one short line or image after the reveal (the sting), then end within ~1 s. The last
   line echoes or reframes line 1, so the loop works.

## What carries over from our own rules
- Pip, a naive narrator who misreads (STANDARDS 0a); the misreadings ARE the two-way clues.
- Named things on screen. A game mechanic is optional; when one is used, it's shown and true.
- Same-place links to other stories, background only (5a). Each story stands alone.
- A casual viewer gets it in one sentence after one watch (0z).
- Length: tight like the hits (they run 25-45 s); never padded, but not cut for its own sake (r:r08fd98).

## What it replaces
- "Cut on the reveal" with no words: the reveal is now one spoken line (the cut still lands ~1 s after it).
- "Don't say what the picture already says" still holds for clues, but not for the reveal line.
- Names: no name the viewer hasn't been told who it is (Gold's "Nico").
