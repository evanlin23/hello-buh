# FINAL REPORT

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend
"So there's this huge Minecraft tower, forty floors, and they hit build limit so they can't go higher. Once a year everyone does this jump: you jump from whatever floor and if you land it, you get to live on that floor. Forty is the rich floor. Every year exactly one person dies. The main kid, Buh, lost his brother Ash that way three years ago. With an old guy named Fen and a rich girl from the top floor (the Highkeeper's granddaughter), he figures out it's rigged. Whenever a family gets bigger, the Highkeeper makes that family choose someone to go, gives them an empty bucket, and rings a bell twice. The bell runs a piston that moves the landing pool so they miss and fall into the fog. But Fen secretly dug a lake at the bottom, so all thirty 'dead' people are alive in a camp down there, Ash included, and their mom picked Ash. So on Drop Day they plan to expose it with the whole town watching. Buh jumps from 40 and calls his family 'thirty-three,' counting everyone who was sent down. The Highkeeper's own grandson is born that morning, so he jumps himself instead of making anyone choose. Turns out he lands in Fen's lake too. Everyone comes up, the mom sees Ash, and they decide to build outward instead of up. Then at the end they hear something in the fog that's 'not an echo'."

What I couldn't tell my friend: how the Drop works exactly (who jumps, how often), what the landing looks like, how Buh got back up from the Below, who sabotaged the lever room, what happened to Dace, and what the 8 thing was.

## Plot holes and contradictions
1. **Buh goes down and comes back; the thirty can't.** (BAD.) "The trapdoor only opens from the top" is the reason nobody escapes, but Buh goes down Fen's ladder and is home on Floor 3 the next scene. If there's a ladder, the thirty can use it. If Fen can open the trapdoor, Fen could have ended this any day in thirty years. This is the hole the comments will find first.
2. **One fall a year can't balance a city's births.** (MEDIUM.) "Every year a family gets bigger, someone falls" plus "One a year. Never two. Never none." A forty-floor city has more than one birth a year. If two families grow, who falls? Either the city is tiny (say so) or the rule is something else (say that).
3. **"Then it's me this year" contradicts the rule just stated.** (MEDIUM.) Buh's family shrank. The suspense of "And if it's me?" rests on a misunderstanding the script never admits. If it's deliberate (Buh is wrong, the grandson is the real trigger), have someone point it out.
4. **The Highkeeper's plan vs. the cut wire.** (MEDIUM.) If somebody cut the wire before the drop, the Well can't move. So how does anyone "fall" this year? The Highkeeper presumably jumps off the side on purpose. But then what was the empty bucket / "His bucket" for, and who cut the wire? The mechanics of the climax don't add up by ear.
5. **Nobody notices for thirty years.** (MINOR.) The whole city watches the Drop. The Well slides a block, a jumper with an empty bucket misses it, two rings every time. Fine for a fable, but one line ("Nobody watches the bottom, they watch the jumper") would cover it.
6. **"It was never about room" vs. "The tower is full."** (MINOR.) The theme line fights the plot.
7. **Ash's cold-open line "I'll hear it from the top."** (MINOR / probably fine.) He already knew he was the one (he "made" Mom choose him). Plays as a lie to his little brother, which is sad and works, but only if someone acknowledges it later. Nobody does.
8. **Dace's three-person family "all moving to Forty."** (MINOR.) Is "the floor you jump from" for the jumper or the whole family? The Plank asks "How many in your family?" so I assume the whole family moves, but it's never said.

## Best 3 moments
1. **"Thirty-three. Mom. Pip. Me. And the thirty you sent down."** It turns the movie's refrain into an accusation, works completely by ear, and gets chills.
2. **"Took you long enough, Buh."** The part-2 ending. A voice we know from the cold open, saying the hero's name, from a place everyone says nobody comes back from. Perfect audio reveal.
3. **"He doesn't pick. He makes the family pick." / "Mom picked." and then Mom: "I said take me. They don't let you choose yourself."** The real gut punch of the movie, all in dialogue.
(Honorable mention: the market chant. "The floor you jump from is the floor you live on" with the crowd repeating it is a great world-rule delivery.)

## Worst 3 moments
1. **The climax is close to silent.** Highfall (no lines), the lever room (one line), Dace (one line, no outcome), Buh's POV fall (no sound written, no bell), the Needle reveal (two lines), Highfall and the Below (no lines). The movie trains me to listen for the bell and then doesn't use it when it matters. For a listener the ending is a blur with one great speech in the middle.
2. **Scenes 11-14 in a row:** "No no no" / "You swapped it" (swapped what?), "Eight." (eight what?), "Fifteen. Sixteen. Seventeen?" (counting what?). Three scenes where the information is purely visual. That's where I'd lose the thread while doing dishes.
3. **The cold open's "8" joke.** The second exchange of the movie is a visual pun that's meaningless by ear, and it's never paid off in dialogue. It makes the first 20 seconds feel like I missed something.

## Top 5 changes (most important first)
1. **Make the climax audible and built around the bell.** The movie spends two acts teaching me one ring = safe, two rings = the Well moves. Then the climax drops the bell entirely. Script it: Dace jumps, one ring, splash, crowd cheers (we hear Dace survive). Buh steps up, gets the empty bucket ("Empty." / "I know."), jumps. One ring... and the second ring doesn't come, because the wire's cut. Make that silence the held breath of the movie. Then explain who cut it out loud (the Highkeeper: "I cut it myself this morning"). Give the reunion more than one word: Ash speaks to Mom, Pip is in the scene, the crowd reacts. Right now four of the last nine scenes have no dialogue, and the half-watcher misses the ending.
2. **Close the Buh-goes-down-and-comes-back hole, and say why Buh is needed.** One line each: how Buh gets back up ("Fen hauls me up in the bucket lift. It only takes one" / "the ladder only goes down"), and why Fen can't just open the trapdoor ("It's at the foot of the Needle. Highkeeper's men watch it every day but Drop Day"). Otherwise the whole "nobody can come back" premise falls apart at the first rewatch.
3. **Say what the Well is in Act 1, and spell out the Drop.** One line at the market or on the Plank: "Forty floors down into the Well. Hit the water, the floor is yours. Miss it, and the Below keeps you." That makes "the Well got hungry", "the Well moves one block" and the bucket mean something the first time I hear them, without rewinding. Also say once whether the whole family moves up with the jumper (the "How many in your family?" question depends on it).
4. **Fix the "why Buh" thread, or cut it.** By the rule, Buh's family can't be picked this year. Either (a) have Tilly read the ledger out loud, "Buh, brother of Ash, the bucket," and make the Highkeeper's reason explicit (he's planning to dodge his own grandson's turn by pinning it on Buh, then changes his mind on the Plank when Buh says "Thirty-three"), or (b) make "His bucket" mean the Highkeeper's own, and have Tilly say so: "It's his name. Grandpa wrote his own name." Either way it has to be SAID. Right now the main suspense question of Act 2B ("And if it's me?") is built on a clue only the eyes get and a rule that says no.
5. **Say the names, fix the sound-alikes, rescue or cut the visual puns.** Have "Tilly" and "Dace" said aloud early and often. Rename Dace (it's "days"). Plant "Orlo" before the last scene. Say the countdown out loud at least once ("Six days to the Drop, genius"). Either make the 8 audible and pay it off (Ash scratched 8s on the fog ledge so Buh would know he's alive, and Buh says "It's his eight. He's alive.") or cut it from the cold open and cut scene 13. Same for "Fifteen. Sixteen. Seventeen?": add the noun ("Seventeen families... he grew every one of them by one"). And rewrite "It was never about room" so it doesn't contradict "the tower is full" (e.g. "Forty floors up, and nobody ever looked down").

## Overall
Interest by part: 7, 8, 8, 7. I'd finish it. The premise, the chant, "Took you long enough, Buh", "Mom picked" and "Thirty-three" are a strong spine that works fully by ear. The problems are where the story hands the important information to the eyes (the 8, the swap, the counting, the name tag, the ladder, the climax), and one big logic hole (Buh's round trip vs. the trapdoor rule).
