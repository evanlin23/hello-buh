## Part 1 log (cold open + Act 1)

### 1. What I think the story is so far
A vertical tower-world: people live on 40 floors, the poor "Lows" under the clouds, the "Highs" above. At 16 every kid does the Drop: jump from a floor into one block of water (the Well) on top of a pillar; the floor you jump from is the floor you get to live on. Three years ago Buh's older brother Ash jumped from 40 and died, and the cold open makes it plain it was rigged: two bell strokes, the water slides one block, and his bucket is empty. Now Buh (15) wants to jump from 40 too. The Highkeeper writes his name in a book. At the end of the act an allay rises out of the fog when Buh plays the brothers' song and gives him a lodestone compass that points straight down. Buh thinks Ash is alive.

### 2. Confusion log
- **"How many in your family?" / "Four." / the Highkeeper looks at him a moment too long.** HOOKED. Clearly the count matters and is a death sentence, but I don't know why yet. Good.
- **DONG. DONG. Then the Well slides one block left, then the bucket is empty.** HOOKED, with a small LOST. The bell = signal = someone moves the water, which works. But I wasn't sure whether the empty bucket and the moving Well are one plan or two separate sabotages, or why a jumper gets a bucket at all if the target is the Well (is the bucket a backup clutch? is it a ritual?). Spell out the bucket's job once, early, maybe at the Practice Ledge. If the audience doesn't know the bucket is the jumper's backup, "empty bucket" doesn't hit as hard as it should.
- **"For eight frames the hotbar shows it."** ANNOYED for a screenplay reader, fine as a director's note. Do eight frames read on YouTube? Probably only on a rewatch, which may be the point. If it's a clue the audience is meant to catch on first viewing, hold it longer.
- **Geography: "Below him, forty floors of lanterns fall away, then the clouds, then the Needle"** vs Floor 3 being "under the clouds" and the Highkeeper coming "below the clouds." ANNOYED. As written it reads as if every floor is above the clouds. I worked out that the clouds sit somewhere between Floor 10ish and 40, but the image line contradicts that. One establishing wide of the whole tower (Lows / cloud layer / Highs / Needle / fog) would fix it.
- **Upside down on the page, Buh can read his own name.** LOST, slightly. Of course his name is in the book, he's signing up. What makes this sinister? Is it underlined, crossed out, in a different column, next to a mark? It needs one visual detail that makes it wrong (a tick, a red mark, the same mark that's beside "ASH" further up the page).
- **Mom: "She's asleep. Don't you dare wake her."** ANNOYED. Feels like a non sequitur answer to "I want her next birthday above the clouds." I can read it as Mom shutting the subject down, but it plays as the wrong line. Could be a deliberate deflection; if so, let the beat breathe.
- **Tilly's family: "Grandpa says the Highkeeper's family jumps from 40."** Slight LOST. I inferred Tilly is the Highkeeper's granddaughter, but the line is worded as if Grandpa is quoting someone else. Say it straight once.
- **"Fen says the fog echoes."** ANNOYED. Who is Fen? A name dropped with no face.
- **The bell rings in the fog with no jump, and the "echo" exchange.** HOOKED. Nice plant: something down there has a bell.
- **Lodestone compass spinning "as if whatever it points at is right underneath him."** HOOKED. Clear and eerie. The allay coming to the note block is a great use of a real mechanic.
- **"8UH'S ROOM."** HOOKED in a small way. Feels like a plant (Ash's 8 will come back as a signature or a code).

### 3. Predictions
- The Highkeeper runs a population cull. The tower can't grow past build limit, so space is fixed; when a family gets too big (or a High family needs room, e.g. Tilly's mom's baby due on Drop Day), he marks a kid from a "too large" family and the bell signals someone below to move the Well. "There's always room on Forty" is a lie, or room is made.
- Buh is marked for this year's Drop, to make room for Tilly's new sibling. That's why Grandpa is "weird all week" and why Buh's name looked wrong in the book.
- Ash is alive below the fog. The Below isn't empty: the fallen land somewhere (water, a lower world, the ground) and there's a community down there with a bell. Ash sent the allay. "The Below keeps what it takes" is literal: people are kept, not killed.
- Fen is someone who knows about the Below (maybe a Keeper or an old jumper).
- Bigger guess: "build limit" is a lie and the world goes on below, or there's another tower.

### 4. Interest
8/10. Yes, keep watching. The cold open is a real hook: a death that's clearly murder, a ritual with rules I understand in thirty seconds, and a mystery object at the act break. The brothers' song plus the allay is a lovely emotional device. My main worry is clarity of the rules (bucket, book, geography), not interest.

## Part 2 log (Act 2A)

### 1. What I think the story is so far
The Drop is a cull. Since the tower hit build limit thirty years ago, exactly one jumper "misses" each year (thirty tally marks on the Needle), timed to the one family that grew. The Highkeeper keeps the ledger, Ash was crossed out the year Pip was born, this year's page is torn out with a note that "the Buh boy asks too many questions," and there's one empty bucket in his chest of seventeen. Ash, via the allay, warns Buh not to take the Highkeeper's bucket. Buh climbs down Fen's hidden ladder inside the Needle, through the fog, and finds a green world under it with a lake, a village, and Ash, alive at 19.

### 2. Confusion log
- **Fen: "Not hungry yet, are you. Five more days."** HOOKED. Talking to the Well like a beast is a great creepy-old-man beat.
- **"One a year. Thirty years. Never two. Never none." + the ledger: "Every year, one family has a +1."** HOOKED as a mystery, but it raises a logic problem I'll log under plot holes: is there really exactly one birth per year in a 40-floor tower? If two families have babies, do two kids fall? "Never two" says no. The rule needs one sentence: maybe the tower allows exactly one birth a year (a licence, a lottery), and the cull pays for it. Right now kids in the comments will do the maths.
- **"FEN: Twice. It rings twice for the one." / "Why?" / "Echo."** HOOKED. Fen is lying and we know it.
- **"the Needle, one block wide" vs a stool "next to" the Well, a hammock "by the Well," a trapdoor under the stool.** ANNOYED. If the top is one block wide there's no room for Fen. Either the "one block wide" belongs to the steps or the Needle's top is wider than the cold open suggests. Also relevant to the cold-open mechanic: the Well sliding "one block left" only makes sense if there's a surface to slide onto.
- **Dace swaps the bucket.** Works as a red herring and an action beat, and the ladder save is the first time Buh's skill solves a problem. Dace's motive ("Scared people jump from Floor 10") reads clearly. Small LOST: I'm still not 100% on the bucket's role in the real Drop. Here Buh "switches to his bucket" on the way down, so the bucket is his clutch; on the Needle the Well is the target. Is the bucket the backup, or are jumpers expected to clutch on the Well block? Pick one and say it.
- **The name tag "DON'T TAKE HIS 8UCKET."** HOOKED, small LOST. The 8 pays off the cold open instantly, which is satisfying. But "his" took me a second: the Highkeeper's? Fen's? I settled on "the bucket the Highkeeper hands you." It fits the empty seventeenth bucket two scenes later, which helps.
- **Fen climbs inside the Needle "with a sack."** HOOKED. Supplies for the Below? Something for the Well?
- **Tilly slips into the Highkeeper's study and finds everything in one go** (ledger, Ash crossed out, torn page, margin note, the chest, the empty bucket). ANNOYED. It's the most convenient scene in the draft so far: no lock, no guard, no near-miss, and four clues in one shot. It reads like an exposition drop. This is a set piece waiting to happen.
- **Why is the page torn out if he also leaves a note about Buh in the margin?** Minor LOST. If he wanted to hide the choice he'd hide the note too. Reads as the writer needing both a mystery and a clue.
- **"The Buh boy asks too many questions."** HOOKED, but it bends the system. The cull was about the family that grew (Tilly's mom's baby). Buh's family already shrank. So this year the Highkeeper is choosing Buh for a different reason: punishment, silencing. That's interesting (the system is corrupt, not only cruel), but nobody in the scene says it, so it may read as a rule change rather than a choice.
- **Ten seconds of white on the ladder.** HOOKED. Great quiet beat before the reveal.
- **The Below.** HOOKED. Clear, big and beautiful. But it immediately raises: if a ladder goes straight down to the Below, why has nobody come back up in thirty years? Why hasn't Ash climbed it in three? Fen guarding a trapdoor isn't enough of a wall on its own. I'm waiting for an answer.

### 3. Predictions
- The fallen land in the round lake (fog plus height into deep water = survivable), and the Below holds the thirty "dead." Fen supplies them through the Needle; the sack is food or tools.
- The Highkeeper knows. The Drop is exile, not murder, and the lie protects the hierarchy: if the Lows learned there's a whole green world below, nobody would fight for Floor 40, and the Highs would lose their power. Or the reverse twist: the Highkeeper thinks they die, and Fen (or Fen's dad) built the lake as a secret mercy.
- Something keeps the fallen from climbing back: a rule, a threat to their families, or they choose not to (life is better below). Ash will have a reason he never came back, and Buh will be hurt by it.
- Act 2B conflict: Ash wants to stay or wants Buh to bring Mom and Pip down; Buh wants to go back up and end the Drop for everyone. The brothers disagree.
- The finale is on Drop Day: Buh jumps from 40 (or 41, building past the limit, per his Act 1 line) knowing what's below, in front of everyone, to expose it. Tilly's baby sibling is the +1, so the Highkeeper's own family is the reason for this year's fall, which should land on him somehow.

### 4. Interest
8.5/10. Yes. The midpoint reveal (green world, Ash alive, "Took you long enough") is exactly where it should be and turns the story from "who's killing us" to "why are they lying." Pace is very fast; I'm getting clues faster than I can feel them.

## Part 3 log (Act 2B)

### 1. What I think the story is so far
Below the fog, the thirty "ones" are alive: Fen dug a lake under the Needle in the first year, and every fallen jumper landed in it. The cull is mechanical: a bell wired down forty floors to a sticky piston under the Well, two rings and the Well shifts one block, and the one gets an empty bucket so they can't clutch. The Highkeeper doesn't choose the one; he makes the family that grew choose, and Mom chose Ash (because Ash talked her into it to spare 12-year-old Buh). Buh confronts Mom, who can't hear that Ash is alive. The plan for Drop Day: Tilly cuts the bell wire on 40, Dace jumps just before Buh as a body in the Well, and Buh jumps last and opens the trapdoor so thirty "dead" people climb out of the fog in front of the whole tower.

### 2. Confusion log
- **"Twenty-nine people around the fires. Kids, grown-ups, an old woman with white hair." / "Everybody here is a 'one'."** LOST, and a plot hole. The oldest possible "one" fell thirty years ago; if they fell at 16 they're 46, not a white-haired old woman. And kids? Either kids were born down here (then they're not "ones," and the head count of thirty is off), or the "one" can be anyone in the family, not just a 16-year-old jumper. Mom's line "They don't let you choose yourself" suggests adults can be the one, which then begs: how does an old woman "fall" if the Drop is for 16-year-olds? Decide who can be the one and how they're made to fall.
- **Head count: 29 at the fires + Ash = 30.** ANNOYED. I had to do the arithmetic. Say "twenty-nine, and me."
- **"Fen dug that lake the first year."** HOOKED. Fen flips from creepy gatekeeper to secret saviour in one line. Great reversal, but it's delivered as a line of dialogue about a man who isn't in the scene. Fen's choice is one of the best ideas in the script and it happens off-screen.
- **"The trapdoor only opens from the top."** LOST for a Minecraft audience. Thirty people in a whole green survival world with a lake, trees and presumably stone can mine, build a pillar, or punch through a trapdoor. "Just pillar up" will be the top comment. Ash's other reason ("Go back and they send you down again") is the real one and is stronger; lean on that and make the physical barrier something Minecraft players accept (an iron trapdoor plus Keepers at the top, obsidian, a Needle so tall a pillar is pointless, or the fallen choosing not to).
- **Does the Highkeeper know the fallen live?** LOST. This is the biggest missing piece. If he knows, the Drop is exile and the lie is about power. If he doesn't, Fen has been defying him for thirty years. The meaning of the whole system, and of the finale, hangs on this and the script hasn't said.
- **If there's a whole green world under the fog, why is "the tower full"?** HOOKED, and close to LOST. "One in, one out" only makes sense if nobody up top knows there's land. That's the thematic engine (the limit is a lie, the world is bigger), but no one names it yet. I want a character to say it, or to be shown it.
- **The flashback: "This time we watch the Well slide. It was there the whole time."** ANNOYED. What was there the whole time? The piston? The clue? We already saw the Well slide in the cold open, so the replay adds nothing unless it shows something new: the piston arm, the redstone, the lake beneath. Show a new detail or cut the replay.
- **The single bell in the fog in Act 1 (DONG, then nothing).** Still unexplained. Ash says one ring is "just a bell." So who rang it the night Tilly visited, and why from the fog? Either pay it off (the Below has its own bell; a test ring from 40) or cut it.
- **"He doesn't pick. He makes the family pick." / "Mom picked."** HOOKED, strongly. Best twist so far. It reframes every Mom beat (the curtain, "Don't look," Floor 10, "don't you dare wake her," protecting Pip) and gives Buh something real to feel. It also exposes a problem: by that rule this year's family is Tilly's (her mom's baby is due on Drop Day), so why is the Highkeeper's note about Buh? I'm assuming he is breaking his own rule to protect his own family and silence Buh. If so, someone needs to say it, because that hypocrisy is the villain's whole case.
- **Sticky piston moving the Well.** ANNOYED for the Minecraft-literate. Pistons can't push or pull a water source block in vanilla. Worth a check with whoever audits the mechanics: it may need to be a block under the water, a waterlogged block, or the water being placed/removed by dispensers.
- **Mom scene: "I said me. I said take me. They don't let you choose yourself."** HOOKED. Lovely line. But the whole scene is ten lines and ends on "Go to bed, Buh." This is the emotional core and it plays as a beat, not a scene.
- **Dace: "I'll jump right before you. If the Well does anything weird, I'll be standing in it."** LOST on mechanics and ANNOYED on character. How does standing in the water stop a piston? Does he block it? And Dace turns from bucket-swapping rival to self-sacrificing ally in four lines because he "heard everything." Unearned.
- **Buh and Tilly are 15 but the Drop is for "sixteen-year-olds."** ANNOYED. Either make them 16 or say their birthday falls before Drop Day.

### 3. Predictions
- The plan breaks on Drop Day. Most likely failure points: Tilly is caught in the lever room by her grandfather, or the bell has a second wire; the Highkeeper hands the empty bucket to someone we don't expect (Tilly herself, as this year's real +1 family, or Dace).
- The Highkeeper's family really is this year's +1, and the climax forces him to face it: his rule now takes one of his own. Either he tries to dodge it with Buh, or Tilly volunteers.
- Buh pays off "Then I'll jump from forty-one": he goes above the limit (a block on the roof, a pillar on the Plank) and turns the jump into a public statement. Or "41" ends up being the Below: the floor you jump from becomes the world you live on.
- Thirty people climb out of the fog. Mom sees Ash. The song (low, low, high, low) is the cue for the reunion, and Mom finally opens the curtain on the Drop Face.
- Fen opens the trapdoor at the crucial moment if Buh can't.
- The ending reframes "build limit": there was never a limit, only a direction nobody looked.

### 4. Interest
8/10. Yes. "Mom picked" is a gut-punch and it's the strongest piece of writing so far. But Act 2B is almost entirely people explaining things to each other (bell, piston, bucket, lake, plan), and the one scene with real emotional weight (Mom) is cut off just as it starts. I'm following easily; I'm feeling less than I should.

# FINAL REPORT

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend
There's this giant tower that hit build limit, forty floors, poor people at the bottom under the clouds and rich people at the top. When you turn sixteen you do the Drop: you jump off a floor into one block of water on a stone pillar, and whatever floor you jump from is the floor you get to live on. Except every year exactly one kid "misses." It turns out it's rigged. A bell wired down the tower moves the water one block, and the Highkeeper hands that kid an empty bucket so they can't clutch. Since there's no room to build up, every time a family has a baby, someone has to go, and the Highkeeper makes the family pick who.

The main kid, Buh, lost his big brother Ash that way three years ago. Then a little allay comes up out of the fog with a compass and a name tag spelled the way only Ash spelled things, and Buh climbs down a secret ladder inside the pillar and finds a whole green world under the fog. All thirty "dead" kids are alive, because the grumpy old Well-keeper, Fen, secretly dug a lake for them to land in. Then the gut-punch: Mom picked Ash, because Ash told her to pick him instead of twelve-year-old Buh. They plan to expose it on Drop Day. But when Buh jumps, the Highkeeper's own grandson has just been born, and instead of making his family choose, he jumps off himself. Buh opens the trapdoor, thirty people climb out in front of the whole city, Mom sees Ash, the Highkeeper turns out to be alive in the lake too, and everyone moves down to the green world. At the end they see other towers on the horizon and hear another double bell, so it's happening everywhere.

(What came across: the premise, the rig, the Mom twist, the reveal. What didn't: what Buh himself did to win, and why "it was never about room.")

## Unanswered questions

### GOOD AMBIGUITY (comment-section fuel)
- **Who cut the second lever's wire?** Almost certainly the Highkeeper, but nobody says so. People will argue whether it was him or Fen. Keep it, but make sure one frame (redstone dust on his sleeve, his pickaxe) lets the attentive viewer win the argument.
- **Did the Highkeeper mean to give Buh the empty bucket before "Thirty-three"?** His hand hangs over it. Was he still undecided, or always planning to jump? Good if the film then gives one more clue either way.
- **The other towers.** Do they have lakes? Is anyone alive under them? Who built the towers, and why do they all stop at build limit? Strong sequel bait.
- **Did Fen do the right thing?** He saved thirty lives and let thirty families grieve for thirty years. That's a great argument to leave open, if the film acknowledges it on screen.
- **The 8.** Ash's 8-for-B as a signature is a lovely detail people will hunt for on rewatch.

### CONFUSING (feels like a mistake)
- **The margin note "The Buh boy asks too many questions" and the torn-out page.** Set up as a threat to Buh, never explained or paid off. Since the Highkeeper ends up sacrificing himself, the note reads as a writer's red herring.
- **The old woman with white hair and the kids in the camp.** The oldest "one" fell thirty years ago. If ones are sixteen-year-old jumpers, nobody down there can be old. If kids were born below, they aren't ones and the head count is wrong.
- **Who can be "the one," and how they fall.** Mom says "They don't let you choose yourself," implying an adult could be picked, but the only mechanism shown is the sixteen-year-olds' Drop.
- **Exactly one +1 a year.** One birth a year in a forty-floor city, every year, for thirty years.
- **Why Buh steers left with a full bucket.** It contradicts what Ash told him.
- **"It was never about room."** The film doesn't show what it was about instead.
- **The cold-open geography** ("forty floors of lanterns fall away, then the clouds, then the Needle," while Floor 3 is "under the clouds").
- **Fen's unexplained name in Act 1, and "Highfall" first named in a scene heading in Act 3.**
- **"It was there the whole time"** in the Act 2B flashback: it shows nothing new.

## Plot holes and contradictions (with severity)
1. **The fallen could just mine or pillar up.** HIGH for this audience. Thirty survival players in a green world are kept below by "the trapdoor only opens from the top." Minecraft viewers will reject that instantly. The better reason is already in the script ("Go back and they send you down again"): make staying below their choice, give it weight, and make the physical barrier believable (an iron trapdoor, Keepers above, an unclimbable Needle), or drop the trapdoor line.
2. **The hero's climactic fall has no danger.** HIGH (a structure problem more than logic). He holds a full bucket, the Well doesn't move, and he knows a lake waits below anyway. The only tension is self-inflicted and unmotivated.
3. **The margin note / torn page about Buh go nowhere.** MEDIUM-HIGH. It reads as an abandoned subplot.
4. **Fen "couldn't stop your bell."** MEDIUM. He sits on the piston with a private trapdoor into the Needle. Give a concrete reason he couldn't (tampering rings an alarm on 40; the Keepers inspect it; the Highkeeper would just push the one off the side instead, so a lake is the only sabotage that can't be detected).
5. **The ages and kids among the "ones."** MEDIUM.
6. **One birth a year, exactly.** MEDIUM. One line fixes it: births need the Highkeeper's licence, one a year, and the price of the licence is the Drop.
7. **Pistons can't move water source blocks in vanilla.** MEDIUM for this audience. Have the piston move the block the water sits on (or swap a waterlogged block, or use a dispenser pair). Worth a mechanics check before filming.
8. **The exiles never noticed the other towers in thirty years.** MEDIUM. Either Ash knew (better: it's his secret and a sequel thread) or the Below near the lake is boxed in (a valley, nights full of mobs, nobody dared go far).
9. **The Highkeeper never knew about the ladder or the lake,** with Fen going down with a sack in full view (Buh sees it from Floor 3). LOW-MEDIUM. Make Fen's trips night-only and covered.
10. **Buh and Tilly are 15 at a "sixteen-year-olds" Drop.** LOW.
11. **The plan's three jobs.** Tilly's wire, Dace in the Well and Buh's trapdoor: two of them evaporate. Not a hole, but a broken promise to the audience. MEDIUM.
12. **"One ring is just a bell" vs two levers.** LOW.

## Best 3 moments
1. **"How many in your family?" / "Thirty-three."** The census question has been repeated since the cold open, and the payoff turns it into a public accusation. Perfect use of a refrain.
2. **"He doesn't pick. He makes the family pick. ... Mom picked."** And then Mom's "I said take me. They don't let you choose yourself." It reframes every earlier Mom beat (the curtain, "Don't look," Floor 10) and is the moral heart of the film.
3. **The cold open fall.** Two bell strokes, the blue dot sliding one block, the empty bucket in the hotbar, then Mom's hand on the curtain: "Don't look." A complete, wordless set-up of the world, the rig and the grief in about two minutes. (Close runner-up: Fen's "Thirty years, Orlo. I couldn't stop your bell. So I dug a lake.")

## Worst 3 moments
1. **Buh's climactic fall (scene 26).** Full bucket, no second ring, a lake below, and a pointless steer-left. The movie's biggest set piece has no stakes, and the hero doesn't cause the resolution.
2. **"Somebody beat me to it."** Tilly's climax is taken from her by an off-screen character, and Dace's plan role vanishes too. The plan scene promises three jobs and pays off one.
3. **"Mom holds Ash and doesn't let go," inside a time-lapse.** The emotional payoff the whole film is built toward, cut down to a montage line. The brothers never get their real scene either.
(Dishonourable mention: Tilly's ledger scene, where four clues are found in one unguarded room in one shot.)

## Persona section: story editor

### Structure as I experienced it
In the 20-minute draft, the proportions are textbook, and that's a strength:
- **Cold open (scenes 1-4):** Ash's rigged fall. Excellent hook.
- **Act 1 (5-9):** world, rules, Buh's want (Floor 40 for Pip), the census, Tilly, the bell in the fog. **Act 1 break: the allay and the compass, "Ash?"** It lands at about 25%, which is right, and it turns the story from grief to search.
- **Act 2A (10-17):** investigation. Fen and the tally marks, the Dace fake-out, the name tag, the ledger, the descent. **Midpoint: the Below and Ash alive.** It lands at about 50% and turns the question from "who's killing us?" to "why are they lying?" The strongest structural beat in the draft.
- **Act 2B (18-21):** explanation, "Mom picked," the Mom confrontation, the plan. **This is where the structure sags.** There is no reversal against the hero and no all-is-lost moment. Everyone Buh asks says yes (Ash, Tilly, Dace), nobody opposes the plan, and the act ends on a smooth "DROP DAY" card. "Mom picked" is a great internal turn, but it doesn't change anything Buh does. He confronts Mom, she says "Go to bed," and the plot carries on unaffected.
- **Act 3 (22-31):** a chain of seven turns: the wire already cut, the full bucket, no second ring, the Highkeeper jumps, the trapdoor, Fen and Orlo, the other towers. Each one is a real turn on its own, but almost all of them are done *to* Buh, or done by Fen, the Highkeeper or the unknown wire-cutter. **The hero's agency in the climax amounts to one line ("Thirty-three") and opening a trapdoor.** The antagonist resolves the plot by choice, which makes him the protagonist of Act 3.

**Which twists turn the story?** The allay (yes), Dace's swap (a good fake-out), the ledger (yes, it raises the stakes on Buh), the Below (a big turn), Mom picked (yes, internally; it needs a plot consequence), the cut wire (it turns, but deflates Tilly), the Highkeeper's jump (yes, the best late twist, but it needs to be caused by Buh), the other towers (yes, it reframes the world and is a great button).

### Character arcs
- **Buh.** *Want:* jump from 40 like Ash, and give Pip a birthday above the clouds. *Need (implied):* to stop living in Ash's shadow and break the game instead of winning it. The draft sets this up (Ash took his place; "Then I'll jump from forty-one") but never makes him *feel* it or *choose* it. He "can't breathe" for one line and then plans logistics. He never has to decide to put himself in danger for someone else the way Ash did, which is the obvious mirror the story is asking for. **Payoff: partial.** He ends where the theme wants him (looking out, not up), but the change happened around him. The Act 1 "forty-one" line is never paid off.
- **Ash.** Static. Cocky in the cold open, cocky at 19, right about everything. The one crack ("He laughs. Then he doesn't.") is the most interesting thing about him and it goes nowhere. Three years alone below, knowing his mother agreed to it: that's anger, loneliness, maybe resentment of Pip or Buh. **Payoff: none on screen.** His reunions with Buh (one quip) and Mom (one montage line) are both underplayed.
- **Mom (Marla).** The best-designed arc in the draft: guilt, then denial ("Don't." / "Go to bed"), then sight ("rips the curtain open." / "Ash."). Every beat is right; every beat is one line. **Payoff: lands as an image, not as an emotion.**
- **The Highkeeper (Orlo).** A villain who turns out to be a tragic man who made families choose for thirty years and finally chooses himself. A great turn, lightly seeded ("a moment too long," "gently: Three, son"). But the margin note muddies it, we never see him alone with his guilt before the end, and his choice isn't caused by the hero. **Payoff: strong in concept, under-motivated in execution.**
- **Tilly.** The rebel granddaughter and investigator, set up with her own Drop ("Floor 1, imagine his face") and her family's baby. In the climax her job is pre-empted, she never jumps, and her big line is "Grandpa, no!" **Payoff: weak.** She's the character with the most untapped story, because this year's +1 is *her* family.
- **Dace.** Rival, then saboteur, then instant ally, then hero-flex. Fun, but the turn is unearned (four lines) and his plan job is never used. **Payoff: comic only.**
- **Fen.** Creepy gatekeeper turned secret saviour. The best reversal in the script, and it's delivered in dialogue by Ash about a man who isn't in the scene, then confirmed at the very end. **Payoff: the line is perfect; the character deserves more screen time.**
- **Pip.** Never seen. She's the living reason Ash fell, and the audience should meet her.

### The emotional core
The core is **"who does a family give up so the rest can have room?"**, carried by two brothers and a mother. Ash gave himself for Buh; Mom had to say yes; the Highkeeper finally gives himself rather than make his family choose. The draft has all the pieces and three great lines ("Mom picked," "I said take me," "Thirty-three"). **It lands intellectually and only half lands emotionally**, because the three scenes that should carry it are missing or compressed:
1. Buh and Ash alone, on what it means that Ash fell in his place. ("You were twelve." / "You should have let it be me.")
2. Buh and Mom, past "Go to bed": a real fight and a real reconciliation.
3. Mom and Ash, on screen, not in a time-lapse.

And the **song** (low, low, high, low) is the film's emotional signature and it disappears after Act 2A. There's a gorgeous inversion sitting unused: Ash said "Play it every night and I'll hear it from the top," and he heard it from the *bottom*. That should be said out loud by Ash, and the song should come back at the climax or the reunion, ideally played by Mom, who has refused to look for three years.

### Pacing
- **Rushed:** Tilly's ledger heist (four clues in one unguarded room); the descent to the Below (one climb, no obstacle); the Ash reunion (one quip); Ash's exposition dump (bell, piston, lake, why nobody returns, who picks, all in one scene, drawn in the dirt); the Mom confrontation; Dace's turn; the Highkeeper's decision; the entire aftermath (a time-lapse).
- **Drags:** nothing drags yet; this draft is too fast, not too slow. The one stretch that will *feel* slow on screen is scene 18 (people standing at a fire explaining mechanics). Show it instead: Ash takes Buh up inside the Needle and they see the piston, the redstone, the lake from the underside.
- **Clue density:** Act 2A delivers a clue every scene, which is good for YouTube retention but leaves no room for feeling. The 60-minute version should let each discovery cost something.

### Growing it to 60 minutes: what should become real scenes
**Underdeveloped beats that should grow into full scenes (most important first):**
1. **The climax fall, rebuilt around Buh's skill.** Scene 5 plants that Buh doesn't aim for the water, he aims for the stone next to it and places his own. Pay that off: the second ring *does* come (someone else pulls a backup lever, ideally a face with a motive; see below), the Well slides, and Buh clutches on bare stone in front of forty floors. Or Buh takes the empty bucket himself ("They don't let you choose yourself." / "Watch me."), choosing himself the way Ash did, and that public act is what breaks the Highkeeper. Either way, the hero has to win the climax. (3-4 min)
2. **Tilly's family picks.** This year's +1 is the Highkeeper's grandson. By his own rule, his family has to give one, and Tilly is the jumper this year. Make the torn-out page hold *Tilly's* name (or show that she volunteered, mirroring Ash), and make the note about Buh the Highkeeper's temptation to swap her for the boy who asks too many questions. Then the Highkeeper's jump is a grandfather taking his granddaughter's place, an exact mirror of Ash, and "Grandpa, no!" becomes the most emotional line in Act 3 instead of a reaction. This one change fixes the margin-note hole, gives Tilly stakes, and motivates the Highkeeper. (spread across chapters 2-5, 4-5 min total)
3. **The brothers' scene in the Below.** A night by the fire: the guilt, the anger, the song. "I said I'd hear it from the top. I heard it every night from the bottom." (2-3 min)
4. **Mom, in full.** The confrontation runs long enough to hurt; later, a quiet scene where she does something new (plays the four notes for the first time since Ash, or goes to the Highkeeper and says "take me" again). Then the reunion on the Needle, on screen, where she has to run down forty floors of stairs to reach him. (4-5 min)
5. **The Below as a real place with its own problem.** Thirty people, nights, mobs, a society. Make the old woman someone specific (ideally the very first "one," and Fen's mother or sister, which explains the lake and Fen's thirty-year silence). Give the camp a dissenting voice who doesn't want to go back up to the people who threw them away, so Ash's plan has to win an argument. (3-4 min)
6. **The Highkeeper alone.** One or two short scenes before Drop Day: counting the tally marks at night, the book, a moment with Tilly. Plant the guilt so his final choice reads as inevitable in hindsight. (2 min)
7. **Fen, on screen.** Buh catches Fen on the ladder, or Fen catches Buh. Fen's reasons for staying silent get spoken, and they have to be good ones. (2-3 min)
8. **An all-is-lost at the end of Act 2.** Something goes wrong before Drop Day: Fen locks the trapdoor (he's afraid the Highs will drain his lake if it's discovered), or Keepers catch Buh coming up from the fog, or Mom forbids the jump and tells Buh to jump from Floor 10, or Tilly learns it's her. (2-3 min)
9. **Earn "it was never about room."** In the ledger heist, show that Floor 40 has empty rooms: "There's always room on Forty" (Act 1) is literally true, just not for Lows. Now Buh's last line means something concrete: the tower was never full; it was unequal. (1 min inside existing scenes)
10. **Training and Dace.** A short escalation of Buh's practice jumps (6, 12, then a forbidden 25) with Dace as rival. Give Dace a real reason to switch sides (he finds out his Floor 40 future is paid for in bodies, or his own family was nearly a +1). (2-3 min)
11. **Pay off "forty-one" and Tilly's "Floor 1."** Tilly should jump from Floor 1 on Drop Day as a deliberate insult to the system; it's a great crowd beat. "Forty-one" can pay off in the final scene: the Below *is* the forty-first floor, the one under the first.

**New scenes or subplots that earn the length:** the Tilly-family-picks thread (the single most valuable addition); the Below's internal conflict (stay or return); the Mom thread as a full B-story; a face for the opposition in Act 3 once the Highkeeper turns (Tilly's father, a High who wants his newborn son's place guaranteed and pulls the backup lever), so the climax has an active antagonist after the villain becomes tragic.

**What would be padding (don't):** more practice-jump montages beyond one escalation; a biome tour of the Below with no story job; extra allay visits (two is right, three at most, with the third being the payoff); a long redstone explainer; more Drop Day montage than the current four jumps; any exploration of the other towers before the final scene; comedic side quests for Dace.

## Top 5 changes (most important first)

1. **Give Buh the climax.** Right now the hero's big fall has no danger (a full bucket, no second ring, a known lake), his steer-left is unmotivated, and the villain resolves the plot by his own choice, with a wire he cut off-screen. Make the second ring come (a backup lever pulled by a High with a motive), make Buh clutch on stone the way scene 5 promised, or make him *take the empty bucket himself*, choosing himself the way Ash did. The Highkeeper's jump should be caused by what Buh does in front of him, not decided before Buh arrives.

2. **Make this year's +1 the Highkeeper's family, on purpose, and make Tilly the one.** That pays off the torn page (her name), turns the note about Buh into the Highkeeper's temptation, gives Tilly real stakes and an active climax, and makes the Highkeeper's jump an exact mirror of Ash's. Right now the margin note is a dangling red herring and Tilly is a bystander at her own grandfather's fall.

3. **Put the emotional payoffs on screen.** A real Buh-Ash scene about Ash falling in his place. A Mom-Buh confrontation that runs past "Go to bed," and a reconciliation. The Mom-Ash reunion as a scene, not a time-lapse line. And bring the song back for the climax: Ash heard it "from the bottom" every night, and the four notes should be what Mom plays when she finally stops refusing to look.

4. **Fix the rules before Minecraft viewers do.** (a) Why the fallen can't mine or pillar up: make staying a choice with weight, and the barrier physical and believable. (b) Pistons can't move water source blocks: move the block under it. (c) Who can be the one, and how a non-jumper falls (the old woman, the kids), plus exactly one birth a year (a birth licence fixes it). (d) Fen "couldn't stop your bell": give a concrete reason, and make him answer for thirty years of grieving families. (e) State the bucket's role once (it's the backup clutch, and the one gets an empty one). (f) Fix the cold-open geography with one establishing wide.

5. **Add an all-is-lost and make the plan's promises count.** Act 2B currently has no reversal. Everyone says yes and the plan rolls into Drop Day. Add a real setback before Act 3 (Fen locks the trapdoor; Keepers catch Buh; Tilly learns it's her). Then pay off every job in the plan scene (Tilly's wire, Dace in the Well, Buh's trapdoor, Ash's thirty) by either working or failing in a way that forces improvisation. Also pay off or cut "Then I'll jump from forty-one," Tilly's Floor 1 jump, and "it was never about room" (show Floor 40's empty rooms early so the last line means something).
