## Part 1 (cold open + Act 1)

### 1. What I think the story is so far
A 40-floor tower above a fog. Once a year sixteen-year-olds step off a plank on the floor of their choosing and must land in a
single block of water (the Well) on top of a stone pillar (the Needle); if you live, your whole family moves to the floor you
jumped from. Buh's brother Ash jumped from 40 three years ago, the Well shifted one block during his fall, his bucket came up
empty, and he fell into the fog. Now Buh (15) is about to do the Drop himself, and an allay rises out of the fog and hands him a
lodestone compass that points straight down. He thinks it's from Ash.

### 2. Confusion log
- "forty floors of lanterns fall away, then the clouds, then the Needle" vs. Floor 3 being "a cramped stone room under the
  clouds" and the Highkeeper being "a High below the clouds" when he visits Floor 3. Where are the clouds? The Plank shot puts
  all forty floors above them; the rest of the act puts the Lows under them. I can't build a picture of the tower. LOST (for a
  minute), and it matters because "above/below the clouds" is the class line.
- The bucket. The Highkeeper "holds out a water bucket." Mid-fall Ash "swings his bucket. Nothing comes out. For eight frames
  the hotbar shows it: an empty bucket." Was it full when handed over and emptied somehow? Swapped? Or is this the Minecraft
  ghost-placement thing (client shows empty for a moment, server rejects it)? Eight frames is a third of a second. Nobody
  watching on a phone sees that. ANNOYED now, but it's the central clue of the murder, so it has to land. HOOKED if it gets
  shown again later.
- "DONG. The bell. DONG. Again." Then later: one DONG in the fog and Buh says "Nobody's jumping." So does the bell ring once
  per jump, and twice means something else? I'm guessing, but I'm not sure. HOOKED.
- "Upside down on the page, Buh can read his own name." He just told the Highkeeper his name, and the man wrote it down.
  Why is that a beat? Was it already written before he said it? Written in a different column? Crossed out? As written it's a
  nothing moment pretending to be a something moment. LOST.
- "Fen says the fog echoes." Who is Fen? First mention, no face. ANNOYED.
- "Grandpa says the Highkeeper's family jumps from 40." I *think* Tilly's grandpa IS the Highkeeper, but the line reads like
  her grandpa is reporting a rule about somebody else's family. One word ("My grandpa. The Highkeeper.") would fix it. ANNOYED.
- Ages: "He walks the line of sixteen-year-olds" and Buh is in it, but Buh is 15 (12 three years ago, and the cast list says
  15). Tilly is 15 and also plans to jump. Is the Drop for 15 or 16? ANNOYED.
- The lodestone compass spins. In vanilla, a spinning lodestone compass means the lodestone was broken or is in another
  dimension, not "it's right underneath you." Minecraft kids WILL read it the vanilla way. HOOKED (either reading is
  interesting), but the script needs to pick one.
- The Highkeeper "looks at him a moment too long" at "Four." Love it. HOOKED.

### 3. Predictions
- The Drop is a cull. There is a fixed amount of room in the tower (it's at build limit, it can't go up), and the
  Highkeeper rigs jumps (moves the Well on a bell signal, empties or swaps buckets) when a family is too big for the floor it
  wants. "There's always room on Forty" is the creepiest line in the act: Forty always has room because almost nobody who
  aims for it is allowed to live.
- Tilly's mom's baby on Drop Day means the Highkeeper's own family needs a slot, so someone else's kid gets bumped. Possibly
  Buh. Possibly Tilly.
- Ash is alive below the fog. There's a world down there (the bell in the fog with no jumper, the allay, the compass), and
  "the Below keeps what it takes" means the people who "die" are kept, not killed.
- "Then I'll jump from forty-one" is a promise: Buh will find a way to go above build limit, or the ending reframes what
  "up" means (the real world is down).

### 4. Interest
8/10. Yes. The cold open is a real hook: a rigged-looking death, a brother, a song, a creepy old man. The rules are a little
mushy, but I want the answer.

### Holes noticed so far (feeding the persona section)
- If every jumper gets a water bucket, the Well is optional; you can MLG onto the Needle's top. So the Drop is only deadly if
  you miss the pillar entirely, or if the bucket fails. Is that the rule? It's never stated.
- Ash is aiming at the Well, the Well moves one block, his crosshair is "on stone", and he "clips the edge of the Needle and
  tumbles past it into white." If he was falling onto stone he lands on stone and dies there, on the pillar, in view. In
  Minecraft you don't "clip" an edge and tumble; you land or you don't. The script needs him to vanish, so it fudges physics.
- Water source blocks can't be pushed by pistons. How does the Well "slide one block left"?
- In Minecraft an empty bucket and a water bucket have different icons. Ash would have seen an empty bucket the moment he
  took it.
- Family rule: "The floor I jump from is the floor I live on," and the whole family moves. So what happens to a family with
  two kids who jump to two different floors? Does the family move every time a kid turns 16? What happens when Pip jumps from
  Floor 1 in 13 years: does Buh, on 40, move down? What about adults who marry across floors?
- If there are stairs between floors (the Highkeeper walks them, Tilly drops down them), what physically stops Lows from
  walking up? Two iron golems for 40 floors?
- If Forty "always has room" and everyone who lands from 40 moves there with their family, Forty should be overflowing after
  a few generations. Where does room come from?

## Part 2 (Act 2A)

### 1. What I think the story is so far
The tower stopped growing thirty years ago when it hit build limit, and since then exactly one jumper a year "falls": the
Highkeeper keeps a ledger where each year one family gets a +1 (a birth) and one name gets crossed out, and he rigs that
person's Drop with an empty bucket while Fen, the old man who lives on the Needle, shifts the Well on a double bell. Ash was
crossed out the year Pip was born. This year's page is torn out and the Highkeeper has written that Buh "asks too many
questions." Buh climbs down a ladder hidden inside the Needle, through the fog, and finds a whole green world with a lake,
a village, and Ash, alive.

### 2. Confusion log
- "a few stone steps lead off the tower to the Needle, one block wide, standing alone in the fog. On top: the Well. Next to
  it, on a stool, FEN." If the Needle is one block wide, the top IS the Well; there's no room for a stool, a hammock or a
  trapdoor, and you can't fit a ladder shaft inside a 1x1 pillar. If "one block wide" means the steps, say so. Part 1 also
  had stone around the Well ("The crosshair is on stone"). I don't know how big the target is, which is the whole sport.
  LOST.
- The Needle is reachable by walking down the stairs. So the Well sits roughly at Floor 1 height, right next to the tower?
  Then a Floor 1 jump is a hop, and the Plank shot ("forty floors of lanterns fall away, then the clouds, then the Needle")
  makes it sound miles away. I've now lost the geography twice. LOST.
- "One a year. Thirty years. Never two. Never none." Great line. But then the Drop has never killed anyone by accident in
  30 years? So the jump is safe unless rigged? Then why is anyone scared of Forty? HOOKED, but I'm doing math now.
- Ledger: "Every year, one family has a +1." One birth a year in the whole tower? Then where do the seventeen
  sixteen-year-olds (seventeen buckets in the chest) come from? The numbers don't work. LOST.
- Tilly reads "every year a family gets bigger, someone falls" while her own mom is giving birth on Drop Day, and doesn't
  connect it to herself. If that's coming later as a reveal, fine. If not, she's dumb for a scene. HOOKED (I assume it's a
  setup: her family is this year's +1, so by the ledger's rule SHE should be the one, and Grandpa has picked Buh instead).
- "The page is torn out. On a scrap in the margin..." A scrap of a torn-out page, still in the book, with the incriminating
  sentence on it. Convenient. ANNOYED.
- Dace swapping Buh's bucket at practice. "Scared people jump from Floor 10! Not dead!" You emptied a kid's bucket while he
  was doing an MLG off Floor 12. That's attempted murder, and then the scene moves on like it was a prank. And why does Dace
  care where Buh jumps? Competition for room on Forty? Friendship? I can't tell. ANNOYED.
- "DON'T TAKE HIS 8UCKET." I get the 8 (it's Ash). "His" = the Highkeeper's. Took me a second. HOOKED. But why didn't Ash
  just rename the tag "IT'S ASH. I'M ALIVE. LADDER IN THE NEEDLE"? Two cryptic deliveries when one plain one would do.
  ANNOYED.
- The fog is ten seconds of ladder thick (about 25 blocks). There is a village with campfires about 25 blocks under the
  kids' practice pool. Nobody has heard a cow? HOOKED-turning-to-ANNOYED.
- "Took you long enough, Buh." Long enough? Buh got your first message five days ago. You've been alive three years.
  HOOKED (I need the answer), but the line plays as smug.

### 3. Predictions
- Everyone who "fell" in thirty years lives down here: about thirty people. The lake under the Needle catches them (water
  cancels fall damage), so "the Below keeps what it takes" is literally true: it keeps them, it doesn't kill them.
- Fen is the go-between. His sack goes down and comes up: the tower probably gets wood, iron, food from the Below, which is
  how a floating stone tower has lanterns, buckets and hay.
- This year's +1 is Tilly's family, so the rule says Tilly. The Highkeeper is going to break his own rule and send Buh
  instead, to save his granddaughter and shut up the kid asking questions. Tilly finds out and has to choose.
- The reason the tower exists at all is probably the night: mobs on the ground. Ash's scar is from something down here. The
  Below is not pure paradise.
- Big reveal still owed: why Ash never climbed back up a ladder that's been sitting right there for three years.

### 4. Interest
8/10. Yes. The Below reveal at dawn is a strong image and the ledger is a good, chilling device. But the world's arithmetic
is starting to wobble, and the "why didn't Ash come home" question is now the loudest thing in my head.

### Holes noticed so far (feeding the persona section)
- The tower was built thirty years ago by Fen's dad and his generation. Built from what? Standing on what? If they built it up
  from the ground, the whole adult population over ~45 remembers the ground. The Highkeeper is in his 70s. How does a society
  believe "there's nothing down there" when its grandparents walked on it?
- "Build limit, couldn't go higher" so room ran out. Build limit only caps height. You can build sideways forever. "Just build
  out" will be the top comment.
- The Below is "a whole green world." Infinite land under a fog that's 25 blocks thick. The room problem the whole society is
  built on is fake. Fine if the Highkeeper knows and lies, but then why the lie?
- Where do the tower's materials come from? Iron (buckets, golems, lanterns), wood (planks, note blocks, ledger), food. If
  it's all through Fen's sack, the Below is supplying the tower and somebody up there knows.
- Exactly one death a year for thirty years "never two, never none" requires that NO jumper ever misses by accident. And if
  the lake catches everyone who misses, then accidental misses would also show up Below. Either the Drop is trivially safe
  (then why not everyone from 40?) or the count can't be exactly one.
- One +1 per year vs. 17 jumpers per year: the birth rate can't be one a year if seventeen kids reach 16 every year.
- If the victim always comes from the +1 family, what happens the year the +1 family has no sixteen-year-old?
- The Needle trapdoor: the most sacred spot in the world, visible from every Drop Face window, guarded by one old man who
  sleeps in a hammock next to it. In 30 years no kid has opened it?
- Second "empty bucket in the hotbar mid-fall" scene. Water bucket and empty bucket look different. Buh, the MLG kid,
  doesn't check his hotbar before stepping off? Ash didn't either? Same mistake, two smart brothers.
- Allays: an allay only reacts to a note block within about 16 blocks, and only drops items matching what it's holding. It's
  rising 25+ blocks through the fog. Minor, but the allay-as-mailman is the movie's best idea, so it should follow its rules.
- Why can't the people Below just pillar up 25 blocks? They're Minecraft people.

## Part 3 (Act 2B)

### 1. What I think the story is so far
Every "one" from the last thirty years is alive in the Below: Fen secretly dug a lake under the Needle the first year and
they all landed in it. The cull is a redstone trick: the bell on 40 is wired to a sticky piston under the Well, two rings
and it shifts a block, and the one gets the Highkeeper's single empty bucket so they can't clutch. The Highkeeper doesn't
pick the one; he makes the +1 family pick, and Mom picked Ash (at Ash's insistence) over twelve-year-old Buh. Plan for Drop
Day: Tilly cuts the bell wire on 40, Dace jumps just before Buh and guards the Well, Buh jumps last and opens the trapdoor so
thirty "dead" people climb out of the fog in front of the whole city.

### 2. Confusion log
- "Twenty-nine people around the fires. Kids, grown-ups, an old woman with white hair." / "Everybody here is a 'one'." The
  first one fell thirty years ago. If the ones are sixteen-year-old jumpers, the oldest is about 46 and nobody is a kid. So
  either the kids were born down here (then not everybody is a one, and the Below is growing) or the one can be any age
  (then grandmas and children have done the Drop in public and the whole city knows who the one is). LOST. This also
  breaks Ash's count: is it 29 plus Ash = 30? Probably, but I had to stop and add.
- "She picked you. Over me." / "You were twelve." Buh was twelve and not jumping that year. How could he have been the
  one? Does the one have to be a jumper or not? If not, how does a twelve-year-old end up on the Plank on Drop Day without
  the city noticing? LOST. This is the rule the emotional core of the movie sits on, and I don't understand it.
- "They don't let you choose yourself." Why? Who enforces "the family picks" at all? What happens if Mom refuses to pick?
  There's no stated threat. Without one, the most monstrous rule in the movie has no teeth. LOST.
- "And the trapdoor only opens from the top." Then how did Buh get back up two scenes later? He walked in "soaked in fog."
  Either he left it open (then Fen notices, and anyone Below could have come up with him that night), or someone opened it
  for him. LOST, and it's a real plot hole, not a nitpick.
- "Up to what? The tower's full. Go back and they send you down again." Thirty people have had up to thirty years and a
  ladder that goes to the tower. They don't need room up there. They need to say one sentence: "We're alive and there's a
  whole world down here." That ends the cull. Why has no one in thirty years tried? ANNOYED-heading-to-LEAVE for a
  Minecraft-literate audience.
- "Fen dug that lake the first year." So Fen is secretly saving everyone. Does the Highkeeper know? Ash says "they send you
  down again," which sounds like the Highkeeper knows the ones live. If he knows, he's an exiler, not a murderer, and
  everything about the cold open's menace changes. If he doesn't, Fen is the bravest man in the movie and has had thirty
  years to tell one mother. LOST.
- Why do they need Buh to open the trapdoor? Fen opens it every day. Fen is on their side (he dug the lake). Ask Fen.
  ANNOYED.
- "The bell is wired all the way down... It ends at a sticky piston under the Well." A piston under the Well pushes it up,
  not one block left. And pistons can't move water source blocks at all. The Minecraft kids in the comments will have this
  posted within the hour. ANNOYED.
- Wiring the sabotage through an audible bell that everyone in the city hears ring twice for the one, every year, is the
  worst villain engineering I've seen. Also: what was the single DONG "far off in the fog" in Part 1, when no one was
  jumping? The bell is on 40 according to this. HOOKED if it's paid off, CONFUSES if it isn't.
- Flashback replay of Ash's fall where "we watch the Well slide." Good. That's the fix for the eight frames. HOOKED.
- Mom: "He's alive." / "Don't." Buh is holding a name tag with Ash's 8 on it and doesn't show her. ANNOYED.
- Dace: "If the Well does anything weird, I'll be standing in it." Standing in it does what? A player in a water block
  doesn't stop a piston. If he means he'll place his own water where the Well was, say so. Also, two days ago Dace emptied
  Buh's bucket mid-jump; now he's on the team because "might as well be a hero." Fast. ANNOYED.
- Still nobody has said out loud that Tilly's family is this year's +1. HOOKED (I'm sure it's coming).

### 4. Interest
7/10. Yes, but with growing irritation. "Mom picked" is the best beat so far and it hit hard. But the Below's existence
makes the tower's rules look stupid, and the movie hasn't yet given me a reason anyone stays up there or why nobody ever

### Holes noticed so far (feeding the persona section)
- Survival-mode logic: Buh places ladders and water; Fen digs a lake; the ones build wooden houses. So everyone can place

- The "+1 family picks" rule needs the +1 family to contain someone eligible. Undefined who is eligible.
- Who enforces any of this? Two iron golems and an old man with a book versus a whole city.
- Families who picked (30 of them) all know the Drop is rigged. 30 families keeping a secret for 30 years works only if
  guilt keeps them quiet. The script almost says this with Mom; it should say it.
- Buh's plan only needs the trapdoor opened in public. Why does Buh need to jump at all, and why last? (Answer I can infer:
  so he lands on the Needle next to the trapdoor. Should be one line.)

## Final report

### The whole story, as I'd tell a friend
There's this city in a huge tower that hit build limit thirty years ago. Every year the sixteen-year-olds jump off whatever
floor they want into one block of water on a pillar, and if they make it their family gets to live on that floor. Exactly
one kid "falls" every year. Turns out it's rigged: the old Highkeeper keeps the population fixed. Whenever a family has a
baby, that family has to pick someone to go, and he rigs their jump with an empty bucket and a piston that moves the water
on a double bell. Buh's mom picked his older brother Ash three years ago. But the guy who lives on the pillar, Fen, secretly
dug a lake under the fog, and every "dead" kid is alive down there in a whole green world. Buh climbs down, finds Ash, and
they plan to bring all thirty back up in front of everyone on Drop Day. On the day, the Highkeeper's own grandson is born,
so he can't make his family pick. He spares Buh and jumps himself. He lands in Fen's lake too. Everyone moves down to the
ground. Last shot: there are other towers out there, and you can hear their bells ringing twice.

### Unanswered questions

**GOOD AMBIGUITY (keep, people will argue):**
- Who cut the second lever's line: the Highkeeper, Fen, Mom, Dace? (Keep it open, but give Tilly something else to do; see
  changes.)
- Was the Highkeeper going to hand Buh the empty bucket before Buh said "Thirty-three"? His hand hangs over it. Great.
- Does the Highkeeper deserve to be "Grandpa says we build out now" after thirty years of what he believed were killings?
  Does Mom? Is Fen a hero or a thirty-year accomplice who let mothers grieve?
- The other towers. Who built them, do they have lakes, are their ones dead? Perfect sequel bait.
- "He laughs, or he cries. Both."

**CONFUSING (it reads like a mistake):**
- Why the thirty ones never came back or sent word, given a ladder, a trapdoor Buh himself climbs back through, and a game
  where you can pillar 25 blocks in a minute.
- Ash says "they send you down again," but the Highkeeper turns out not to know anyone survived.
- Who can be the one: only that year's jumpers, or anyone in the family? (Buh "at twelve", "kids" and "an old woman with
  white hair" Below, Mom "take me", the Highkeeper taking himself.)
- The +1 arithmetic: one +1 a year vs. seventeen jumpers a year.
- How nobody in the tower knows there's ground 25 blocks under the fog when the tower was finished within living memory.
- Tower geography: clouds above or below Floor 3, the Needle "one block wide" with a stool, hammock and ladder shaft, the
  lake "right under the Needle" but Buh steps onto grass, and what holds the tower up.
- The bucket "held out" full in the cold open but the one gets the empty one.
- Who pulls the bell lever while the Highkeeper is on the Plank and the lever room is empty.
- The far towers at build limit being invisible from our Floor 40.

### Plot holes and contradictions (summary; full list with fixes in the persona section)
1. Why the ones never came back. BREAKS THE STORY.
2. Nobody knows about the ground; the tower is too young for that. BREAKS THE STORY.
3. If no one ever misses by accident, nobody would choose below 40, and "always room on Forty" contradicts "the tower is
   full." BREAKS THE STORY (it's the premise of the floors).
4. Eligibility for "the one" and the +1 arithmetic. CONFUSES, heavily.
5. Tilly (15) and Buh (15) were born after the cull began, so their births were +1s too. Who fell for them? CONFUSES.
6. Climax jump has no stakes. CONFUSES (and kills tension).
7. Fen "couldn't" stop a bell whose piston he sleeps on. CONFUSES.
8. Lever logistics and redstone that isn't vanilla. CONFUSES for the target audience.
9. Ending towers visible from Floor 40. CONFUSES.
10. Smaller: ages, Pip's birthday, the name tag ignored, Dace's dropped plan, the margin scrap, "Fen" before Fen.

### Best 3 moments
1. "Mom picked." / "She picked you. Over me." / "I made her." then Mom: "I said me. I said take me. They don't let you
   choose yourself." The best twenty lines in the script. This is the movie.
2. "How many in your family?" / "Thirty-three." The question the Highkeeper has asked all movie, turned on him in front of
   the city. Perfect callback.
3. Mom ripping the curtain open: "Ash." Pays off "Don't look" from the cold open with one word.
   (Runner-up: "Thirty years, Orlo. I couldn't stop your bell. So I dug a lake.")

### Worst 3 moments
1. Scene 18: "Up to what? The tower's full. Go back and they send you down again. And the trapdoor only opens from the top."
   This is the scene that has to close the biggest hole in the movie, and it opens three more: the ending contradicts it,
   Buh contradicts it by going home that night, and Minecraft contradicts it.
2. Scene 26: Buh's fall. Steer left, steer back, splash, while holding a full bucket. The climax jump is the only jump in the
   movie with nothing riding on it.
3. Scene 23: "Somebody beat me to it." The co-lead's plan is done for her offscreen. Then Dace's plan is forgotten too. The
   whole team-up from scene 21 evaporates and the climax becomes the Highkeeper's.

## Persona section: the skeptic's hole list

Severity key: BREAKS THE STORY / CONFUSES / NITPICK.

**1. The thirty never came back, never sent word, in up to thirty years.**
Scene 18 (Ash: "Up to what?... Go back and they send you down again. And the trapdoor only opens from the top."), contradicted
by scene 20 (Buh walks home the same night, so the trapdoor opened for him) and scene 29 (the Highkeeper didn't know they
lived, so nobody would have "sent them down again"; their return would have ended him). Plus it's Minecraft: they build
houses, Fen dug a lake, Buh slaps ladders on walls. You can mine a trapdoor or pillar 25 blocks next to the Needle.
The top comment will be "why didn't they just pillar up."
BREAKS THE STORY.
Smallest fix: change "can't" to "won't," and make the reason true. Ash: "Fen made us swear. The lake only works while Orlo
thinks we're dead. One of us shows his face up there and next year he doesn't use the Well. He uses a sword." One line,
and it also explains Fen's silence (hole 7) and why the plan needs the whole city watching. Then show how Buh gets back up:
Fen is awake at the top of the ladder, opens the trapdoor for him, and says nothing. That one look turns Fen from
a convenience into a character.

**2. Nobody knows there's ground 25 blocks below a fog, though the tower was finished in living memory.**
Scene 10: "My dad laid the last block up top. Thirty years ago." The Highkeeper is in his 70s. So his generation built the
thing; somebody stood on the ground. Yet the city's belief is "There's nothing down there" (scene 8) and "More fog" (Fen).
Also, the tower presumably stands on something, and the Below reveal (scene 17) never shows what's holding it up.
BREAKS THE STORY.
Smallest fix: make the tower old. "My dad laid the last block. His great-great-grandad laid the first." Two hundred years of
building means nobody alive has seen the ground and the fog is legend. Add one shot in scene 17 of the tower's underside
(a floating base the Needle props up, or legs lost in the fog), so the image answers "what holds it up."

**3. The Drop has no natural risk, so the floor system makes no sense.**
Scene 10: "One a year. Thirty years. Never two. Never none." Scene 22: every jumper lands, including a girl screaming the
whole way and a shaking Dace from 40. So in thirty years no one has missed by accident. Then why would anyone ever choose
Floor 5? And "There's always room on Forty" (scene 6) contradicts "The tower is full" (scene 27).
BREAKS THE STORY (it's the premise of the floors).
Smallest fix: the one always jumps from 40. The Highkeeper tells the picked family "your one goes from the top." So the city
learns "the Well takes from Forty," which is why mothers say "Floor 10 has windows," why Forty "always has room" (it's the
ones' families who never moved up), and why Dace and Buh choosing 40 is brave. One line in the ledger scene: "Every one of
them jumped from 40." It makes "always room on Forty" retroactively chilling, and gives "it was never about room" something
to point at (see hole 15).

**4. Who can be the one? The rule the emotional core sits on is undefined.**
Scene 18: "She picked you. Over me." / "You were twelve." Buh wasn't jumping. Scene 18 also: "Kids, grown-ups, an old woman
with white hair. ... Everybody here is a 'one'." A sixteen-year-old who fell thirty years ago is 46 now. Scene 20: Mom:
"I said take me." Scene 27: the Highkeeper takes himself. If the one can be anyone, then grandmas and twelve-year-olds have
stood on the Plank in front of the city and everyone would know who the one is. If it has to be a jumper, Mom's choice
between her sons wasn't a choice.
CONFUSES (heavily; it's the "Mom picked" scene).
Smallest fix: early jumping is allowed (which also fixes Buh and Tilly being 15 in "the line of sixteen-year-olds"). Ash:
"You'd have jumped at twelve. I told her I'm the better clutcher." For the Below: make the old woman "gray at forty-six,
the first one," and either cut the kids or have Ash say "Everybody here is a one, or a one's kid," and count accordingly
in scene 28.

**5. The +1 arithmetic.**
Scene 14: "Every year, one family has a +1. And one name is crossed out." The chest holds seventeen buckets, so seventeen
kids turn sixteen a year, so roughly seventeen babies are born a year. One +1 a year can't be births. Also: old people die;
doesn't that free room?
CONFUSES. Comment-section math guaranteed.
Smallest fix: the ledger is a running count, not births. Tilly: "Births minus burials. Every year it comes out one over. And
whoever's baby tips it over picks." Same drama, no math hole.

**6. Ash, Buh, Tilly were all born after the cull began. Who fell for them?**
Tilly is 15; the cull is 30 years old. Under the scene 14 rule, her birth was a +1 for the Highkeeper's own house. So "Not
this year" (scene 27) can't be the first time his family faced it. Also: no father is ever mentioned for Tilly, or for Buh,
Ash and Pip.
CONFUSES (and wastes a gift).
Smallest fix (with hole 5's "tips it over" rule this mostly goes away), or use it: Tilly's dad is one of the thirty. He
climbs out of the trapdoor in scene 28. Biggest free emotional beat in the movie, and it explains why Grandpa has been
"weird all week."

**7. Fen "couldn't stop your bell."**
Scene 29. The piston is "under the Well" (scene 18), and Fen lives next to the Well and sleeps there in a hammock. He could
break it by hand any night. He could also tell one grieving mother. He let thirty families mourn for thirty years.
CONFUSES.
Smallest fix: covered by hole 1's line, or give Fen his own: "Break the piston and he pushes them himself. Tell one mother
and he finds my lake." Then "So I dug a lake" plays as the only move he had.

**8. Does the Highkeeper know the ones live?**
Scene 18 implies yes ("they send you down again"); scene 29 says no. The movie answers it, but Ash's line is wrong in
hindsight and viewers will catch it on rewatch.
CONFUSES.
Smallest fix: hole 1's rewrite.

**9. The bucket in the cold open.**
Scene 2: "The HIGHKEEPER (70s) holds out a water bucket." Scene 19/14: the one gets the single empty bucket. Scene 3: "For
eight frames the hotbar shows it: an empty bucket." Water and empty buckets have different icons; Ash would see it the moment
he took it, and eight frames is invisible on a phone. Then in scene 11 Buh, the MLG prodigy, makes the same mistake (he
doesn't check his hotbar before stepping off Floor 12). Two smart brothers, same blind spot.
CONFUSES.
Smallest fix: Ash takes the bucket into his offhand without looking, eyes on the drop, grinning. The camera lingers on the
empty bucket in his offhand for a full second before he steps. The audience knows; he doesn't. For scene 11, Dace swaps it
in a chest/hand-off the audience sees, so it's an ambush, not carelessness.

**10. The climax jump has no stakes.**
Scene 25-26. Buh holds a full bucket. Missing the Well onto stone is his signature move (scene 5). Missing the Needle drops
him into a lake he swam in two days ago.
CONFUSES (and deflates the climax).
Smallest fix: Buh obeys the name tag. "DON'T TAKE HIS 8UCKET." He refuses both buckets: "Keep them." He jumps with nothing,
so the second ring really is life or death, and steering left on a guess is a real gamble. The Highkeeper is left on the
Plank holding a full and an empty bucket, which is a better image for his final speech anyway.

**11. Who pulls the levers?**
Scene 22: the Highkeeper is on the Plank beside the bucket chest; the bell rings for every jumper. Scene 23: Tilly walks into
the lever room and nobody's in it. So who's ringing the bell, and who would have pulled lever two during the one's fall?
CONFUSES.
Smallest fix: the first ring is automatic (a tripwire at the end of the Plank). The second lever is on the Plank post under
the Highkeeper's hand; the lever room is just where the line goes down the shaft, which is why Tilly goes there to cut it.

**12. The redstone isn't vanilla, and the audience is Minecraft kids.**
Scene 18: "a sticky piston under the Well" that moves it "one block left." A piston under a block pushes it up, not
sideways. Pistons can't move water source blocks at all. A bell can be rung by redstone but doesn't output a signal, so
"two rings, and the Well moves" can't be the trigger. Scene 23: two levers, one to the bell, which contradicts Ash's
counter version.
CONFUSES (for this audience, it reads as the writer not knowing the game).
Smallest fix: two dispensers inside the Needle top, one holding an empty bucket (scoops the Well up), one holding a water
bucket (pours it one block left). One lever pulse fires both and rings the bell. Ash: "The second ring covers the
click." That also answers why the sabotage is wired through a loud bell at all.

**13. Tilly loses her climax; Dace's plan evaporates.**
Scene 21 sets up three jobs. Scene 23: Tilly's is already done ("Somebody beat me to it"). Scene 24: Dace's ("I'll be
standing in it") is never used. Scene 12: Dace emptied Buh's bucket mid-jump and calls it a scare; nine scenes later he's a
hero with no beat in between.
CONFUSES (structure).
Smallest fix: Tilly finds the line already cut AND a second, hidden line (or the Highkeeper's backup lever on the Plank), so
she has to run to the Plank. That puts her there physically for "Grandpa, no!" and gives her a real choice: stop him or
let him. Dace: either cut "I'll be standing in it," or have him actually stay in the Well and haul Buh out, so his "might as
well be a hero" pays off.

**14. Tower and Needle geography.**
Scene 2: "forty floors of lanterns fall away, then the clouds, then the Needle" (all forty floors above the clouds) vs.
scene 1/6: Floor 3 is "under the clouds" and the Highkeeper is "a High below the clouds." Scene 10: the Needle is "one block
wide" yet holds a stool, a hammock, a trapdoor, a ladder shaft and stone around the Well (scene 3: "The crosshair is on
stone"). It's reachable by stairs from Floor 1, so it sits at ground-floor level, but scene 2 frames it as impossibly far.
Scene 17: the lake is "right under the Needle," but Buh "steps off the ladder onto grass."
CONFUSES. The size of the target is the whole sport, and I couldn't picture it.
Smallest fix: one establishing wide shot in scene 2 that shows the clouds cutting the tower at about Floor 20, the Needle as
a 3x3 stone pillar beside the base with the one-block Well in the middle, and the fog under all of it. Cut "one block wide"
or move it to the steps. In scene 17, the Needle stands on the lakeshore with the lake spreading out from its base.

**15. "Forty floors, and it was never about room."**
Scene 31. The Highkeeper believed the tower was full; he jumped to make room. Nobody in the movie argued otherwise.
CONFUSES (the thesis isn't earned).
Smallest fix: with hole 3's fix, there was always room on Forty, and Buh can say exactly that: "There was always room on
Forty." Or: "Forty floors up, and the room was under us the whole time."

**16. "Build out, not up" was always possible.**
Scene 31: "Grandpa says we build out now." Build limit caps height, not width. Viewers will ask why they didn't build
sideways thirty years ago.
CONFUSES.
Smallest fix: one early line that the tower has no new blocks. Mom, sorting mushrooms: "Nothing new comes up here. Every
block we've got, we've had since Grandma's grandma." Then building out needs the ground, and "we build out now" is a payoff,
not an admission.

**17. The far towers at build limit would be visible from our Floor 40.**
Scene 31: "another tower rises all the way to build limit." The Highs live above the clouds in "sun and gold."
CONFUSES.
Smallest fix: "another tower, its top lost in its own cloud bank." Or a Floor 40 line early on ("Grandpa says those are
mountains"). Keep the double ring; it's the right ending.

**18. Why only now? The allay mail.**
Scene 1: "Play it every night and I'll hear it from the top." Presumably Buh has played the song every night for three years.
The allay shows up five days before the Drop. Also: an allay hears note blocks only within about 16 blocks, and it only
delivers items matching what it holds. And why is message two a cryptic name tag instead of "I'M ALIVE"?
CONFUSES (the "why now" part) / NITPICK (mechanics).
Smallest fix: Ash, scene 18: "Three years to find an allay." (Allays are rare in vanilla, and Minecraft fans will love the
line.) For the cryptic tag: Ash: "Fen reads everything that goes up that ladder."

**19. Ages.**
Scene 6: "He walks the line of sixteen-year-olds" and Buh (15) is in it. Tilly (15) says she's jumping, then never does.
NITPICK (fixed by hole 4's "early jumping" or by making them 16).

**20. "Upside down on the page, Buh can read his own name."**
Scene 6. He just said it; the man wrote it. Non-beat.
NITPICK.
Smallest fix: his name is already on the page before he speaks, with a mark next to it. Or cut it.

**21. The margin scrap.**
Scene 14: the page is torn out, but a scrap in the margin with the one damning sentence survives.
NITPICK.
Smallest fix: Tilly finds the torn page balled in the fireplace or the chest, half-burned. Or cut "The Buh boy asks too
many questions." The empty seventeenth bucket already does the work.

**22. Pip's birthday.**
Scene 30: "Weeks later... Pip's birthday." She turned three a week before the Drop.
NITPICK.
Smallest fix: "Pip's first day under a real sky."

**23. Mom doesn't get proof.**
Scene 20: Buh is holding a name tag with Ash's 8 and doesn't show it.
NITPICK.
Smallest fix: he holds it out; she won't look at it. Stronger anyway.

**24. Who enforces anything?**
Two iron golems and an old man with a book vs. forty floors of people. What stops Lows walking up the stairs to empty rooms
on Forty? What happens if a family refuses to pick?
CONFUSES (only the second part matters).
Smallest fix: Mom, scene 20: "If you don't pick, he picks. And he picks the baby." One line and the whole system has teeth.

**25. Fen's sack, and where the tower gets food, iron and wood.**
Scene 13: Fen climbs into the Needle with a sack. Never explained.
NITPICK. Inferable (supplies for the ones). Can stay unanswered.

**26. Thirty families who picked kept the secret for thirty years.**
That works only because they're guilty. Mom almost says it.
NITPICK as a hole, but worth one line because it's thematically the strongest answer in the movie to "why did nobody
talk": Mom: "Every mother who picked keeps her mouth shut. That's how he knew we would."

**Keep deliberately unanswered (people will argue, and that's good):**
- Who cut the second lever's line.
- Whether the Highkeeper would have handed Buh the empty bucket without "Thirty-three."
- Whether the Highkeeper, Fen and Mom deserve forgiveness.
- The other towers: who built them, whether their ones live.
- Why the founders climbed in the first place (as long as hole 2's "it's ancient" fix exists, the origin can be a sequel).
- Where Buh's dad is (unless you use Tilly's dad per hole 6, in which case leave Buh's open; one absent father is a
  mystery, two is a pattern people will theorize about).

**Must be answered on screen:**
- Why the ones stayed hidden (hole 1), and how Buh got back up.
- Who can be the one, and what happens if a family won't pick (holes 4, 24).
- The count, as a net number (hole 5).
- Why anyone fears Forty if nobody ever misses (hole 3).
- Why nobody knows about the ground (hole 2).
- Tower/Needle geography in one clear wide shot (hole 14).
- The bucket at the cold-open hand-off, held long enough to read (hole 9).
- Who pulls the lever (hole 11).

## Top 5 changes, most important first

1. **Close the "why didn't they come back" hole with one true line, and show Buh's return trip.** Ash: "Fen made us swear.
   The lake only works while Orlo thinks we're dead." Fen at the top of the ladder lets Buh out and says nothing. Add "Three
   years to find an allay." Without this, the comment section is one long "just pillar up," and the ending makes Ash's
   current excuse false.
2. **Rewrite the rules in the ledger scene so they hold.** Net count ("births minus burials, one over every year"); the
   one always jumps from 40 ("always room on Forty" becomes the creepiest line in the film, and the floor system makes
   sense); early jumping allowed (so "You were twelve" is a real choice and Buh/Tilly's ages work); "if you don't pick, he
   picks the baby." Clean up the kids and the white-haired woman Below.
3. **Put stakes and the team back into the climax.** Buh obeys "DON'T TAKE HIS 8UCKET" and jumps with no bucket, so the
   second ring is life or death. Tilly finds a backup line or lever and has to reach the Plank, so she's there for her
   grandfather's choice by her own action, not by accident. Either pay off Dace's "I'll be standing in it" or cut it. Use
   Tilly's dad as one of the thirty.
4. **Make the world's history and physical layout coherent.** Make the tower centuries old so nobody alive has seen the
   ground. Add one line that no new blocks ever come up (so "build out" was impossible until now, and the last scene is a
   payoff). Add one wide shot fixing clouds/Needle/target size, and a shot in the Below reveal showing what holds the tower
   up. Fix the far towers so they aren't visible from our Floor 40.
5. **Make the mechanism vanilla and the clue readable.** Two dispensers (scoop, pour) instead of a piston under water; the
   second ring "covers the click"; the second lever under the Highkeeper's hand on the Plank. In the cold open, hold the
   empty bucket in Ash's offhand for a full second instead of eight frames. This audience knows redstone better than the
   script does, and right now they'll be able to tell.
