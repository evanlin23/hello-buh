## Chapter 1: You Fall Once

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
In a giant tower city (Highfall), every kid makes one jump in life ("you fall once"), using a water bucket to land in a pool far below, and the floor you jump from becomes the floor you live on. Three years ago Buh's big brother Ash jumped from Forty, the top, and his bucket turned out to be empty; he fell past the Needle into the fog and everyone, including the official census, counts him as dead. Now Buh, about 15, is six days from his own jump and is set on Forty, while his mom wants the safe Floor 10. Each night he plays Ash's four-note song, and an allay out of the fog has started bringing him things: a compass that points at the Needle and a name tag in Ash's handwriting warning him not to take "his" bucket.

**2. Confusion log.**
- "The Well blinks one block over" (scene 3). I don't know what "blinks" means here. Did the landing pool *move*? It sounds important and I can't parse it. Combined with Rook's "Practice pools don't move," I'm guessing the real Well moves. HOOKED, but close to LOST: if this is the central trick, the image has to be readable on first watch.
- "DONG. DONG." during the fall, then later "a single bell rings, though nobody is jumping." I infer a bell rings when someone jumps. I picked that up only from the second one. ANNOYED; it's fine if the shot shows the bell.
- Rook in the market: "All three of us are moving to Forty." Three of who? Rook's family? Rook, Buh and Tilly? (Tilly hasn't been introduced yet.) Rook's own role is fuzzy: friend, rival, bully? LOST for a beat.
- "The laws, chanted once." The treatment doesn't say what the laws are. As a viewer I'd hear them, so I can't judge whether they teach the rules. As an editor: this is the one scene that can carry the world rules cleanly, and it's blank. ANNOYED (treatment gap).
- "Her mom has the Cradle this year, due on Drop Day." No idea what the Cradle is. A pregnancy? A job? A thing you carry? LOST, but small.
- The kid's block past the Edge getting smashed. I get that building out from the tower is forbidden. I don't know why. HOOKED.
- Buh's age now: 12 + 3 = 15. Ash jumped at 16. Is the jump at 15 or 16? Minor. ANNOYED.
- The Highkeeper hands Ash the bucket in the cold open and smiles "There's always room on Forty" later. It reads as an obvious villain tell. Not confusion, but I already "know" who did it, which may or may not be intended.
- "Don't take his... bucket." Whose? Probably the Highkeeper's. The ellipsis makes it a puzzle; fine. HOOKED.
- Lodestone compass pointing at the Needle: there's a lodestone at, or in, the Needle. HOOKED.

**3. Predictions.**
- Ash is alive, somewhere below, at or under the Needle, in the fog. He sent the allay. The lone bell is him (or another survivor).
- The Highkeeper rigs Forty's buckets, or moves the Well, so that Forty jumpers die and Forty stays exclusive. "Always room on Forty" means people who aim there don't make it. Maybe the clouds hide it.
- Buh will refuse the Highkeeper's bucket on Drop Day and bring his own; Tilly will jump from Floor 1 and be the comic sidekick and wildcard.
- "Then I'll jump from forty-one" is a setup. Somebody builds above the limit, or jumps from the Needle, in the climax.
- Rook will either betray Buh or be the one who dies in the trap, raising the stakes mid-movie.

**4. Interest: 8/10. Click next: yes.**
The cold open is an emotional hit and the empty-bucket shot is a great image. The cliffhanger is strong and clean: "with an eight" pays off the sign in scene 1 in a way any 10-year-old gets instantly, and it converts "dead brother" into "brother alive and warning me." Small worry: the 8UH setup is so on-the-nose that sharp viewers will see the payoff coming 10 minutes early. That's mostly fine; it feels earned rather than cheap.

Editor notes, chapter 1:
- First 30 seconds: scene 1 is a quiet note-block moment with no danger. The billed "hook" (the fall) doesn't land until about 1:45-2:00. On YouTube that's late. Consider opening on a 3-second flash of the empty bucket mid-fall, then cut back to the note block, or tighten scenes 1-2 to 60 seconds combined.
- Runtime as written: about 10:30-11:30. The beat timings add up to 12:00, but scenes 1, 2, 4 and 8 are a handful of lines each. The 2-minute ledge and market scenes are the only ones with room to breathe. It's a full chapter, not padded.
- Crowded: the chapter introduces Ash, Buh, Mom, Pip, Rook, the Highkeeper, Keepers, Tilly, Tilly's grandpa and mom, the Well, the Needle, the Puddle, the Edge, the clouds, the census, the bell, Drop Day and the Cradle. That's a lot for 12 minutes. The Cradle and the grandpa can wait.

## Chapter 2: The Seventeenth Bucket

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
The landing pool, the Well, sits on top of the Needle, a stone spire whose top is level with the low floors. Jumpers from Forty aim at it from far above. Every year for thirty years exactly one Forty jumper "misses." Buh and Tilly steal a look at the Highkeeper's ledger and find that it isn't a miss: each year a family is granted a baby (the "Cradle," +1) and someone is "Named." Ash was Named the year Pip was born. One of the seventeen buckets is empty, ready for the swap. Buh thinks he's this year's Named, follows the hermit Fen down a ladder inside the Needle, and comes out under the fog into a green world with a lake, campfires and people. Ash is alive there.

**2. Confusion log.**
- Hook: the empty bucket at Floor 12, solved 90 seconds later by Rook's confession. It's labeled a red herring, but it doesn't last long enough to mislead anyone, and I'm unclear why Rook wanted Buh scared. Fewer rivals for Forty? ANNOYED.
- Geography click: "the Needle's top: the Well set into stone," reached by a walkway from Floor 2. I finally understand that the Well is *on top of the Needle*, so jumpers aim down at a spire, and a miss means falling past it into the fog. This should be clear in chapter 1's establishing wide. Right now I only get it in chapter 2. LOST in chapter 1, fixed here.
- "It rings twice for the one." / "Echo," says Fen. I don't know who says the first line (Buh? Tilly?), and I still don't know what the bell is: a bell on the Well that rings on landing? Rings twice when someone passes it? LOST on the mechanism, HOOKED on the mystery.
- "Thirty, one a year, never two, never none." It lands: it's a quota, not an accident. HOOKED.
- The ledger: "+1 (Cradle)" and "Named:". "Marla's house. +1 (Pip). Named: Ash." I *think* this means one birth costs one life, the tower being full (build limit), but nobody says it. It's a great, dark idea and it's left entirely to inference. Does the Named come from the family that gets the baby? Ash was. If that's the rule, *Tilly's* family has the Cradle this year, so the Named should be Tilly, not Buh. LOST: I can't tell whether I'm meant to notice that.

- The Highkeeper sees Tilly and lets her go without a word. HOOKED, nicely creepy. Either she's already Named (so it doesn't matter) or he wants her to talk.
- "The Buh boy asks too many questions," in another hand. Whose hand? A Keeper? Someone else above the Highkeeper? HOOKED.
- "Then it's me this year." Buh concludes he's Named from one margin note, while the ledger pattern points at Tilly's family. I'm not sure whether he's being dumb, or I am. LOST.
- Fen: hermit at the Needle, keeps the tally, denies the bell, secretly carries sacks down into the Needle, has an allay sleeping in his hut. So Fen is the allay sender? Or Ash's courier? Fine as a mystery. HOOKED.

- "Took you long enough, Buh." Ash sent the warning tag ("don't take his bucket") but also expected Buh to come down. Mixed motive; I want to know which he wanted. HOOKED.

**3. Predictions.**
- One-in, one-out: the tower can't grow (build limit, and building past the Edge is banned), so every licensed baby is paid for by a Named jumper from Forty. The Named don't die. They fall past the Needle into the hidden lake and live exiled below the fog. Either the Highkeeper knows and it's an exile system, or he thinks it's a death and Fen secretly saves them.
- Fen is the ferryman: he supplies the people below, owns the allay, and sent the compass on Ash's behalf.
- The bell rings once for a landing in the Well and twice when someone falls past to the lake. "Echo" is Fen covering.
- This year's Named is Tilly (her family has the Cradle) and Buh will realize it late. Or it was switched to Buh because he's nosy.

- Climax: Drop Day, with Buh doing something public that breaks the system. My guess is he jumps from Forty with his own bucket and deliberately misses, or brings the people from below up through the Needle.

**4. Interest: 9/10. Click next: yes, immediately.**
Best cliffhanger so far: a world reveal and the dead brother alive, with a great line. The ledger heist is the right set piece at the right spot.

Editor notes, chapter 2:
- Structure: the descent is the act-one break of the movie, landing at about 24 of 60 minutes (40%). That's late for a feature, but in a serial it's a perfect end-of-episode turn. Fine.

- Biggest clarity risk: the one-birth-one-death rule is the movie's thesis, and the chapter delivers it as ledger shorthand. Somebody needs to say it out loud. Buh's "Named. Somebody picks" is half a line.
- Runtime as written: about 12 minutes. The heist at 3 minutes is properly fat. The Needle visit at 2.5 is dense, with tallies, the bell, Fen and the allay. Training and night are 1 minute each and could be 40 seconds. Holds 12.
- First 30 seconds: yes. An empty bucket at POV height is an instant hook.

## Chapter 3: The Below

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Ash explains the trick with a working model. For the Named jumper, the Well is shifted one block by dispensers (an empty bucket picks the water up, a full one puts it down next door), and a piston opens the floor where it was. The Named, already holding an empty bucket, drops through the Needle into a hidden lake below and survives. Thirty of them live down there, started by Fen's daughter Wren, the first one. They can't go back one at a time, because their family would just name someone else and the Highkeeper might fill the lake. They can only go back all at once, in public. Then the gut-punch: the Highkeeper doesn't choose the Named. A family that gets a baby has to name one of its own, and Mom named Ash.

**2. Confusion log.**
- "Two buckets. One empty, one full. That's the whole trick." Is this about the dispensers in the model, or the jumper's swapped bucket plus the Well's water? I read it both ways. As a line it's great, but it has to mean one clear thing on first hearing. LOST for a beat.
- The rig needs both an empty bucket in the jumper's hand *and* the Well moving. That makes sense once I think it through (a skilled jumper places water, a normal one aims for the Well), but the chapter never says why both are needed. ANNOYED.
- "The floor under it opens." So the Named fall *through* the Needle. Chapter 1 says Ash "drops past the Needle into the fog," which sounds like outside it. Through or past? CONFUSES the cold open, now that I know the rig.
- Who presses the button? Two presses means two dings, which I now get is "it rings twice for the one." But is it the Highkeeper on Forty, a Keeper, or Fen at the Needle? The question of who's complicit hangs on this. LOST.
- The lone bell in chapter 1 ("though nobody is jumping") is still unexplained. Ash testing his model? Somebody signaling? ANNOYED.
- Does the Highkeeper know the Named survive? Ash's line ("if he ever finds the lake, he fills it with gravel") says no, he thinks they die. But then why does his rig open a trapdoor into a shaft instead of just letting them hit stone? A trapdoor makes sense for hiding a body. It also implies somebody, presumably Fen, put the lake there in secret, maybe after Wren. The chapter doesn't say who built the lake or when. LOST, and it's load-bearing.
- "We could dig out any day." Buh just climbed *down a ladder*. Why dig? Why not climb? NITPICK, but it'll get comments.
- "If one of us goes back, our family names another. You." Clear and strong. HOOKED.
- "Only all thirty, at once, in front of everyone." That's the movie's goal statement, good. But it's buried in the "why they stay" scene, and Wren has just said she won't go back. Who wants the plan: Ash? Is it a plan or a dream? LOST on whether it's agreed.
- "Mom named me." Huge. It recasts "Don't look," "Floor 10 has real windows" and "look where it got him." HOOKED in the best way. Logic check: did Ash know he was Named when he stepped off grinning? He seems not to. Then how does the system make sure the Named jumps from *Forty*? All thirty tally marks are 40s. Ash chose Forty himself ("Which floor?" "Forty."). Was he lured, or does the rig only work from 40? LOST.
- This confirms my chapter 2 hunch: Tilly's family has the Cradle, so Tilly's family names one this year. Grandpa's "our family jumps from Forty, family rule" now sounds like steering her into the trap. If Buh "named himself" from a margin note in chapter 2, that's now plainly wrong by the rule stated here. That's good if it's a deliberate misdirect Buh will realize. If it isn't, it's a mistake.
- The Below has children born free with no Cradle needed, which is a quiet thematic counterpoint. Nice, if a character points it out.

**3. Predictions.**
- Tilly is this year's Named. Her grandpa named her, which is why he pushes Forty, and why the Highkeeper let her walk out of the study: she's already doomed.
- Buh goes back up, and chapter 4 opens on Mom. A confrontation with Marla is the emotional centerpiece.
- The Highkeeper finds out about the lake, maybe by following Buh or Fen, and moves to fill it with gravel before Drop Day. So this year's Named would really die. That's the ticking clock for chapter 4.
- Climax on Drop Day: the thirty climb the Needle in front of everyone while Buh or Tilly jumps. Buh probably takes Tilly's place or her bucket, and maybe uses the rig itself, or reverses it, in public. Someone (Fen?) has to choose a side.
- Wren is the internal antagonist of the plan, and she'll come round at the last second.
- "Build limit" / "forty-one" still has to pay off. Maybe Buh builds above the limit, or the "forty-first floor" is the Below.

**4. Interest: 9/10. Click next: yes.**
"Mom named me" is a top-tier cliffhanger. It turns the story from "corrupt official" to "complicit family," which is the most adult move in the piece, and it stays clean enough for kids. The fire scene ("I heard it every night. From the bottom.") is the best line so far.

Editor notes, chapter 3:
- This is the exposition chapter, and it mostly gets away with it: the model rig shows instead of tells, and the mob siege gives it action. Buh, though, has no goal and no clock in this chapter. He watches, learns and fights a wall he has no stake in. Add one line that sets the clock: "Drop Day is in three days. If you're not up by morning, they'll know." Or have the camp notice someone's been watching.

- Runtime as written: about 12 minutes, the most honestly 12 of the three. The fire scene at 2.5 minutes can go longer if the acting's there.
- First 30 seconds: yes. The punch reunion is a great open, because it's conflict before warmth.

## Chapter 4: One In, One Out

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Ash made Mom name him so twelve-year-old Buh wouldn't spend four years knowing it was him. Mom had offered herself, but you can't name yourself. Fen built the rig under orders and secretly dug the lake the same year, because the first Named was his daughter. Tilly turns out to be from the Highkeeper's own family. Her family has the Cradle, and the torn page says "Named: Tilly." Her father Hollis wants to swap in Buh instead ("Nobody misses a Low"). The kids and the Below plan to beat the rig in public on Drop Day: Tilly cuts "the line," Buh jumps from Forty, Fen opens the hatch, and Ash brings all thirty up in front of everyone. Then Hollis removes Fen, puts a Keeper on the lever, and Mom forbids Buh to jump from anywhere but Floor 10. Buh climbs to Forty anyway.

Orlo says "That's not how it works," so I think Orlo is the Highkeeper (rule-keeper, grandpa) and Hollis is the son. But then "Hollis comes down with Keepers" in the all-is-lost, so Hollis gives Keepers orders too. Until now the Highkeeper has had no name. LOST, and this is the main antagonist. Name the Highkeeper in chapter 1 and introduce Hollis on screen before he's needed.

That's a good plant, but it's never *stated* to Buh or to me. It comes through only as subtext in the overheard scene. LOST.

As a viewer, an old man taking shears out of a drawer means nothing to me. LOST. The plant has no object to attach to.

- "The Wellwright is unwell." "Wellwright" is Fen's title, never used before. ANNOYED.
- Rook "told the truth and sick about his family moving to Forty." I think his family is moving into a dead Named family's room? Or he's sick that Forty is built on the Named? I don't quite get why moving to Forty is guilt-making for Rook specifically. ANNOYED.
- Mom: "I said take me. They don't let you choose yourself." "He's alive." "Don't." Clear and strong. HOOKED.

## Chapter 5: Thirty-Three

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
On Drop Day Tilly escapes being Named by jumping from Floor 1. In the lever room she finds the line already cut by her grandfather, the Highkeeper Orlo, with his shears, and her father Hollis repairing it to send Buh into the rig. At the Plank, Buh answers the census with "Thirty-three," and Orlo gives him the full bucket. The Well moves in front of the city anyway, but Buh beats it with his Floor 6 trick, lands on the Needle, and opens the hatch: the thirty climb out. Mom plays the song, and Ash and Mom reunite. Orlo's own grandson was born that morning. He names himself, steps off past the Well expecting to die, and surfaces in Fen's lake, alive. He never knew about it. Weeks later a staircase runs down to the green world, and on the horizon there are other towers, with their own double bell.

ANNOYED (and a Minecraft-literate audience will be loud about it).

# FINAL REPORT (written after reading bible.md)

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend

There's a city that's one giant tower, forty floors high, standing over a sea of fog. At sixteen every kid makes one jump with one water bucket, aiming at a single block of water on top of a stone pillar called the Needle, and whatever floor you jump from is where your family lives forever. Every year one kid who jumps from the top "misses." Buh's big brother Ash was one of them. Three years later Buh is about to jump from the top himself, when an allay starts bringing him messages in Ash's handwriting. Buh and Tilly, a rich girl from upstairs, steal a look at the Highkeeper's ledger and find that the misses are chosen. They follow the old hermit Fen down a ladder inside the Needle and come out under the fog in a hidden green world. Ash is there, alive, with thirty others. The Well is rigged to slide over and drop the chosen one through a hole, and Fen secretly dug a lake to catch them. The real horror is that when a family gets permission for an extra baby, it has to name one of its own children to fall. Mom named Ash, because Ash made her do it to spare Buh. This year the named one is Tilly, the Highkeeper's own granddaughter, and her dad wants to swap in Buh instead. On Drop Day Tilly saves herself by jumping from Floor 1. Buh jumps from the top, the Well moves in front of everybody, and he lands on bare stone with his own water placement, a trick he learned on Floor 6. Then he opens the hatch and all thirty "dead" kids climb out and wave. The Highkeeper, sick of what he's done, jumps to his death, except he lands in the lake and lives. They build a staircase down to the green world. In the last shot there are other towers on the horizon, and one of them is ringing its bell twice.

## Did the chapters deliver the bible? Rules and clues that did NOT come across from the chapters alone

**World rules that never reach the screen:**
1. **The fog is believed to be the void** ("The Below keeps what it takes"). This is never said, and it's load-bearing. Without it, the audience can fairly ask why nobody has ever gone down to look, and the green-world reveal is surprising but not *sacrilegious*.
2. **The Keepers take everything off you at the plank; you get exactly one bucket, from the Highkeeper's hand.** Never said. Without it, "Don't take his bucket" reads as advice Buh can simply follow, and he then visibly doesn't (he takes the Highkeeper's bucket in chapter 5). The audience will also wonder why no Forty jumper brings a spare.
3. **Forty is "cursed": one Forty jumper misses every year, and everybody knows it.** Never said in chapter 1. We learn the pattern privately from Fen's tallies in chapter 2. Without the public curse, Mom's "Floor 10 has real windows" and Rook's motive ("scared off Forty") lose their grounding.
4. **The Drop is at 16.** Never said, and the chapters contradict it: Buh is 12 in a cold open set three years ago, which makes him 15. Ash's "you'd have waited four years" implies 16.
5. **The bell is on Floor 40 and rings as each jumper steps off.** Never shown. I spent two chapters thinking the bell was at the Well or in the fog, and chapter 1's "far off in the fog, a single bell" made it worse.
6. **The Count (a fixed number of beds) and the Cradle as the one licensed baby.** The word "Count" never appears, and "one in, one out" is said only by Orlo in chapter 5. The core rule (a family that gets the Cradle must name one of its children) is stated exactly once, in the chapter 3 cliffhanger, as a reveal. It's never stated as a rule people live under.
7. **"A cradle needs its parents"** (why parents can't name themselves). Only the rule comes across ("They don't let you choose yourself"), not the reason.
8. **The lever room on Forty, its two levers, and the line running down the tower to the Needle.** Never shown before chapter 4. In chapter 4, "the line," "the lever room" and "the trapdoor's lever" all arrive as references to things the audience hasn't seen.
9. **Nothing past the Edge is "the Founders' law, and nobody remembers why"; the Keepers are iron golems.** The smashed block reads. The why-nobody-knows doesn't.
10. **Floors 25-40 above the clouds (Highs) versus 1-24 below (Lows), with sun and gold against rain and mushrooms.** Only fragments come through ("below the clouds," "Nobody misses a Low"). The rank system is inferable, not shown.
11. **Fen is "the Wellwright."** The title appears once, in chapter 4, unexplained.

**Character intentions that don't come across:**
12. **Buh's want: get Mom and Pip above the clouds.** He never says it. His want reads as "be like Ash." "I'll jump from forty-one" reads as a build-limit joke, not "beating Ash's record."
13. **Ash's arc ("cocky martyr who did it alone, to letting his brother help").** Not dramatized at all. The brothers part in fury at the top of chapter 4, and the next time they're in a scene together the plan is already agreed, off screen.
14. **Tilly is the Highkeeper's granddaughter from Floor 40.** It's inferable only in chapter 4, and confirmed only by "Grandpa, no!" in chapter 5. Her arc ("stands up to her own father") isn't delivered: she grabs his arm too late and never confronts him.
15. **Orlo named Tilly, and Orlo was tempted to swap her for Buh.** On screen it's Hollis who proposes the swap and Orlo who refuses ("That's not how it works"). Nobody says who named Tilly. The bible's darkest beat (a grandfather naming his granddaughter) is absent.
16. **Orlo is "kind."** The treatment plays his chapter 1 smile as a villain tell. His kindness only becomes visible in chapters 4-5.
17. **The margin note is Hollis's handwriting.** No handwriting match is ever shown. I guessed it from his "Name the Buh boy" line, but the clue isn't paid off as a clue.
18. **Fen's arc ends with him opening the trapdoor.** In chapter 5 Buh opens it, because Fen was marched up to Forty. So the bible and the chapters contradict each other on Fen's payoff.
19. **Wren is "the voice the plan has to win over."** No scene wins her over. She says one line in chapter 3 and climbs out in chapter 5.
20. **The allay's name (Ping), and that it lives close enough to hear Buh's note block.** It's never named, and it drops out of the story after chapter 1.

**Clue-ladder rungs that didn't land:**
21. **"There's always room on Forty" leading to "it was never full, it was unequal."** The empty rooms are shown in chapter 2, but nobody draws the conclusion. They read either as creepy décor or as a plot hole: why kill for space when Forty has empty rooms? This is the movie's political thesis and it's left on the floor.
22. **Tally marks all "40," paid off in chapter 3 by "the one is always made to jump from Forty."** Chapter 3 never says this. Chapter 4 says "the rig only works from Forty," but *how* the named one is steered to Forty (Tilly doesn't know she's named; Ash did) is never explained.
23. **The compass pointing at the Needle, leading to Fen's hut.** On screen nobody follows the compass. Buh and Tilly just go to Fen's. The plant is orphaned.
24. **Rook's bucket swap rhyming with Rook holding up the dispenser's bucket.** Technically present, but the swap is resolved so fast (90 seconds) that the rhyme has nothing to rhyme with.

**Rungs that landed well:** the census "Four / Three, son" paid off by "Thirty-three"; the Floor 6 stone trick paid off from Forty; 8UH to 8UCKET; the song paid off by "from the bottom" and Mom playing it; Tilly's Floor 1 joke paid off as her survival; seventeen buckets paid off by the two buckets on the Plank; the lone bell paid off by the other towers (it works on a re-watch); and the Edge paid off by the staircase.

**Bible document issues (for the writers, not the audience):**
- "The ones stay hidden by choice (see the first fix above)": there is no "first fix" in the bible. It's a dangling reference.
- "One new baby a year, by the Highkeeper's leave" doesn't square with sixteen 16-year-olds jumping every year. The ledger's "+1 (Cradle)" implies the Cradle is one baby *above* the Count, not the only birth. Pin this down before dialogue, or a line will break the math. One clean version: "Two children is the Count. A third needs the Cradle. The Cradle needs a name." That fits Marla's house exactly: Ash, Buh, then Pip.
- The line is "redstone" (bible, chapter 5), but Orlo's clue is *shears* (chapter 4). Shears don't cut redstone.

## Unanswered questions

## Plot holes and contradictions (severity, smallest fix)

1. **The climactic fall has no mortal stakes.** Buh knows the lake is under the hole. If the rig takes him, he lands beside Ash. The two-minute slow-motion fall is played as peril the audience knows isn't there. *Severity: CONFUSES (tension leak; close to BREAKS for the climax).* *Smallest fix:* make it a choice. In the chapter 4 plan, Ash says, "If the floor opens, drop through. The lake'll catch you." Buh: "Then nobody sees." On Drop Day he deliberately aims at the stone instead of the hole, a one-tick window at full speed, so the city can see. The danger he chooses *is* the climax.
2. **The ages don't add up** (12 + 3 = 15, jumping at 16). *NITPICK, but it will be the top comment.* *Fix:* Buh is 13 in the cold open, and Ash's line becomes "you'd have waited three years knowing."
3. **"Drops past the Needle" (cold open) versus falling *through* the opened floor (chapter 3 model, chapter 5).** *CONFUSES the flashback.* *Fix:* the cold open shows the stone opening and Ash dropping *through* the Needle's top.
4. **Empty rooms on Forty against the one-in-one-out logic.** *CONFUSES.* *Fix:* one exchange in the chapter 2 heist. Tilly: "Half of Forty's empty." Buh: "Then what are they counting?"
5. **"Don't take his bucket," then Buh takes his bucket.** *CONFUSES.* *Fix:* establish in chapter 1 that the Keepers strip jumpers (one bucket, his hand, no exceptions). Then Ash's warning becomes "make him show you it's full," and at the Plank Buh says "Thirty-three" and then "Show them it's full." It's active, public, and pays off the tag.
6. **How the named one gets to Forty.** *CONFUSES.* *Fix:* Ash, in chapter 3: "They never tell the one. Your family just says 'we jump from Forty.' I found out. So I made her pick me." This also explains Tilly's grandpa line in hindsight.
7. **Shears versus a redstone line.** *NITPICK (but a Minecraft audience will pounce).* *Fix:* the cut is a missing repeater, and Orlo's clue is a repeater in his drawer. Or make the line tripwire string and keep the shears, and have Hollis re-string it.
8. **The Keeper guarding the hatch lever is beaten by "the Keeper turns; Buh pulls it anyway."** *NITPICK.* *Fix:* Rook, who's standing right there, steps between them. That gives Rook an active beat, not just a shout.
9. **Hollis rings twice for a Low kid in front of the whole city and suffers nothing.** *CONFUSES.* *Fix:* a 15-second beat on the Plank after Orlo's line. Tilly faces her father: "You rang it. For him." He has no answer, and the Keepers look at him, not at Buh. That also delivers Tilly's bible arc.
10. **Fen opens the trapdoor (bible) versus Buh opens it (chapter 5).** *Bible contradiction.* *Fix:* keep Buh pulling it, because hero agency is worth more, and rewrite Fen's arc in the bible to end at the lake line, which is better anyway.
11. **Orlo "tempted to swap" (bible) versus Orlo refusing Hollis's swap (chapter 4).** *Bible contradiction.* *Fix:* give Orlo one beat of temptation in chapter 4: he looks at Buh's name in the ledger, picks up the pen, and puts it down, *then* takes out the cutting tool.
12. **The Cradle math** (see the bible notes). *NITPICK on screen today, BREAKS THE STORY if a line says "one baby a year."*
13. **"We could dig out any day" when there's a ladder.** *NITPICK.* *Fix:* "We could climb out any day."
14. **The compass and the allay are orphaned after chapter 1.** *NITPICK.* *Fix:* see change 5 below.

## Best 3 moments
1. **"Thirty-three. Mom, Pip, me, and the thirty you sent down."** The cleanest setup and payoff in the script, and the title of the finale. It turns a bureaucratic question into the hero's thesis.
2. **"Mom named me."** (Chapter 3 cliffhanger, plus the chapter 4 hook "I made her.") The movie's midpoint reversal: it moves the guilt from a villain to the family. That's the most adult idea in the piece, and it's still kid-legible.
3. **The Floor 6 stone trick, done from Forty.** The hero's skill, planted in minute four as a show-off move, is precisely the counter to the villain's machine in minute fifty-four. The hero causes the climax with something we watched him learn.

(Close behind: "I heard it every night. From the bottom." Fen's "I couldn't stop your bell. So I dug a lake." Tilly's Floor 1 jump and the crowd's half-laugh.)

## Story editor's section

### Structure as experienced
- **Act 1 (chapters 1-2, 0-24 min).** The inciting incident is the allay's name tag (about 12:00). For a feature that's late, since it would usually land around 6:00, but it's exactly right for an episode end. The first chapter's mid-turn is the census "Three, son" plus the X beside Ash (about 6:00), and it does its job. Lock-in is Buh going down the Needle (24:00). At 40% that's a late act-one break, but it reads well in a serial because chapter 2 has its own complete heist arc.
- **Act 2A (chapter 3).** The Below: wonder, explanation, a siege. **Midpoint: "Mom named me" (about 36:00, 60%).** It's a true reversal, from "the Highkeeper is evil" to "the families chose." This is the strongest structural beat in the script.
- **Act 2B (chapter 4).** "I made her" (softening), Tilly named by her own family (a second twist that makes Tilly the stake and Buh "not the one"), the plan, then the **all-is-lost at about 46:00**, which is the weakest structural beat in the movie. It's a logistics setback, not a loss. Nobody dies or is lost, nothing irreversible happens, and the hero's belief isn't broken.
- **Act 3 (chapter 5).** Climax at about 52-54 min (the fall and the reveal), then a *second* climax (Orlo's leap and the lake), the epilogue and the button.

**Does each twist turn the story?**
- Rook's swap: **no**. It's dead weight.
- The ledger, "Named": **yes**, from accident to selection.
- The green world and Ash alive: **yes**, from death to exile.
- "Mom named me": **yes**, the biggest turn.
- "I made her": **yes**, but it partly undoes the previous turn within 30 seconds. That's great as a serial hook, slight whiplash in a binge.
- "Named: Tilly": **yes**.
- Fen taken: **no**.
- The line already cut: **yes, small**.
- Hollis re-laying it: **yes**.
- Orlo's leap: **yes**.
- Orlo alive: **yes, emotionally**.

That's a strong twist density for 60 minutes. The two "no" beats are the two weakest scenes.

### Arcs, and whether they pay off
- **Buh.** His real spine on screen is *who you count*: "Four" (refusing to subtract Ash) to "Thirty-three" (refusing to subtract anyone). That works and it's lovely. What doesn't come through is his stated want (getting Mom and Pip above the clouds), so his chapter 4 defiance of Mom has no cost to *his* goal. He has no flaw to overcome; he's admirable from start to finish. In a 60-minute serial that's acceptable, but a flaw would deepen it: maybe pride in being Ash's brother, the "forty-one" bravado. **Does the hero cause the climax? Mostly yes:** he chooses Forty, says "Thirty-three," does the stone trick and pulls the hatch. The one leak is that Orlo *hands* him survival with the full bucket. Change 2 below plugs it.
- **Ash.** The bible arc ("does it alone, then lets his brother help") doesn't exist on screen. He's warm and wounded (the fire scene is his best), but he never changes. **Doesn't pay off.**
- **Mom.** It's delivered in images: the curtain, the note block she can't play, kneeling, then playing. It's strong *imagery*, but the payoff (seeing Ash) gets no dialogue, and she and Buh never resolve "Floor 10 or don't jump." **Half pays off.**
- **Tilly.** From a rebel joke to named to surviving *by her own joke*: great. "Stands up to her father" never happens. **Half pays off.**
- **Rook.** Rival to witness. Small, and it works once he's on the Needle.
- **Orlo.** The most complete arc in the film: kind gatekeeper, the shears, the hover, self-naming, the lake. It may be *too* complete, because in chapter 5 it out-weighs Buh's family.
- **Hollis.** Arrives in chapter 4 and never ends. **Doesn't pay off.**
- **Fen.** The chapter 4 confession plus the lake line pays off emotionally, even though the bible's payoff (opening the trapdoor) moved to Buh.
- **Wren.** One line in, one climb out. **Doesn't pay off.**
- **Pip.** The fourth-birthday image is a good bookend.

### Emotional core
It's two brothers and a mother who chose, and the "chose" is what makes it land. "Mom named me" followed by "I made her" is a genuinely affecting turn that plays for both kids and adults. **It lands at the midpoint and is underpaid at the end.** As written, the three people at the heart of the movie (Buh, Ash, Mom) never share a scene with dialogue after the reveal. Buh and Ash part angry, Mom refuses to believe, and the reunion is a stage direction. Meanwhile Orlo gets the last big monologue and the last laugh-cry. The final 4 minutes currently belong to the villain. The family reunion should come *after* the lake scene, as the emotional end, and it needs real lines.
