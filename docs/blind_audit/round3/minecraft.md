# Final report (written after reading bible.md)

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend

A city lives in one stone tower that reaches exactly to build limit, above a sea of fog. The Highs who run it say the tower is full because "there is no forty-one". So every family may have two kids, nobody may build out past the Edge, and at sixteen every kid jumps once, from the floor they want to own, into a one-block Well on a pillar called the Needle. They carry one bucket handed to them by the Highkeeper as a backup.

The dark secret: a third child (the Cradle) costs a name. The family picks one of its own kids, who gets an empty bucket and a rigged jump from Forty. A slow redstone bell line rolls down the tower, a dispenser drinks the Well, a sticky piston opens a hole, and the kid drops through the Needle into the "void". Except the old Wellwright, Fen, secretly dug a lake at the bottom. Thirty "dead" kids are alive down there, including Buh's brother Ash, whom Mom named so she could have Pip.

Buh finds them, learns the truth, and plans to make the trap fire in public. The Highkeeper's son Hollis, who wants to name Buh instead of his own daughter Tilly, fills the Needle with gravel so the hole kills. On Drop Day, Buh does the impossible one-tick water clutch from the top of the world beside the opening hole, in front of everyone. The thirty dig themselves out with a torch trick. The old Highkeeper pays his family's debt by jumping himself, and lands in Fen's lake. The city walks down a staircase into a green world and starts building out. Then, far away, another tower's bell rings twice.

## Did the chapters deliver the bible?

Mostly yes. The three-step reveal (lake, then trick, then the Cradle's price) lands in the intended order, and the clue ladder is unusually tight. These things in the bible did **not** come across from the chapters alone:

1. **What a normal Drop looks like** (you aim for the one-block Well, and the bucket is the backup). I didn't get this until the Ch3 model and Ch5's "one toll, one splash". For two chapters I couldn't tell what the bucket was for.
2. **"Build limit is the only rule nobody made" / "Then who made the rest?"** The theme lands at the end ("41", "build out"), but the Ch2 line that is supposed to state it read as gibberish to me. I didn't connect "the rest" to "the rest of the rules".
3. **Half of Forty is empty *because* of the Edge law** (the shortage is manufactured). "Half of Forty's empty" landed as a clue, but the causal link to "Nothing past the Edge" never got said.
4. **The Cradle is "celebrated".** It was never shown, so the cost has no public joy to contrast against.
5. **Wren is Fen's daughter.** This isn't in the chapters, and it's why Fen dug the lake. Without it, Fen's "So I dug a lake" is generous rather than personal, and Wren standing "beside her father" means nothing.
6. **Fen built the rig.** This is only implied by "Wellwright" and his last line.
7. **Cass is Jory's brother.** It's adjacent in Ch3 ("his house named his brother" / "Cass jumped wide") but never joined. I read Cass as someone else.
8. **Orlo believes the fog kills.** It's only stated in Ch5. In Ch3 I couldn't tell whether the Highs knew about the lake, or who made it.
9. **How the string relates to lever two.** I never could draw the circuit. The bible's "on 40 it runs through a tripwire" isn't on screen, and isn't vanilla anyway (see below).
10. **Buh "never higher than Floor 12" and wanting to get Mom and Pip above the clouds.** Both were faint. Mom's Floor 10 was the motive I actually heard.
11. **Hollis is Tilly's father.** Ch1's "Tilly's mom got the Cradle" plus "Teo holding Hollis's hand" made me think Hollis was the mother until Ch4.
12. **"Did Ash lie to his brother?"** (he promised "from the top" when he already knew). Too subtle; I didn't notice it at all.

## Persona section: does it play straight as vanilla 1.21? (Java veteran's audit)

Checked against minecraft.wiki and Mojang's bug tracker after the five chapter logs, plus my own tick-by-tick simulation of player gravity (each tick: move, then v = (v − 0.08) × 0.98).

### The fall and the clutch

**Water-bucket clutch from about 190 blocks, one-tick window: WORKS (fix the numbers in the bible).**
- From Forty a step-off takes **91 ticks (4.55 s)** and hits at **3.28 blocks/tick (66 b/s)**, not the bible's "3.6 blocks a tick, about five seconds". You haven't reached terminal velocity (3.92 b/t) by then.
- Water only places when the stone is within 4.5 blocks of your eyes. Eyes are 1.62 above your feet, so that means feet at most 2.88 above the stone. At 3.28 b/t you get exactly one valid tick in about 88% of falls and none in about 12%, so **about one in eight, not one in five**. "From Forty you get one tick. Sometimes none" is literally true, and speedrunners will love that line.
- Below about Floor 25 you always get at least one tick, sometimes two. That suits the Floor 6 trick.
- Keep the bible's rule: place in the last tick, scoop straight back, empty bucket back in the hotbar, never fire water at the ground from a distance.

**"He can't take the hole. He aims at the stone beside it" (Ch5): WORKS WITH A TWEAK.** Air strafing moves you a fraction of a block per tick, so a one-block shift has to start about 30-40 blocks up, before the hole visibly opens. Fix: he was never aiming for the Well. It's his plan from Ch4, so write it as a commitment from the Plank, not a last-second dodge.

**Ladders stopping a fall (Ch2 hook): WORKS.** Moving into a ladder while falling caps your descent at ladder speed (about 3 b/s) and you take no damage. Landing on top of a ladder does hurt. Tweaks:
- It must be a ladder column several blocks tall, so he's inside one at a tick boundary.
- "Stops dead" needs him holding sneak. Otherwise he slides.
- Bonus: Ch2's "ten seconds of white" works out to ladder descent (3 b/s) × 10 s = 30 blocks, exactly the bible's fog thickness. Nice.

**Hay bales (Ch1): WORKS.** They cut fall damage by 80%. "Hay bales are a lie" is the other 20%. From 190 blocks, 20% is still lethal, which is a good joke to keep.

**Landing in Fen's lake from Forty (Orlo): WORKS.** Any water negates fall damage. The lake just has to be wide enough to be under him when he jumps wide (see plot hole 6).

### The rig

**Dispenser with an empty bucket drinking the Well and pouring it back: WORKS WITH A TWEAK.**
- Real behaviour: it picks up the source, the filled bucket goes into an empty slot, the next firing places it and leaves an empty bucket. There's a 4-game-tick delay, and it fires on the rising edge only.
- Load **exactly one** bucket, or it fires a random slot.
- It needs **two separate pulses** (drink, then pour). A lever that just stays on gives one drink and never pours. The rig needs a pulse source (see the tripwire below) and a delayed second pulse.

**Sticky piston pulling the block under the water source: WORKS WITH A TWEAK.**
- **Sequence it: drink first, open one repeater-tick later.** If the floor opens while the source is still there, the source stays put and pours a waterfall down the shaft (which would also save the jumper, and everyone would see it).
- **Geometry: a 3x3 Needle can't contain a 2x1 shaft with walls on every side.** It needs at least 4x3. The horizontal sticky piston also needs two blocks beside the drop column (base plus head), so house it inside a two-layer cap.
- Ch1's "for one frame the square goes dark" is physically several ticks. With dispenser and piston delays plus a safety margin it's about 0.3-0.5 s, so "a blink". The game can't do less than one tick.

**Slow redstone line with repeaters and bells: WORKS WITH A TWEAK (the bible's numbers and logic need changing).**
- A 190-block descent in redstone dust is a staircase that needs **at least 13 repeaters**, so it takes 1.3 s at minimum delay and 5.2 s at full delay. "Full delay ≈ 4.5 s" is wrong. At full delay the hole opens about 0.6 s after the jumper has landed.
- The line must reach the cap by about 4.2 s (landing at 4.55 s, minus the dispenser's 4 ticks, the piston's 2 and a margin).
- **A uniform line would ring *ahead* of him** (bells below him first), not "chase him and catch up". For the treatment's image, hold the signal about 2.9 s on Forty (repeaters in the lever room), then run it down fast with 1-tick repeaters at about 146 b/s. It overtakes him around Floor 10 and reaches the cap about 0.35 s early. That's the "chasing, catching up" shot, and it's buildable.
- **"It's always Forty" must come from where the trigger is, not from timing.** Floor 39 lands only 2 ticks earlier.
- Lever one's toll "every bell at once" also needs delay-matched branches (every bell waits as long as the bottom one). Then they ring together about 1.3 s after the pull.
- Bells: one rings per redstone signal. Their sound volume is 2.0, so about 32 blocks (the wiki's base table says 16), and one bell every five floors is fine.

**Tripwire cut with shears: WORKS (the cut), BROKEN (how the bible wires it).**
- Shears break tripwire without the hooks firing. That's Java behaviour, and 1.21.2 restored shears breaking it. Placing string back into the gap re-links the hooks, so Hollis "bridging" is fine.
- But a redstone line can't "run through a tripwire": active tripwire produces no power, string runs only in a straight line of up to 40 blocks between two hooks, and it's a sensor, not a wire.
- **Fix:** string across the lip of Forty's Plank, run through a hole in the wall into the lever room. Lever two arms it (an AND gate). The jumper stepping off trips it, which starts the line on the exact tick (no human reaction lag), gives the dispenser its natural pulse (the hook stays on about 10 game ticks after the jumper clears it), and makes "only Forty" true.
- Change the Ch3 model to "a string across the Plank, a wire down the side", and Ash's line to "The string's only on Forty." Cut the "bell rope".

**An iron trapdoor opened by a redstone torch beside it: WORKS WITH A TWEAK.**
- True: iron trapdoors only toggle with redstone power, and a lit torch powers its cardinal neighbours (not the block it's attached to).
- But if the trapdoor is flush in the floor, a torch standing on the floor next to it is diagonal and does nothing. Show Buh breaking the stone beside the trapdoor and setting the torch in the notch, or seat the trapdoor on top of the floor. Fen's hut already has torches (Ch2 plant).
- **BROKEN elsewhere:** Ch4's hand-lifting and hand-shutting, and Ch5's diggers holding pickaxes under a mineable trapdoor (any pickaxe, about 1.9 s for stone). Fixes as in plot holes 1 and 2.

**Seventeen buckets (Ch2): WORKS, and it's a deep cut.** Water buckets don't stack, so counting the chest means counting slots: "Fifteen. Sixteen. Seventeen?" Players will notice and approve.

### The gravel night and the dig

**Gravel poured down a shaft, stone layers, digging up through gravel with the torch trick while climbing: BROKEN AS WRITTEN, fixable.**
- **Pour:** falling gravel goes straight down through air and water and stacks as a **pillar** on the lake bed under the drop column. "A mound rises" is wrong.
- **Ladder:** gravel can't fill a column with ladders in it. It stops on the highest rung (or pops into an item), so it plugs the top and leaves the ladder below clear. "The ladder is buried" doesn't happen.
- **Stone layers:** you can't place stone 60-100 blocks down from the hatch (reach 4.5, stone doesn't fall).
- **Recommended fix (pure vanilla, and creepier):** a Keeper drops down the drop column into the lake, then pillars up the shaft all night, jumping and placing gravel under himself. Every ten blocks he lays stone, and he breaks the ladder rungs beside him as he rises, filling that column too. That's how a player really fills a shaft. The thirty watch a torch-lit figure climb away above them, sealing them in.
- **Torch trick:** Moss's version has the order backwards ("puts a torch under a column, breaks one block"). There's no gap under a standing column. Break the bottom gravel and slap a torch in the gap, or (the climbing version) stand on a stone layer, set a torch on the wall at your feet, and break the gravel above your head. The whole ten-block stretch rains down and pops into items at your feet.
- The stone layers are what make it a night-long clock: torch, rush, climb (placing new ladders: "passing torches **and ladders** up"), pickaxe, pickaxe, repeat. That's exactly the Ch5 hook's rhythm. Keep the layers, but have someone legal place them.
- **"The floor opens onto gravel" is lethal: WORKS.** Landing one block lower on gravel still charges the whole fall from Forty.

## Summary (under 350 words)
