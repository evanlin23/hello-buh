# BUILD LIMIT: blind read, cinephile reader

Persona: late-twenties adult film lover (favourites: Interstellar, The Prestige, Arrival, The Truman Show, Snowpiercer); doesn't play Minecraft, doesn't watch kids' content; holding this to the bar of those films.

## Chapter 1: You Fall Once

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Highfall is a single forty-floor tower above a bottomless fog. Once a year, on Drop Day, kids jump off the Plank from a floor of their choosing, and that floor becomes their family's status for life; they're meant to land in the Well (a small pool on a neighbouring pillar, the Needle) with a water bucket as a backup. Three years ago the ruler, the Highkeeper, quietly swapped Buh's brother Ash's full bucket for an empty one, so Ash died, or seemed to. Now Buh is about to jump, a girl called Tilly from the doomed Floor 40 has turned up, and someone using Ash's signature is sending Buh messages by allay from the Needle.

**2. Confusion log.**
- The hook's bucket swap: clear and chilling. The "we see it; he doesn't" hotbar shot is good dramatic irony even though I don't play. I had to guess the hotbar is the item bar at the bottom of the screen; the treatment tells me, but the film has to make "plain bucket vs water bucket" readable to someone who has never seen the icons. HOOKED.
- Ages: "Ash (16)" and "Buh (13)" are given in a flashback set three years ago. Is Buh 13 now or 16 now? The kids on the ledge read as about 13. ANNOYED.
- Family arithmetic. Ash tells the Highkeeper "Four." Today Buh says "Mom, Pip, me. And Ash." But Pip is 3 and a "Cradle baby", so Pip may not have been born yet when Ash jumped. Then who was the fourth three years ago: a dad, or an unborn Pip? I get that the count matters (the Highkeeper asks before he swaps the bucket), so I want to be able to do the sum. LOST, briefly.
- "Two to a house." Two adults? Two children? Two people? It's clearly the key to the cull, and I can't work out the rule. ANNOYED, edging toward HOOKED.
- The Cradle. From context: a permit to have a baby. "Pip was a Cradle baby. We were lucky," right after we've watched Ash die, made me suspect Pip's birth was paid for by Ash's death. HOOKED, and the darkest idea so far.
- The second toll that "rolls down the tower bell by bell, chasing him". Is that the bells tracking the faller? Lovely image, unclear meaning. HOOKED.
- The blue square going dark for one frame: something covered the Well, or something went into it. HOOKED.
- "Far off in the fog, a single bell rings, though nobody is jumping." So there's something out there. HOOKED.
- The Puddle, the Edge, the Below, the Plank, the Needle, the Well, the Cradle, Drop Day, Keepers, Highkeeper, plus Brann, Orlo, Hollis, Teo, Juno, Rook, Kit, Tilly, Pip, Mom: about twenty proper nouns in twelve minutes. Hollis and little Teo are introduced holding hands with no function yet, so I don't know why I'm meeting them. ANNOYED.
- The law chant in the market is efficient world-building, but it's a list being read to the audience. In Snowpiercer the rules come out of Mason's mouth as a threat, in a scene with stakes. ANNOYED (minor).
- The MLG water trick on the practice ledge: I didn't know it, but the scene teaches it cleanly (pour water at the last moment, land in it, scoop it back up). Good.

**3. Predictions.**
- The tower is at capacity ("The tower is full. There is no forty-one"). Each year the Highkeeper kills one child on Floor 40 with an empty bucket to make room for a Cradle baby. "Forty's cursed. One a year." "There's always room on Forty." The census is how he picks the household that's over quota.
- Tilly is this year's target: her mom got the Cradle and the baby is due on Drop Day. Buh will try to save her, probably by jumping alongside her or switching buckets.
- Ash is alive, somewhere in or under the Needle, maybe below the fog. The dark frame in the Well was him going through it, not hitting stone. The allay, the compass and the "8" signature are his. "Don't take his bucket" means don't accept the Highkeeper's bucket.
- Possibly the fog/Below isn't bottomless at all and there's a world or community down there (the lone bell). Truman-style: the "edge of the world" is a lie maintained by the authorities.
- Orlo is the villain, but I suspect a reveal that the cull is "necessary" (a real resource limit, the build limit), so the film can ask whether the system is evil or just arithmetic.

**4. Interest and next chapter.**
Interest: 7/10. Yes, I'd click. The cold open is a genuinely good piece of cinema-grammar storytelling: the swap happens in plain sight, the line "The floor I jump from is the floor I live on" curdles the moment he steps off, and the intercut with the note-block song gives the fall an emotional counterweight. The premise is familiar (The Lottery, Logan's Run, The Giver, a bit of Snowpiercer's population maths), but the bucket is a fresh, concrete murder weapon. The cliffhanger is strong: a dead brother's handwriting telling you how not to die, delivered six days before your own jump. My worry is the noun density and the kids'-gang scenes (the joke-y "build limit, genius" banter), which edge toward the register the makers say they're avoiding.

## Chapter 2: The Seventeenth Bucket

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Someone empties Buh's practice bucket and he nearly dies. Orlo responds by "protecting" him with a minder (Brann) and a permit barring him from Floor 40. Buh and Tilly, who turns out to be Orlo's granddaughter, follow the compass to the Needle and meet Fen, a Wellwright who keeps a tally: thirty deaths, one a year, all from Forty. On Forty they find a lever room, a ledger that records each Cradle birth next to a "Named" child ("Marla's house. +1 (Pip). Named: Ash."), and a seventeenth, empty bucket for sixteen jumpers. Buh decides he's this year's Named. Then he climbs down inside the Needle, past a sticky piston holding up the Well's floor, through the fog, and finds a green world with people and campfires, and Ash, alive at 19.

**2. Confusion log.**
- The hook: "Someone emptied it, and the tag said 'Don't take his bucket.'" This was Buh's own practice bucket, not the Highkeeper's. Who emptied it: Ash, to teach him? Orlo's people? Hollis? And how does the tag's warning ("his" bucket) map onto a bucket nobody handed him? The sentence implies the tag explained this, and for me it doesn't. LOST.
- The permit, FLOORS 1 TO 39. If every Named child jumps from Forty (the tally says so) and Orlo has just barred Buh from Forty, then Orlo can't be planning to Name him. Yet Buh concludes "It's me this year." Either the permit is a trick (it pushes him to defy it and go to Forty), or it's protection. I don't know which, and the film needs me to. LOST.
- The dispenser "keeps it topped up". I clocked it as the trick: a dispenser can presumably pull the water out as well as put it in. Good plant-in-plain-sight, Prestige-style. HOOKED. As a non-player I'm guessing at the mechanics; if a dispenser can't drain water, the clue means nothing to me.
- Sticky piston "and the stone block it holds" under the Well. I read it as a trapdoor floor: pull the block, drain the water, and the faller drops down the Needle's shaft. HOOKED. But again, I'm inferring what a sticky piston does from the word "sticky".
- "It rings twice for the one." "Echo." This ties back to the rolling second toll in Ch1, and to the lone bell in the fog. The bell rope in the lever room is presumably the second toll: a signal to someone below that a Named child is coming. HOOKED, nicely planted.
- The red text "Height limit for building is 320." Is this the game breaking the fourth wall, or does the character actually see it? "He reads it twice", so he does. That means the characters can read the engine's own UI, which is a Truman Show "hitting the painted sky" moment. It's either the most interesting idea in the piece or a gag, and I can't tell which the writers mean. HOOKED / ANNOYED.
- "Then who made the rest?" I'm not sure what "the rest" is: the empty half of Forty? The world? The tower itself? I sense it's important. LOST (mildly).
- Ages again. Ash is 19 now, so Buh, 13 in the flashback, is 16 now. But Orlo "kneels to Buh's height", and the gang's banter reads younger. Pick one. ANNOYED.
- The margin note "the Buh boy asks too many questions", "in a different hand". Whose? Clearly a clue, but I have no handwriting samples to match it against, except that Ash writes B as 8 and this says "Buh" (spelled how on screen?). HOOKED, if it pays off.
- The heist: the kids get past Keepers, into a locked lever room (only through a grate), the study, the ledger and the bucket chest in one 3.5-minute pass, and the one incriminating page is helpfully torn out next to a helpfully incriminating margin note. Convenient. ANNOYED.

**3. Predictions.**
- The mechanism: the Named jumper gets the empty bucket. On their fall the lever room fires: the dispenser drains the Well, the piston opens its floor, and the second, rolling toll warns the people below. The child falls through the Needle, through the fog, into the lake at its foot, and survives. The bucket is empty so they can't save themselves on the cap and so they don't "miss" the hole.
- So the cull is an exile, not a murder. The Below is a livable green world that the tower has been told is a void. Orlo is maintaining the lie to keep the tower obedient and its population fixed, or (darker, and more interesting) because the Below has a cost the tower doesn't know about.
- Ash has been sending the allay. He'll want Buh to come down, or to bring the truth up. I expect Ch3 or Ch4 to reveal something wrong with the Below (it isn't paradise, or not everyone survived the fall, which would make the thirty tally marks a body count after all).
- Tilly's family has the Cradle this year. Somebody in her household must be "Named". Orlo won't name his own granddaughter, so he'll name Buh (the questions) and fix the ledger. Or the twist: Orlo does have to name Tilly, and that's his tragedy.
- The "different hand" in the margin is Hollis, who watched the permit change hands and is the real operator behind Orlo.

**4. Interest and next chapter.**
Interest: 8/10. Yes, definitely. The cliffhanger is the strongest kind: it overturns the premise. But I'm wary. If being Named means waking up beside a lake with campfires and your big brother, then the dread of Ch1 (an empty bucket, a boy falling to his death) is defused at the 24-minute mark of a 60-minute film. The film now has to find a second, worse truth, or it turns into a cosy "the scary place was nice all along" story. The Prestige and Snowpiercer earn their reveals by making the truth more costly than the lie, not less. Craft-wise: Fen's tally and the "Echo" line are excellent, economical, Arrival-quiet clueing. The heist is the weakest section, because everything is too easy to find.

## Chapter 3: The Below

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Below the fog is a camp of about thirty "ones", the Named, who survived the drop through the Well, plus children born there. They live behind a wall against nightly monsters, led by Wren, the first of them, and none of them will ever go back up: when Jory went back, his family simply named his brother. The real law is family arithmetic: a house may have two children; a third needs the Cradle, and the Cradle costs a child the house itself names, who then has to jump from Forty, because only from Forty does the fall last long enough for the levers to drain the Well and open its floor. Ash wasn't picked by the Highkeeper. Their mother named him to have Pip, and the curtain in Ch1 was her not letting Buh watch.

**2. Confusion log.**
- "Mom named me." Not confusion; the best moment of the film so far. It retroactively poisons three Ch1 lines: "Don't look," "And look where it got him," and "Pip was a Cradle baby. We were lucky." That's a Prestige-grade re-read. HOOKED.
- Did Ash know he was Named? The rule says the one "jumps from Forty". In Ch1 Ash grins, chooses Forty, and never checks his bucket: he reads as not knowing. If he didn't know, why did he pick Forty? Was he steered there ("Our family jumps from Forty" is Tilly's grandpa's line, not Ash's)? If he did know, his grin and "the floor I jump from is the floor I live on" mean something completely different (a boy walking knowingly to his death for his mother, which would be devastating). The treatment has to decide, and show it. LOST.
- "The house names one of its own ... or the house names another." Another of its own, or another house's child? I read it as "if the Named won't go, the house names a different one of its own" (that's what happened to Jory's brother). But the Ch2 margin note ("the Buh boy asks too many questions") and Buh's "It's me this year" only make sense if a house can name someone outside it. This is the load-bearing rule for the climax and it's ambiguous. LOST.
- "Two to a house" (Ch1) vs "Two is the Count": two children per household, apparently. So Ash's "Four" in Ch1 was Mom, Ash, Buh and an unborn Pip. Now the sum works, but only in retrospect, and no father is ever mentioned. ANNOYED (minor).
- "Thirty ones" vs Fen's thirty tally marks, yet Cass is dead ("jumped wide") and Jory went up and evidently came back. The numbers don't quite reconcile. NITPICK, but this film invites you to count.
- What do the Highs think happens? Wren: "If the Highs ever find this lake, they'll fill the Needle." So the Highs operate a drain-and-open mechanism from their own lever room but believe the hole leads to the void? Then who built the shaft down to a lake, and why? Fen (the "Wellwright") is presumably the secret mercy. Is he working for the Highs or against them? And what does the watching tower see: a child landing in the Well and vanishing? I don't know what the public story is. LOST.
- The model scene. Lovely staging (the boy rebuilding the crime in miniature from memory while his brother only confirms), but it's also the moment the film explains its own trick, line by line. The Prestige gets away with this because the explanation costs someone something. Here it's two brothers agreeing. ANNOYED, though I'd still keep it.
- Wren reciting the central law "between zombies", "as if he should already know it". Exposition with a sword in its hand. Convenient. ANNOYED.
- The gravel-and-torch trick. Planted conspicuously, with a "That's cheating" / "It's faster" button, so it will pay off later. As a non-player I learned from it that gravel falls and a torch breaks it into items. HOOKED (it's a Chekhov's gun).
- "This time he remembers past the curtain." Did Buh really see the floor open three years ago and repress it, or is this reconstruction? If he saw it, the Ch1 window shot (one dark frame, then the curtain) is fair. Good. HOOKED.

**3. Predictions.**
- This year's Cradle is in Orlo's own house (Tilly's mom). The house must name one of its own, which would be Tilly, the "family rule" Forty jumper. Tilly's plan to jump from Floor 1 is, unknown to her, an escape. Orlo, or more likely Hollis, intends to name Buh instead ("the Buh boy"), using some "name another" loophole. The torn-out page is that entry.
- The climax is Drop Day. Buh jumps from Forty on purpose, with his own bucket (hence "Don't take his bucket"), and makes the trick happen in front of the whole tower, maybe holding the Well open, or landing on the cap and surviving with his own water so everyone sees the floor open beneath an empty Well.
- Moss's gravel-and-torch trick gets used to fill or unfill the Needle, or to collapse something fast at the climax.
- There'll be a confrontation with Mom. If the film is serious, it's the real climax, not the Highkeeper.
- Dilemma: exposing the trick would end the secret mercy (the Highs "fill the Needle"), so telling the truth might kill future Named children. That's the adult version of the story, and I hope the film is brave enough to make Buh choose.

**4. Interest and next chapter.**
Interest: 9/10. Yes, immediately. "Mom named me" is a genuinely adult turn, and it moves the story from "evil ruler" to "a whole society, including your own mother, consents to this", which is where Snowpiercer and The Lottery live. The Below also now has a cost (monsters, a grave, Jory's brother, people who won't forgive), so my Ch2 worry that being Named = rescue is largely answered. The fire scene ("I heard it every night. From the bottom." He laughs, then he doesn't) is the emotional peak so far. Weaknesses: two scenes in a row exist to explain the rules (model, wall fight), and the rule I most need for the climax ("or the house names another") is the one I'm least sure of.

## Chapter 4: One In, One Out

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Ash volunteered ("Write me") so that 13-year-old Buh wouldn't spend three years waiting to die, and Mom, forbidden from offering herself, said his name. Buh confronts her, but she won't believe Ash is alive. Buh, Ash, Tilly, Rook and the thirty Named plan to expose the trick on Drop Day in front of the whole city. Then Hollis, who turns out to be Tilly's father and wants Buh named instead of his daughter, discovers the lake, arrests Fen, takes the hatch lever, and fills the Needle with gravel (with a stone layer every ten to beat the torch trick), so the opening floor now means death. The thirty are digging up from below. And the torn ledger page shows Orlo honestly named his own granddaughter, Tilly, which is why she always planned to jump from Floor 1.

**2. Confusion log.**
- The Mom flashback. Excellent: Orlo's kindness is unchanged from Ch1 but now reads as horror; "the house has to say it"; Ash's "Write me" from behind the curtain; Mom at the note block, unable to play. This is the emotional centre of the film. HOOKED.
- Did Ash know? Now yes: he chose. So in the Ch1 hook, "Ash is looking down at the drop and never checks it ... We see it; he doesn't" is either a cheat (it plays as a boy unaware of a swap) or, much better, a boy who knows exactly what he's holding and refuses to look. The treatment needs to direct the second reading: a glance he avoids, not one he misses. If it's played as written, a second-time viewer will feel misled. CONFUSES.
- If the Named know they're Named, did Ash know he'd die, or did he know about the hole? Wren says "They threw us away", so nobody knew about the lake. Ash walked off Forty believing he'd die, with a grin. On second viewing that's devastating, and the film should let it be. HOOKED.
- Rook emptied the practice bucket "to scare you off Forty". It resolves the Ch2 hook, but deflates it: the Ch2 hook explicitly tied the empty bucket to the tag "Don't take his bucket", and the answer is a friend's prank that nearly killed him. And why did Rook want Buh off Forty, when Buh was jumping from Ten anyway? ANNOYED.
- "Buh tells him what Forty is paid with." "My family's moving to Forty." I don't follow the economics. Is Forty status literally bought with Named children (Forty is "always" where the Named jump, so it has "always room")? Is Rook's family about to take a Cradle? A strong hint I couldn't decode. LOST.
- The rule from Ch3 resolves here: Hollis's "Name the Buh boy. Nobody misses a Low" is a cheat, not the law. A house names its own; Orlo did; Hollis is trying to swap in someone else's child. Good. But I only got it because of the torn page; the audience needs Ch3's rule to say "its own" without the "or names another" muddle.
- The plan. Buh refuses to drop into the lake ("Then nobody sees"), so I infer he'll jump from Forty as the decoy Named, land on the opened cap with his own water bucket (hence "Don't take his bucket"), and stay up there in plain view while Fen opens the hatch and the thirty climb out. The treatment never says where Buh lands or how he survives. CONFUSES.
- Wren: "All of us. Then there's nobody left to name." Why does all thirty coming up mean nobody is left to name? Because no single house can be punished (the Jory lesson)? Or because the tower would be over-full by thirty and the system collapses? A good line that I can't parse. LOST (mildly).
- Orlo alone with the shears. The treatment explains "Tripwire string can be cut with shears without setting it off," so I gather he means to disarm the trigger line, to save Tilly from his own trap. But I'm not sure which part the string controls (the toll? the timer? the piston?), so I don't know what his sabotage would actually do. HOOKED, but I needed the stage direction to get even that far, and a viewer won't have it.
- The iron trapdoor "with no lever it has no power". I'm guessing iron trapdoors can't be opened by hand. A non-player needs one earlier shot of someone failing to open it by hand. ANNOYED.
- The allay is suddenly called Ping. First time I've heard the name. ANNOYED (minor).
- Hollis's gravel night. A terrific, purely visual sequence: the escape route buried in silence; the thirty watching it rain into their lake; "every tenth layer a layer of stone" showing the villain is smart. HOOKED.
- Convenience count: Tilly "overheard her father"; Tilly then finds the torn page in her father's room. Two convenient discoveries by the same character in one chapter. ANNOYED.

**3. Predictions.**
- Drop Day: Tilly jumps from Floor 1 and survives (the trap can't fire from that low). Hollis has Buh named and forced up to Forty. Orlo has cut the tripwire, so the trap may not fire at all, or fires wrong.
- The thirty use Moss's torch trick to collapse nine layers at a time and pickaxe through the stone layers, racing to clear the shaft before Buh jumps. Ping's torch is the signal that they're coming. They'll break through at the last second, or not quite, and Buh will need his own bucket on the gravel.
- Mom shows up at the Plank, perhaps finally playing the four notes, or taking the bucket out of Orlo's hand.
- Brann (who took Ash's bucket, who watches with his sword between them, who saw them enter the hut) switches sides at the critical moment.
- Final image: the floor opens in front of the whole city, the Named come up the ladder, and the census "four" is said again, this time counting Ash.

**4. Interest and next chapter.**
Interest: 8/10. Yes. The Mom scene and the gravel night are the two best sequences in the treatment so far, one emotional and one purely visual, which is exactly the range those films have. But the cliffhanger is the weakest yet: "Named: Tilly" confirms something I predicted in Ch2 (her mom's Cradle, "our family jumps from Forty", Orlo's long look). It's a confirmation, not a reversal. The real cliffhanger is the buried shaft, and the chapter should end on that, or on Ping's torch. This chapter also carries a lot of plot logistics (plan, roles, levers, shears, trapdoor power), and I'm starting to feel that the mechanism is more interesting to the writers than it is to me.

## Chapter 5: Thirty-Three

**1. What I think the story is so far (the ending).**
On Drop Day Tilly escapes her own naming by jumping from Floor 1, so Hollis's house must name another, and he rings the trap for Buh (Tilly lets him, so the city will see it fire). At the Plank, Buh answers the census with "Thirty-three", forces Orlo to show the bucket in public, and gets a full one. The floor opens onto gravel, Buh does the last-tick water trick on the stone beside it, and Rook and the Lows see the open hole. Orlo, whose grandson was born that morning, pays the name himself and steps off into the fog. The thirty dig up to the trapdoor, Buh powers it with a redstone torch, and the Named climb out in front of everyone. Orlo survives in Fen's secret lake ("I couldn't stop your bell. So I dug a lake"). Weeks later a staircase goes down through the fog, the city moves onto the grass, Buh hangs "41" on the first house, and bells ring from other towers in the fog.

**2. Confusion log.**
- "How many in your family?" "Thirty-three." The best line in the treatment. It pays off the census motif (Ch1 "Four", the market "Three, son", the flashback "Four"), it renames the Named as family, and it's the title. HOOKED.
- "Show them it's full." Buh's agency at the Plank is turning the swap public, so Orlo can't palm the bucket. Good. But it means "Don't take his bucket" was never followed (he does take it), and in Ch1 Brann searched Ash and took his own bucket, so Buh couldn't have brought his own anyway. The Ch1 cliffhanger's instruction turns out to be advice the plot quietly drops. CONFUSES.
- The one-tick trick from Forty. Fully set up (Ch1 Floor 6, Ch2 "From Forty you get one tick. Sometimes none"), and executed with skill, not luck. Earned. HOOKED.
- Geometry: Ash's pickaxe "rings on iron: the underside of the trapdoor", yet a moment later the floor under the Well opens "onto gravel". So the ladder shaft and the Well shaft are separate columns? I couldn't picture the Needle's insides from Ch2 ("Beside the ladder, in the dark under the Well"). ANNOYED.
- Orlo "steps off, wide of the Needle, into the fog", then "surfaces in the lake, alive". In Ch3, "Cass jumped wide" and there is a grave. If jumping wide kills, Orlo should be dead; if Fen's lake catches wide jumpers, Cass's grave is unexplained (or the lake came after Cass, which the film doesn't say). CONFUSES, and it cheapens the sacrifice (below).
- Orlo "believes the fog kills". Then what did he think the hole under the Well was for? Disposing of the body so the cap stays clean? Plausible, but never stated, and it's the moral hinge of his character (a murderer who thought he was a murderer). CONFUSES.
- "Parents can't give themselves", but a grandparent can. A satisfying loophole that gives Orlo's sacrifice a rule-based logic. HOOKED. It would land harder if anyone had mentioned grandparents earlier.
- Brann. Introduced in the cold open taking Ash's bucket, assigned as Buh's minder, watching from the stairs, reporting the hut, "Brann's sword between them", and he ends with Rook "shouldering him aside". Set up for a turn that never comes. ANNOYED.
- New names in the last chapter: Sallow the crier. ANNOYED (minor).
- Fen in a locked room "hears her voice", and Tilly frees him "in the chaos" off screen. Convenient. ANNOYED.
- The epilogue. The Keepers "stand aside"; a staircase "grows"; "every floor walks down into the sun"; Ping brings the cake. Thirty years of parents naming their own children, and thirty Named meeting the parents who gave them up, is resolved by one hug ("I named you." "I made you."). Wren ("I'm not climbing back up to say thank you") gets no reckoning at all. ANNOYED, edging to LEAVE for this persona: this is exactly where it turns into the kids' movie the makers say it isn't.
- The button: other towers in the fog, and DONG. DONG. "That's not an echo." It pays off Ch1's lone bell and Fen's "Echo" lie. A good final image, but also a franchise hook. HOOKED.

**3. Predictions (where it leaves me).**
- The bigger story is a world of towers, each running the same full-tower arithmetic, each with or without its own Fen. A second film would be "build out" vs other towers' Highkeepers.
- Who built the first tower, why everyone believes the fog kills, and why the system started thirty years ago are left for later, maybe deliberately.

**4. Interest and the ending.**
Interest: 7/10 for this chapter. The climax is cleanly engineered: five locations cross-cut, every plant (bucket, one tick, redstone torch, gravel trick, "let him", the second toll, the curtain) fires, and Mom opening the curtain as Buh falls is the right image. Then the film loses its nerve twice. Orlo's sacrifice is undone ten minutes later, and the society's complicity is dissolved in a montage. The Prestige ends with a tank of drowned men. Snowpiercer ends with nearly everyone dead and two kids in the snow. The Truman Show ends at a door, with the cost unseen. This ends with cake. As a series button, the other towers' bells are strong enough that I'd watch a sequel.

# Final report (written after reading bible.md)

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend

There's a city in a single stone tower above a sea of fog, and it believes it's full: forty floors, and the game itself won't let you build a forty-first. Each house may raise two kids. A third needs a permit called the Cradle, and the price, kept secret, is that the house names one of its own children, who must make the coming-of-age jump from the top floor. The Highkeeper hands that child an empty bucket, a second bell rolls down the tower, the pool they're aiming for is drained, and a hole opens under it. Everyone thinks the fog eats them. Three years ago Buh's mother had to name either Buh or his older brother Ash; Ash made her say his name. Now Buh, about to jump, gets messages from Ash, follows them down the hole, and finds that the old engineer who built the rig secretly dug a lake at the bottom: thirty "dead" kids are alive down there in a green world. The Highkeeper's son learns about the lake and buries it in gravel, then rigs the trap for Buh to save his own daughter. On Drop Day Buh forces the Highkeeper to show the bucket in public, survives the open hole with a last-second water trick, and the thirty dig themselves out in front of everyone. The old Highkeeper jumps in a child's place, and survives. Everyone moves down onto the grass, and in the distance other towers are ringing.

## Did the chapters deliver what the bible intends?

The plot and the clue ladder came through remarkably intact: nearly every plant/test/payoff row in the bible landed for me, in order. What didn't land is the thing the bible calls **the one idea**, and a layer of character secrets. Everything below did **not** come across from the chapters alone:

1. **The one idea: "build limit is the only rule nobody made; the shortage is artificial because the Edge law keeps it so."** I only got this retroactively from the very last line ("We build out now. Not up."). Nobody in the film ever argues or discovers that building out would solve the shortage. The thesis lives in slogans, not in a scene.
2. **Kit's block versus Buh's roof block.** The bible's whole argument is the contrast between the block the game allows (past the Edge, broken by a Keeper) and the one it refuses (on the roof). I registered them as two separate moments a chapter apart, never as a pair.
3. **"Then who made the rest?"** means "who made the rest of the rules". I read it as "who built the rest of the tower / the empty floors". The key middle rung of the thesis ladder failed.
4. **"There's always room on Forty" / "Half of Forty's empty" mean the tower isn't actually full.** I read "always room on Forty" as a death joke (the Named die from there) and "Half of Forty's empty" as a hint about the Named. The Highs-hoard-space meaning didn't come through.
5. **The Drop happens at sixteen.** Never stated. I had to work out Buh's age from Ash being 19, and "You'd have spent three years knowing" only makes sense if you know the jump age.
6. **The Cradle is "celebrated".** Never shown. A celebration scene would make the hidden price far crueller.
7. **Fen built the rig.** Not in the chapters. I assumed the Highs built it and Fen was a caretaker.
8. **Wren is Fen's daughter, and Fen dug the lake the year she was named.** Completely absent, and it's the best character secret in the bible: the engineer who built the machine that took his daughter, and secretly caught her. Fen climbing down "with Wren" in Ch5 reads as two side characters, not a father and daughter.
9. **Cass is Jory's brother.** Wren's lines sit next to each other ("his house named his brother" / "Cass jumped wide"), but I didn't connect them on first read. Jory's silence and "Pass me a torch" lose most of their weight.
10. **Teo is Hollis's son.** Teo appears once, holding a hand, and is never mentioned again. So Hollis's motive (if Tilly escapes, someone in his house still pays) never becomes stakes. Hollis reads as a snob ("Nobody misses a Low") rather than a frightened father.
11. **Hollis is Orlo's son and Tilly's father.** I pieced it together from "her father" plus Orlo "looks at his son", but no line or shot states it, and Hollis's name and "her father" are never on screen together.
12. **Brann reports to Hollis.** Only implied by Brann watching the hut. He also has no real payoff.
13. **Rook is Buh's rival and wants Forty "for his family of three".** He reads as a gang friend, and "what Forty is paid with" / "My family's moving to Forty" didn't decode for me.
14. **Orlo believes the fog kills, so for him the hole is for hiding bodies.** Ch5 states that he believes it, but the chapters never tell me why the rig needs a hole if it's meant to kill. Without that, his moral position is fuzzy.
15. **Buh's want: "to jump from Forty like Ash and get Mom and Pip above the clouds".** Barely dramatized. His Ch1 line is "I'll jump from forty-one", Mom says "He's jumping from Ten", and Buh never says out loud that he wants Forty, which is also why Rook's prank motive doesn't land.
16. **Ping is Ash's allay, kept by Fen.** The allay is unnamed until Ch4, and its relationship to Fen's hut ("asleep in the hut, unseen") is invisible.
17. **The Needle's 2x1 shaft (ladder column vs drop column).** Not legible. In Ch5 I couldn't tell why Ash hits the trapdoor while the Well's floor opens onto gravel.
18. **Lever two's slow line runs through a tripwire on 40, so shears disarm it.** I only understood the shears because the treatment explains them in a stage direction. A viewer wouldn't.
19. **Left open on purpose: "Do Mom, Fen and Orlo deserve forgiveness? The movie doesn't decide."** The epilogue does decide: hug, sunlight, cake, Orlo laughing in the lake. The intended ambiguity is overwritten by the tone.

## Unanswered questions

**GOOD AMBIGUITY (people would argue in the comments):**
- Who cut the string. The shears point to Orlo; nobody says it.
- Would Orlo have handed Buh the empty bucket? The hovering hand is exactly right.
- Did Ash lie when he said "I'll hear it from the top"? Strong, if the cold open is directed so it can be argued (see holes).
- Did Tilly already know, or suspect, she was the Named before she found the page? ("never a joke")
- The red *Height limit for building is 320* text. Do these people know they live inside a rule-system? It's the most Truman Show idea in the piece, and I'd keep it uncommented.
- The other towers. Do they have a Fen, or do their Named really die?

**CONFUSING (reads like a mistake):**
- "The house names one of its own ... or the house names another." Another of its own, or anyone's child? (Breaks the moral core; see holes.)
- Orlo jumps "wide" and lives; Cass "jumped wide" and has a grave.
- Why the rig has a hole at all if the Highs believe the fog kills.
- The permit FLOORS 1 TO 39: is Orlo protecting Buh or setting him up?
- "Then there's nobody left to name": why does everyone coming up end the naming?
- "What Forty is paid with" / Rook's "family of three".
- How Jory got back down after climbing up and being "replaced" by his brother.
- Why in thirty years nobody (High, Low or Named) has gone down the tower's own base into fog that is only about 30 blocks deep, when the tower must stand on that ground.
- Is Orlo's jump legal (a loophole: grandparents may give themselves) or defiance? If it's a loophole, every house with a grandparent had a way out for thirty years.

## Best 3 moments
1. **The census flashback (Ch4) and "Mom named me" (Ch3).** "Take me." "Parents can't give themselves." "Write me" from behind the curtain; Orlo looks only at Mom "because the house has to say it". A whole society's complicity in four lines. Then Mom alone at the note block, unable to play. This is the scene that earns the comparison the makers want.
2. **The cold open.** The swap in plain sight, the hotbar irony, "The floor I jump from is the floor I live on", and the note-block song intercut with the fall. Mirrored in Ch5 by "Show them it's full" and Mom opening the curtain as Buh falls. Rigorous, rhyming, cinematic.
3. **"How many in your family?" "Thirty-three."** The census motif's last turn, the title, and a re-definition of family in one line. (Close runner-up: Hollis's silent gravel night, with the thirty watching their way out rain down into their lake.)

## Worst 3 moments
1. **Orlo's survival plus the "weeks later" montage.** The one real sacrifice is refunded ten minutes later, then "every floor walks down into the sun" and the allay brings cake. Thirty years of parents naming their children dissolves without a single reckoning. This is where it becomes a kids' movie.
2. **The Ch2 heist on Forty.** Everything the plot needs, in one room each, in one pass, with the incriminating page torn out next to an incriminating note. The film's mystery architecture is otherwise careful; here it's a scavenger hunt.
3. **Wren delivering the central law between zombies, "as if he should already know it".** The most important rule in the film arrives as a speech in a fight. It's exposition with a sword in its hand, followed immediately by a second exposition scene (the model).

## Persona section (cinephile: Interstellar, The Prestige, Arrival, The Truman Show, Snowpiercer)

**Does it work for someone who doesn't play Minecraft?** Mostly, and better than I expected. The water-bucket clutch is taught visually in Ch1 and pays off in Ch5, and the gravel/torch trick is taught with a character reaction. Those two show how to do it. But the climaxes lean on mechanics I had to guess:
- the hotbar icon (plain bucket vs water bucket) as the cold open's whole tell;
- that a dispenser can drink water as well as pour it;
- what a sticky piston does;
- tripwire plus shears (I only understood it because the treatment explains it in a stage direction);
- that iron trapdoors need power and a redstone torch provides it (the hatch opening reads as magic);
- that allays fetch items to note blocks, and what a lodestone compass is;
- that ladders stop a fall;
- why a dropped cobblestone "never lands" (no sound);
- that hay bales reduce damage;
- that the red 320 text is the game itself talking.

Each mechanism a climax turns on needs one earlier, wordless demonstration with someone reacting to it, the way Moss's torch trick works.

**Is the central idea one idea or several?** One on paper, three on screen:
- a family-arithmetic sacrifice ("the Cradle costs a name"; The Lottery by way of Arrival);
- a rigged ritual with a hidden mechanism (The Prestige);
- a closed world whose boundary is a lie ("build out, not up"; The Truman Show and Snowpiercer).

The bible says the third is the one idea and the others are its faces. But the film puts all its emotion into the first and all its plot into the second, and gives the third only slogans: a chant, a taunt, a sign. To make it one idea, the climax's revelation has to be "there is room" (the scarcity is a choice), not only "the Named are alive".

**Do the twists feel earned and fair on a second viewing?** Mostly yes, unusually so for the form. "Keeps it topped up", "Echo", "Don't look", "We were lucky", "And look where it got him", "Grandpa says our family jumps from Forty" and the Floor 1 joke are all Prestige-grade plants: true, in plain sight, and worse on re-watch. The unfair ones:
- the cold-open "he doesn't [see]" for a boy who knew (hole #3);
- the tag whose advice the climax ignores (#4);
- the Ch2 hook whose answer is a prank (#5);
- Orlo's survival against Cass's grave (#2);
- the "names another" rule, which the plot uses both ways (#1).

**Is the emotional core strong enough that you'd care without the mystery?** The Mom–Ash–Buh triangle is, potentially. It's this film's Interstellar: a child furious at a parent who made an impossible trade to save the family. But the film doesn't trust it. Mom has about four short scenes. Her confrontation with Buh is one scene, and her reckoning is one hug and a glance ("no Floor 10 now"). Buh himself is mostly a detective engine; his want (Forty, like Ash) is barely voiced, and his real question (can I forgive her?) is never asked out loud. Without the mystery, I'd care about Mom, not Buh. Interstellar earns its reunion with decades of Murph's anger. This spends twelve seconds.

**Which moments are cheap, sentimental or convenient?**
- Orlo surfacing alive and laughing-or-crying in the lake.
- The staircase "grows" and the Keepers simply "stand aside": the Highs' power just evaporates.
- Ping bringing the cake to Pip's birthday.
- "Every floor walks down into the sun."
- Hollis's only consequence is standing alone.
- Wren's exposition between zombies.
- The one-pass heist.
- Tilly overhearing her father and then finding the page.
- Fen "hears her voice" and is freed off screen "in the chaos".
- Rook's instant turn ("What do you need?").
- Brann set up for a turn that never comes.
- "Took you long enough, Buh" twice. It's cute, which is the problem.

**Does the darkness feel purposeful or gratuitous?** Purposeful, never gratuitous. The problem is the opposite: the darkness is refunded. The cull turns out to be an exile, the one on-screen sacrifice turns out to be a swim, the only death (Cass) is a sign on a wall, and thirty sets of parents who named their own children are never seen. Hollis tries to have a child killed for being poor and ends up merely lonely. In Snowpiercer the children in the engine room stay in the engine room; in The Prestige the tanks are full. Here the premise is darker than the film is willing to be. Acts 1 and 2 earn the comparison; the last ten minutes give it back.

**The single change that would move it closest to the films I love: make the ending cost something.** Cut "Fen's lake" (Orlo's fall stays real, or at least unresolved) and cut the "weeks later" montage. Spend those two minutes on one scene: the thirty walking up the walkway into the crowd of the houses that named them. Wren faces her father. Some parents step forward and some don't, and Mom says "I named you" to Ash in front of the city, not in private. Keep the 41 sign and the distant bells as the coda. The Truman Show ends at a door; this should end at that walkway.

## Top 5 changes (most important first)

1. **Make the naming rule unambiguous: one of the house's own children, full stop.** Hollis's "Name the Buh boy" becomes an explicit forgery (the torn page) and a class crime ("Nobody misses a Low"), with Teo as his reason. Without this, Mom's choice, the core of the film, is logically optional.
2. **Let the ending charge a price.** Orlo's sacrifice stays a sacrifice (cut his lake survival, which also removes the Cass contradiction). Replace the cake-and-staircase montage with the Named meeting their namers in public, and have Wren and Fen meet as daughter and father. Let "do they deserve forgiveness" stay genuinely open, as the bible wants.
3. **Dramatize the one idea in the middle, not just at the end.** Put Kit's block and the roof refusal side by side. In Ch2, at night, Buh places a block past the Edge and it holds; minutes later the roof refuses his. Rewrite "Then who made the rest?" as "Then who made the rest of the rules?", and have Tilly's "Half of Forty's empty" get the answer "Then the tower isn't full." Now the climax can expose a lie about room, not just a trapdoor.
4. **Fix the three unfair plants.**
   - Change the tag to "Make him show you the water", so it pays off "Show them it's full".
   - Direct Ash's cold-open hotbar beat as a refusal to look, not a failure to notice.
   - Give Buh an early, spoken want to jump from Forty, so Rook's emptied bucket has a motive, and cut the tag reference from the Ch2 hook.
5. **Make the machinery legible to non-players, and cut the noun load.**
   - Before each mechanism matters, give it one wordless demo with a reaction: the dispenser drinking, the trapdoor that won't open by hand, the redstone torch, the tripwire and shears, the two columns of the shaft. State "At sixteen" in the chant.
   - Name Ping in Ch1.
   - Fold Hollis/Teo into one clear "Orlo's son and his kids" shot.
   - Say outright that Cass was Jory's brother and that Wren is Fen's daughter.
   - Drop late new names (Sallow).
   - Give Brann a choice on the cap, or cut him to a function.
