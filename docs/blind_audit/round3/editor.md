## Chapter 1: You Fall Once (log written before opening Chapter 2)

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Highfall is one stone tower of forty floors over a fog sea. At a coming-of-age jump you step off a floor holding a single bucket the Highkeeper hands you, try to land with water, and the floor you jumped from becomes your home floor forever. Three years ago the Highkeeper swapped Ash's full bucket for an empty one and Ash fell from Forty. Now his brother Buh (13) is six days from his own jump. An allay that answers Buh's nightly note-block song brings a compass pointing at the Needle, and then a name tag signed the way only Ash wrote his B's: "Don't take his bucket."

**2. Confusion log.**
- "Keeper Brann pats Ash down and takes his own bucket." For a beat I read "his own" as Brann's own. Rewrite it as "takes Ash's bucket". ANNOYED.
- The Well and the bucket. The Well is a blue dot of water in the middle of the Needle's cap, so if you hit it, why do you need a bucket? Is the Well the target and the bucket only a backup, or is the bucket the whole test? If Ash hit the Well, the empty bucket wouldn't kill him. I can't tell yet what the rules of a successful jump are, and the whole opening murder depends on that. LOST (low-grade, but it's the central mechanic).
- Two tolls: the first rings every bell at once, the second rolls down the tower bell by bell. I don't know what either one means. HOOKED.
- From Floor 3, the Well's blue square "goes dark for one frame". Something covered it or drained it, or Ash went into it. HOOKED.
- "Two to a house", the census question "How many in your family?", "Pip was a Cradle baby. We were lucky", and Tilly's mom "got the Cradle" with the baby due on Drop Day. I'm piecing together a birth licence and a population cap, but I'm guessing. HOOKED (the good kind of guessing).
- "Two to a house", yet Mom's household is Mom, Pip and Buh (four with Ash). Is that a contradiction, or does it mean two children? ANNOYED.
- The census at Mom's stall. Buh says "Four"; Orlo says "Three, son" and writes. Writes three, or writes four? It echoes the hook's "How many in your family?" "Four." so it reads as Buh being marked. HOOKED.
- A single bell rings out in the fog when nobody is jumping. HOOKED.
- Hollis and little Teo arrive in a list behind Orlo and do nothing. They're names to remember with no reason yet. ANNOYED.
- Too many names for one 12-minute video: Ash, Buh, Brann, Orlo, Mom, Pip, Juno, Rook, Kit, Hollis, Teo, Tilly (12). Only Rook and Tilly have a voice. Juno and Kit are interchangeable. ANNOYED.
- The cap is "at Floor 1's height", so a jump from Floor 1 is a fall of nothing. Tilly's plan to jump from Floor 1 reads either as a brilliant dodge (no fall, so no bucket can kill you) or as a joke I'm not getting. HOOKED.

**3. Predictions.**
- Forty's "one a year" isn't a curse. The Highkeeper culls one jumper a year to keep a full tower full, and the Cradle hands out a birth in exchange for a death. Ash died (or was meant to) to make room for Pip. That's the true meaning of "How many in your family?" and "There's always room on Forty."
- Tilly (Floor 40, mother expecting a Cradle baby on Drop Day) is this year's target. Her Floor 1 plan is a dodge, and Orlo will try to stop it. Buh's "Four" at the census may have put him on the list too.
- Ash is alive. He hit the Well or went through it (the one dark frame) and is inside the Needle or somewhere below the fog, sending allays. The lone bell in the fog is someone down there. "The Below keeps what it takes" is a lie that keeps people from looking.
- The title pays off with Buh's "Then I'll jump from forty-one." In the climax he builds above the top (or proves "build limit" is a lie) and makes a jump nobody can rig.
- Hollis is Orlo's successor or enforcer and may turn. Teo matters later, maybe as a hostage, a Cradle child, or the next to be culled.

**4. Interest and the cliffhanger.**
Interest: 8/10. Would I click Chapter 2? Yes. The cold open is a murder done with a hotbar icon: the audience sees a plain bucket and Ash doesn't. That's the most Minecraft-native dramatic irony I've seen in machinima, and it lands inside the first 30 seconds (pat-down, two buckets, "How many in your family?", the swap). The cliffhanger is strong (8/10): it pays off a setup ("8UH'S ROOM", "Eights are cooler") planted eleven minutes earlier and asks a clean question: is Ash alive? One caveat: it only tells Buh what we already know, so its power rests on "Ash is alive", not on any new information about the bucket.

Editor's running notes, Chapter 1:
- Minute marks (from the treatment's timings): 0:00 hook / 1:00 fall intercut / 2:30 window and TITLE / 3:00 establishing wide / 3:40 practice ledge / 6:10 market / 8:10 stall / 9:10 Tilly / 11:10 Buh's room / ~11:40 name tag. The sum comes to about 12 minutes, so it fits.
- The six-law chant at the market is a list. It's in-world ritual, which helps, but six laws in a row is a lecture with a beat under it. Cut it to the four laws that matter and let the other two come out in scenes.
- The practice-ledge scene (2.5 min) is well done. It shows Buh's skill, delivers the rules as teasing, and gives the theme ("forty-one"). Kit's fine and Juno's cobblestone each do one job, and both are good.
- Hero agency: Buh is skilled and has a want (to beat Ash, to jump higher than the top) but does nothing to cause events yet. The allay comes to him. That's acceptable for a Chapter 1, but Chapter 2 needs him to act.
- The clock is clear: six days to Drop Day.

## Chapter 2: The Seventeenth Bucket (log written before opening Chapter 3)

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
The deaths from Forty aren't accidents. A ledger in the Highkeeper's study records each year's "+1 (Cradle)" birth next to a "Named:" child, so one child is picked for every licensed birth and handed the spare, empty seventeenth bucket. Orlo is Tilly's grandfather, this year's page is torn out, and Buh believes he's the one named. The Needle is rigged with a dispenser, a sticky piston under the Well, levers and a bell rope, and the Named don't die: they drop through the Needle into a green world under the fog, where Ash (19) is alive and waiting.

**2. Confusion log.**
- The hook: someone emptied Buh's practice bucket off Floor 12. Who, and why? A Keeper trying to kill him, a warning from Ash's side, or Orlo scaring him? Nobody in the chapter asks. It's a strong 20-second hook that the chapter forgets. ANNOYED, close to LOST.
- The permit, PERMIT: FLOORS 1 TO 39. If the Named always go from Forty and Orlo is naming Buh, why ban him from Forty? Is it a practice permit only, reverse psychology, or Orlo trying to save him? Then Buh says "It's me this year", which seems to contradict the permit. LOST.
- The Well mechanics, continued. A dispenser "keeps it topped up" (or empties it, since a dispenser holding a bucket can scoop water), and a sticky piston holds the stone under the Well. My best guess: they scoop the water, retract the stone, and the Named drop through a hole into the shaft. But then why give them an empty bucket? I still haven't seen what a normal, successful jump looks like. LOST (this is now the second chapter where the central mechanic is inferred, not shown).
- "It rings twice for the one." "Echo." It pays off the Chapter 1 second toll and the bell rope in the lever room: the second toll is rung by hand, probably to cover the piston. HOOKED, and well done.
- The ledger. "Marla's house. +1 (Pip). Named: Ash." That's a gut-punch, and it gives Mom a name. The page torn out plus the margin note in a different hand ("the Buh boy asks too many questions") is a great double clue. HOOKED.
- "Half of Forty's empty." "Then what are they counting?" If the tower isn't full, the population reason for the cull is a lie. HOOKED.
- "Then who made the rest?" (on the roof, looking at Forty's dark windows). I don't understand the question. Who made the rest of what: the empty rooms, the floors, the world? It's meant as a big thematic line and I lost it. LOST.
- "Height limit for building is 320." Using the real game message is a lovely touch. But it seems to shut the door on "forty-one" for good, so I don't know where the title is heading now. HOOKED.
- Orlo is Tilly's grandfather. It lands at the right moment, but Tilly told us in Chapter 1 that "Grandpa says our family jumps from Forty", so attentive viewers will get there first. That's a fair-play reveal, which is good.
- Ch1 had a cobblestone that "never lands" in the fog, yet there's a green world with a lake under it. You can explain it (the fog is thick, the sound doesn't carry), but the comments will ask. ANNOYED (nitpick).
- How long is the climb down the Needle? "Ten seconds of white" works as a transition. I'm only noting that the trip below seems easy for something nobody in thirty years has found. ANNOYED.

**3. Predictions.**
- This year's torn page says "Named: Tilly". Her mother got the Cradle, so the rule demands a child from her house, and it's Orlo's own family. Orlo (or whoever wrote the margin note, Hollis I'd bet) wants to name Buh in her place. That's the engine for Chapters 3-4: rule against grandfather.
- The cull was never murder. It's exile, carried out by Fen the Wellwright, with the city told they're dead. Either Orlo knows and the Below is part of the founders' design, or Fen has been secretly saving them against Orlo's intent. I lean toward Fen saving them in secret: the sack, the sleeping allay in his hut, and the fact that he chased the kids off.
- The thirty Named (one a year, never two, never none) are the "people" at the campfires. Ash has been working with Fen and sending the allays.
- "Took you long enough" puts Ash's motives in question. Either he wants Buh down here before Drop Day, or he has a plan that needs Buh above (to stop the system, open the Needle, or bring everyone down).
- The climax will turn on getting above the build limit without building, with a wind charge or something like it: "forty-one" as a height you reach, not a floor you build.

**4. Interest and the cliffhanger.**
Interest: 9/10. Would I click Chapter 3? Instantly. The cliffhanger is very strong (9/10): a white-out reveal of a new world, plus the line from the dead brother. As an editor I'll note what it costs. It answers the Chapter 1 cliffhanger completely, at about 24 minutes (39% of 62), which is where an Act 1 break lands. That means Chapters 3-5 need a new engine, which the Tilly/Named question can provide if it's made explicit.

Editor's running notes, Chapter 2:
- Minute marks: 12:00 hook / 12:30 permit / 13:30 compass and Needle cap / 15:00 Fen / 16:30 training / 17:30 heist on Forty / 21:00 roof / 22:30 Fen enters the Needle / ~23:30 white-out and Ash.
- The heist is overloaded: five reveals in 3.5 minutes (lever room, ledger, torn page and margin hand, seventeenth bucket, grandfather). Each one is good. Together they won't breathe. Move the lever room to Fen's scene or to the night beat, and give the ledger and the bucket count room.
- The training minute is thin. "From Forty you get one tick" is the only new thing in it. Fold the line into the roof scene or the heist walk.
- Hero agency is much better. Buh slips his minder, follows the compass, breaks into the study and climbs into the Needle alone. Tilly is a real partner: she counts, she reads the room.
- Exposition by object (tally marks, ledger, bucket chest, game message) is excellent and exactly right for machinima.

## Chapter 3: The Below (log written before opening Chapter 4)

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
The Below is a camp of the thirty Named and the children born there, led in spirit by Wren, the first of them, who won't go back up. Buh works out the whole rig: on Forty, two levers send a slow signal down the tower, the dispenser "drinks" the Well, and the piston opens a hole the Named drop through, holding the seventeenth, empty bucket. Only Forty is high enough for the signal to arrive in time. The rule, which belongs to the city and not to Orlo: two children is the Count, a third needs the Cradle, and the Cradle costs a name, which the house gives from its own children. Anyone who comes back up dooms a sibling. And Ash's last line: "Mom named me."

**2. Confusion log.**
- Wren's rule: "...the one jumps from Forty, or the house names another." Another what? Another of its own children (as with Jory's brother), or a child from another house? This matters a lot. In Chapter 2 Buh says "It's me this year" and the margin reads "the Buh boy asks too many questions", but under this rule Buh's house had no Cradle this year, so he can't be named unless a house can name someone else's child. Either Buh was wrong in Chapter 2 or the rule has a loophole that nobody has stated. LOST.
- Chapter 1 has the Well dark "for one frame". The Chapter 3 memory has the dispenser drinking, the stone sliding away, Ash dropping and the floor sliding back with the water pouring in, which is seconds, not a frame. One of the two has to change. ANNOYED.
- "This time he remembers past the curtain." Past it how? He saw what happened after Mom drew it, or he remembers Mom knew exactly when to draw it? I think it's the second (Mom's "Don't look" was knowing), and if so it's a lovely plant, but the sentence doesn't land it. ANNOYED/HOOKED.
- The empty bucket's job finally makes sense in my head: the Named aim for the Well, so they fall through the hole, and if they miss they hit the cap with nothing in their hand and die (Cass "jumped wide"), so the secret holds. But nobody ever says the Well is the target and the bucket is the backup. I'm assembling it myself. ANNOYED (it was LOST in Chapters 1 and 2).
- "If the Highs ever find this lake, they'll fill the Needle." So the Highs don't know the Named live? Then why build a hole rather than let them land on the cap? I can answer it (vanishing beats a body in public view, and "The Below keeps what it takes"), but someone on screen should, in one line. And Fen goes down nightly with a sack, so he's the Highs' own Wellwright keeping their secret from them? HOOKED, but it needs to be said once.
- Jory. "Year nine, Jory climbed back up... That spring his house named his brother." This is a superb rule-by-story: one sentence, and it explains why Ash never came home. HOOKED.
- Cass "jumped wide". Who was Cass to Wren: a sister, a daughter, a partner? The palm on the sign does the work, and I don't need more. HOOKED (good ambiguity).
- Moss's gravel-and-torch trick is clearly a plant ("They'll fill the Needle"... with gravel?). That's fine, but 45 seconds of plant with no story attached will read as a plant. ANNOYED.
- The Drop Day clock has vanished. How many days are left? Does anyone up top know Buh is gone: Mom, Brann, Tilly? Twelve minutes with no pressure from above. ANNOYED (structural).

**3. Predictions.**
- This year's Named is Tilly. Her mother named her, Orlo (her grandfather) can't stop it because "he doesn't pick", and the torn page is him hiding it or wrestling with it. The margin hand (Hollis?) wants Buh named instead, by some loophole or a cheat on the rule.
- Buh goes back up despite "Nobody goes up". He isn't Named, so he can, and he'll confront Mom and try to save Tilly on Drop Day.
- His absence or his return exposes Fen, the Highs learn about the lake and try to "fill the Needle" (gravel or sand), and Moss's torch trick is how the Below fights back or keeps the shaft open.
- Mom gets a confession scene: "Don't look" and "Floor 10. Real windows" were guilt.
- The climax is Drop Day on Forty. Somebody (Buh) jumps from "forty-one", above the build limit, with no Keeper's bucket, and the city sees the hole, or the Below, or the lie of "the tower is full."
- Wren will refuse to help until the last moment. Jory will matter (he went up once and knows the way).

**4. Interest and the cliffhanger.**
Interest: 8/10. Would I click Chapter 4? Yes. "Mom named me" is a genuine gut-punch and it reframes three Chapter 1 scenes ("Don't look", "Floor 10", "look where it got him"). But it's pre-empted. At about minute 7 Wren states "The house names one of its own... Parents can't give themselves" and Buh says "Pip was a Cradle baby", so the audience has done the arithmetic five minutes before Buh does, and Buh then asks "So he picked you", which makes our hero slower than the viewer. Cliffhanger strength: 7/10 as written, 9/10 if Wren's line stops at "The Cradle costs a name" and the "house names one of its own" half is saved for Ash.

Editor's running notes, Chapter 3:
- Minute marks: 24:00 reunion / 24:30 camp / 26:00 the model / 28:30 gravel bank / 29:15 night at the wall / 31:15 the grave / 32:45 the fire / ~35:15 "Mom named me."
- This is an information chapter. Buh receives: Wren tells the rule, Wren tells the Jory story, Ash tells the Mom truth. The A-plot (Drop Day, who's named, Tilly) doesn't move for twelve minutes, and Buh has no new goal at the end, only a wound. Serialised animation can survive one of these, but only if the chapter ends with a want: "I'm going back up."
- The model scene is the best exposition device in the treatment so far. The hero deduces, the brother confirms in two words, and it rewards attentive viewers. Keep it. It does repeat the ledger and bucket-chest information, so trim the call-and-response to four exchanges.
- The wall scene is overloaded and reads as a lecture. A five-sentence legal code delivered between zombies doesn't stop being a lecture because there are zombies in it. Split it: "Two is the Count" can come from Moss (a child born below repeating a rule he's never lived under), and the cost line from Ash at the fire.
- The camp is thin: thirty people, three names, one line each. Jory "doesn't look up" and then becomes a Wren anecdote. Give Jory one line himself.

## Chapter 4: One In, One Out (log written before opening Chapter 5)

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
Ash volunteered ("Write me"), and Mom said his name because the house has to say it, which spared Buh three years of knowing. Mom won't believe Ash is alive. Rook confesses he emptied Buh's practice bucket to scare him off Forty and joins up. Tilly's father, cheating the rule, means to name Buh ("Nobody misses a Low"), so Buh plans to let the whole city watch the floor open while the thirty climb back up together: "Then there's nobody left to name." Then the plan falls apart. Hollis takes Fen and the lever, sees firelight down the shaft and has the Needle filled with gravel overnight, so tomorrow's hole opens onto gravel and the thirty are sealed in. The allay drops a torch ("They're digging"). Orlo sits with shears over a page that reads "Named: Tilly" in his own hand.

**2. Confusion log.**
- Who is Tilly's father? The chapter says "her father" twice and never names him. I'm inferring it's Hollis (he's always at Orlo's shoulder, little Teo would then be Tilly's brother, the "different hand" in the margin would be his, and he's the one who fills the Needle). If I'm right, it's the most important family fact in the story and it's never said on screen. LOST.
- "You were thirteen. You'd have spent three years knowing." Buh is 13 now. Three years ago he was 10. The line has the wrong number, or it means "you'd have been thirteen at your jump", which isn't what it says. It also raises the jump age: Ash jumped at 16, Buh jumps at 13. Is there a fixed age? Do the Named jump at the next Drop Day whatever their age? ANNOYED (CONFUSES).
- Orlo and the shears. "Tripwire string can be cut with shears without setting it off" is the treatment explaining a mechanic to me, the reader. Nothing on screen teaches the audience that rule, so the beat plays as an old man holding shears. And what's he going to cut, and why? If the line doesn't fire, the floor doesn't open, and a Named jumper with an empty bucket hits the cap. Is he trying to save Tilly, or to stop the hole now that it's full of gravel? (In story order he picks up the shears before the gravel.) LOST (the intent), HOOKED (the image).
- Hollis sees firelight on water and "says nothing", then fills the Needle. Is he killing the camp, sealing it, or turning the hole into a guaranteed death for Buh? All three, I think, and that works. But I'd like one line that tells me which he wants most. HOOKED.
- Rook confessing he emptied the bucket closes the Chapter 2 hook. That's a good payoff, at last. But why would Rook (whose family is moving to Forty) want Buh off Forty so badly that he'd risk Buh's life? "To scare you off Forty" needs a reason: what does Rook know? ANNOYED.
- Wren goes from "votes no" to "All of us" in two lines. What turned her? Ash's "Then I go alone" echoes Jory, which is the right lever, but on the page it's a switch, not a turn. ANNOYED.
- The thirty "wait on the ladder all night", then the ladder is buried under gravel. Were they on it? Were they hurt? ANNOYED.
- At dawn the Keepers shut the trapdoor, "and with no lever it has no power." I follow that the iron trapdoor can't be opened from above. Does that also stop the Well's piston? Which lever did Hollis take: the hatch's, or one of the two on Forty? ANNOYED.
- "Named: Tilly... Her 'Floor 1. Imagine his face' was never a joke." So Tilly suspected all along? Then in Chapter 2, when Buh said "It's me this year", she let him believe it. Is that a secret she keeps, or a fact she learns here? The cliffhanger says both. ANNOYED.
- It's also a cliffhanger the audience has seen coming. The ledger was "open at Tilly's name" in scene 4, four minutes earlier, and "Name the Buh boy" already told us the swap. The viewer is ahead of the reveal again. ANNOYED (structural).

**3. Predictions.**
- Drop Day. Buh refuses the Keeper's bucket ("Don't take his bucket" pays off), or takes the empty one knowingly in front of the witnesses.
- Orlo cuts the line, so the dispenser never drinks the Well and the floor never opens onto the gravel. Buh lands in the full Well (or uses his own water) in front of the packed walkway with an empty Keeper's bucket in his hand: the swap exposed in public.
- The thirty dig up through the gravel with Moss's torch trick, slowed by the stone layers, and come out of the hatch or the Well during the Drop. Mom sees Ash.
- Hollis is the final antagonist. Orlo breaks or confesses, maybe taking Tilly's place ("Parents can't give themselves", but a grandparent?). Brann turns.
- Tilly jumps from Floor 1 or doesn't jump. The Count itself is broken because half of Forty is empty. The title lands when someone jumps from above Forty (the roof, "forty-one"), a floor that belongs to no one.

**4. Interest and the cliffhanger.**
Interest: 8/10. Would I click Chapter 5? Yes, but on the strength of the gravel sequence, not the cliffhanger. The real cliffhanger is "the floor under the Well will open onto gravel" plus the torch, "They're digging". It sits two scenes before the end, and the chapter closes on a reveal the audience already holds. Cliffhanger strength: 5/10 as written, 8/10 if the order flips (Tilly's page at about 45 minutes, gravel and torch last) or if the scene with Orlo alone over the ledger doesn't show the name.

## Chapter 5: Thirty-Three (log written before opening bible.md)

**1. What I think the story is so far.**
On Drop Day, Tilly dodges her naming by jumping from Floor 1, which puts her father's house a name short. Orlo had cut the line, her father bridges it, and Tilly lets him, so the hole will open in front of the whole city. At the Plank, Buh answers "Thirty-three", dares Orlo to "Show them it's full", and gets the full bucket. The floor opens onto gravel, and Buh pulls off his Floor 6 trick from Forty on the stone beside it. Orlo, whose grandson was born that morning, pays his own house's name by stepping into the fog, believing it kills. Buh powers the dead hatch with a redstone torch and the thirty climb out in front of everyone. Mom gets Ash back, Orlo surfaces alive in the lake Fen secretly dug, the city walks down to the grass, and in the far distance other towers ring twice.

**2. Confusion log.**
- Orlo's plan on Drop Day. He cut the line with the shears, so no floor should open. Then why does he hold two buckets, make an offer ("Take the bucket, jump, and it's done"), and let his hand hover? If the line is cut and he hands Buh the empty one, Buh hits the cap and dies in public, which contradicts "Not this year." I can't tell whether Orlo meant to spare Buh, kill him, or didn't know what he'd do. The climax pivots on his hand, and his intent is unreadable. LOST.
- Tilly in the lever room: "Do it, then. Everyone's watching." Does she know the Needle is full of gravel? If she does, she's letting her father ring Buh into a gravel floor, and Buh hasn't got the full bucket yet. That's a dark choice for a heroine, and the treatment doesn't seem to know it's dark. LOST.
- Buh's plan after the gravel. The Chapter 4 plan ("If the floor opens, just drop") died with the gravel, and nobody makes a new one. He climbs the stairs and survives because Orlo tips the water. "Show them it's full" is a clever forcing move, but what would Buh have done with the empty one? ANNOYED (structural, see the persona notes).
- "Cass jumped wide" and has a grave, but Orlo "steps off, wide of the Needle, into the fog" and surfaces in Fen's lake. What's the geometry? If "wide" means missing the hole and hitting the cap, say "Cass hit the cap". As written, the same word kills one and saves the other. CONFUSES.
- Mom's turn. In Chapter 4 it's "Don't. Go to bed" and "Floor 10, or you don't jump". In the Chapter 5 hook she "kneels at the note block for the first time in three years". What changed her mind overnight? ANNOYED.
- "My grandson was born this morning." This pays off Tilly's "the baby is due on Drop Day" from Chapter 1. It's a tidy fair-play payoff, and it explains why Orlo jumps. HOOKED. But "Parents can't give themselves", so is a grandparent allowed? He seems to be breaking the rule and paying it at once. A single line ("I'm not his father. I'm the house.") would make it a choice rather than a loophole. ANNOYED.
- "Fen, freed by Tilly in the chaos on Forty" is told in a subordinate clause. It's fine in a treatment, but on screen it needs two seconds. ANNOYED.
- Sallow the crier: a new name in the last chapter. Cut the name. ANNOYED.
- The far towers. Thirty people lived under the fog for up to thirty years and never noticed another tower's base? "Far away" covers it, mostly. NITPICK.
- Teo never pays off (I assumed he was Tilly's brother and next in line). Hollis's only real ending is "stands alone on Forty". ANNOYED.
- "That's not an echo." This pays off Fen's "Echo" (Chapter 2) and the lone bell in the fog (Chapter 1). Excellent. HOOKED.

**3. Predictions (looking ahead to the bible).**
- I expect the bible to say Hollis is Tilly's father and Orlo's son, that Teo is Tilly's brother, that the Count started thirty years ago under Orlo, and that Fen built the rig and dug the lake. I expect it to give Orlo a clear Drop-Day intention, a rule on what "jumped wide" means, and a reason for Rook's sabotage.

**4. Interest and the ending.**
Interest: 8/10. As an ending it satisfies. The hero lands his own trick from Ch1 (the Floor 6 MLG) at the moment that matters, opens the hatch with a Minecraft-native fix (a redstone torch), and calls the city to look. Mom opens the curtain. Orlo meets the lake. The series button (the 41 sign, "We build out now. Not up.", two tolls from another tower, "That's not an echo") is a strong season-ender that reframes the title. Would I click a sequel? Yes.

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend

There's a city in one stone tower, forty floors high, standing in a sea of fog. At sixteen every kid makes one jump, from any floor they dare, aiming for a single block of water (the Well) on a pillar called the Needle, with one bucket from the Highkeeper as backup. Land it and that floor is your family's for good. Each house may raise two children, and a third (a "Cradle" baby) costs a name: the house has to pick one of its own kids to jump from Forty, and every year one Forty jumper "never lands".

The trick is that the named kid gets an empty bucket and a slow redstone line opens a hole under the Well, so they vanish into the fog, and everyone believes the fog kills. It doesn't. The old Wellwright, Fen, secretly dug a lake at the bottom, and thirty "dead" kids live down there. Buh's big brother Ash was one of them. Their mom said his name (he begged her to, to spare Buh). An allay carrying a message signed the way only Ash writes his B's leads Buh down to the camp.

This year the name is Tilly's, the Highkeeper's granddaughter. Her father plans to put Buh in her place, finds the lake and fills the Needle with gravel, so the hole will open onto rock. On Drop Day Tilly dodges by jumping from Floor 1. Buh makes the Highkeeper show the crowd that his bucket is full, jumps from Forty, sees the floor open onto gravel, and does his trademark last-tick water clutch on the stone beside it. His friend Rook shouts "It's a trick!" to the whole city, and Buh opens the hatch with a redstone torch while the thirty climb out. Old Orlo pays his own house's name by stepping into the fog and wakes up in Fen's lake. Everyone walks down to the grass. Buh nails the sign "41" on the first house built outward. And far off, another tower rings twice.
