# BUILD LIMIT: blind read report (skeptic)

Persona: an adversarial plot-hole hunter who assumes the writer cheated somewhere and writes the "everything wrong with" breakdown.

## Chapter 1: You Fall Once

### 1. What I think the story is so far
Highfall is a city in one stone tower that reaches build limit at Floor 40, standing over a fog sea that "keeps what it takes." At sixteen (I think) everyone jumps once, and whatever floor you jump from is the floor you live on forever. You aim at the Well on top of the Needle, a pillar next to the tower, and the Highkeeper hands you the only bucket you're allowed to carry. Three years ago the Highkeeper swapped Ash's water bucket for an empty one before Ash jumped from Forty. Now his little brother Buh is six days from his own jump, and an allay coming from the Needle has just brought him a note in Ash's handwriting: "Don't take his bucket."

### 2. Confusion log
- **"Buh (13)" in the flashback.** No current age is ever stated, and the jump age is never stated. I worked out that Buh is 16 now, from Ash being 16 when he jumped plus "three years ago" plus "six days." That's maths the viewer shouldn't have to do. ANNOYED.
- **Ash never checks the bucket.** He's about to drop forty floors, his life depends on one item, and the moon visibly catches the water in only one of the two buckets. A Minecraft audience lives in the hotbar. "We see it; he doesn't" gets a pass for a 16-year-old staring at the drop, but the comments will ask. ANNOYED.
- **"Puts the empty one in Ash's hand" vs "He switches to his bucket."** If it's already in his hand, what is he switching from? ANNOYED (nitpick).
- **Buh's practice clutch.** "Places water... lands in it and scoops it back up, and the empty bucket sits in his hotbar again." If you scoop the water back up you're holding a WATER bucket, not an empty one. In a story where empty vs full is the whole hook, getting the bucket state wrong in the first demo muddies the central image. If it's meant as a clue (an empty bucket that somehow still clutches?), it doesn't read as one. LOST for a beat.
- **What is the bucket for if the Well is the target?** If you hit the Well you live whether your bucket is full or empty. So the swap only kills Ash if he also misses. Is the bucket the real mechanism and the Well just the aim point? Unclear. LOST.
- **The blue square going dark "for one frame."** Is the Well being covered, drained, or is that Ash going into it? HOOKED.
- **The second toll "rolls down the tower bell by bell, chasing him."** Lovely, and I want to know what triggers it. HOOKED.
- **"Two to a house."** Mom, Pip and Buh make three in one house, and with Ash it was four. Either the family is breaking a chanted law or "two" means something specific (two children?). Because this is the one law that seems to explain the census question, it needs to be readable. LOST.
- **"Forty's cursed. One a year."** One death a year, and always from Forty? A pattern that tidy should have someone asking questions. HOOKED for now, with a hole pending.
- **"Signed with an eight."** Ash wrote B's as eights, but there's no B in "Ash." So what is the signature, a bare "8"? And the 8UH'S ROOM sign hangs over Buh's door where Mom, Pip and any visitor can see it, so "only one person ever wrote B's as eights" is not proof. HOOKED, but a hole if the story treats it as certain.
- **A single bell in the fog when nobody is jumping.** HOOKED.
- **Why now?** Buh has played the song every night for three years, and only this week does the allay come. ANNOYED unless it's explained.

### 3. Predictions
- The census question is the murder test. "How many in your family?" "Four." Pip was a Cradle baby born about three years ago, so the Cradle (licence to have a baby) pushed the family over "two to a house," and Orlo balanced the books by giving Ash, the Forty jumper, an empty bucket. "There's always room on Forty" means he empties it. "Pip was a Cradle baby. We were lucky." is the darkest line in the chapter if I'm right.
- Tilly is the next target: Forty family, her mom has the Cradle, the baby is due on Drop Day. Her rebellion (jumping from Floor 1) may accidentally save her, or else the rule follows the family and not the floor.
- Ash survived, probably inside or under the Needle (lodestone, allay from that direction), maybe in the Below, and he's the bell in the fog.
- Buh will try to jump from "forty-one," above build limit or off something the rules don't cover.
- Hollis is either Orlo's heir or his conscience. Teo is leverage.

### 4. Interest and next click
Interest 8/10. Yes, I'd click. The bucket swap is a clean, visual crime we see and the victim doesn't, and the note is a strong cliffhanger. But I'm already keeping a list: the Well-vs-bucket logic is the load-bearing wall, and I can't yet tell whether it holds.

## Chapter 2: The Seventeenth Bucket

### 1. What I think the story is so far
The deaths aren't deaths. Every year one jumper from Forty is "Named" in Orlo's ledger, always from the house that just got a Cradle baby, so one in, one out. The Named gets an empty bucket, and the Needle is a rigged trapdoor: a dispenser that can empty the Well, a sticky piston holding the block under it, a lever room on Forty, and a bell rope for the "second toll." The Named drop through the Needle into a green world under the fog. Ash is alive down there, and Buh, convinced he's this year's Named, has just climbed down to him. Tilly turns out to be Orlo's granddaughter.

### 2. Confusion log
- **Who emptied Buh's practice bucket on Floor 12?** Orlo's Keepers? That would be attempted murder, and they don't kill, they exile. Ash or Fen? The chapter treats the emptied bucket as proof the warning is real, but it's Buh's OWN bucket, not "his" (Orlo's), so it isn't what the warning described. If Buh misses that ladder, whoever did it has killed the boy. LOST.
- **Buh's ladder grab.** It works in vanilla, since a ladder resets fall distance. Fine.
- **"Keeps it topped up."** Water sources don't evaporate, and every Minecraft kid in the audience knows a still pool never needs topping up. That's a great planted lie. HOOKED.
- **Thirty tally marks, "never two, never none," and all of them 40.** For thirty straight years, the Named came from a Cradle house AND chose to jump from Forty? Either Orlo picks the Cradle family because their kid has already declared Forty, which is chilling and smart but never said, or the system has a way to force Forty that we haven't seen. The ledger implies the first; the chapter never connects it. LOST.
- **"It rings twice for the one." "Echo."** That pays off the ch1 second toll nicely. HOOKED.
- **"Half of Forty's empty." "Then what are they counting?"** Good, the "tower is full" law is exposed. HOOKED.
- **"It's me this year."** By the ledger's own pattern (Marla's house +1, Named: Ash), this year's Named should come from the house that got the Cradle, which is Tilly's: Orlo's family, a Forty family with a baby due on Drop Day. Buh has just read that pattern. His family has no +1 this year. So his conclusion rests only on a torn page and a margin note, and the obvious deduction (it's Tilly) is sitting right in front of both kids. Tilly doesn't say it either. LOST: his fear is believable, but the scene plays it as a deduction and it isn't one.
- **Orlo catches Buh in his study with the ledger and... lets him go up to the roof.** No Keepers, no detention, no Brann. Then Buh spends the same night watching Fen and climbing into the Needle. Why doesn't the man who runs a thirty-year secret lock up the boy who just read it? ANNOYED, and it'll be worse if Orlo is meant to be competent.
- **Brann is assigned "to keep you safe" and is ditched twice in one chapter.** ANNOYED.
- **"Then who made the rest?"** The rest of what? The empty rooms on Forty? The world, since nobody can build past 320? The line is aimed at something I can't see. LOST.
- **The allay is "asleep."** Allays don't sleep in vanilla. Nitpick, but this audience will clip it. ANNOYED.
- **Ten seconds of white on the way down, and a lake, campfires and people.** The Below is just the overworld. HOOKED, but it opens a big question (below).
- **The ladder.** There's a ladder from the hut straight down to a green world with people in it. Thirty exiles (plus any kids) are a climb away from home. Why has no one ever come back up? The iron trapdoor (redstone only) might be the answer, if the treatment means it. LOST until ch3 explains it.
- **What is the empty bucket FOR in the trap?** If the Well opens and the Named drop down the shaft into a lake, a full bucket wouldn't save them from going down anyway. So the empty bucket only matters if they MISS the Well, and then it kills them on the stone. Is Orlo knowingly risking the "exile" turning into a real death every year? Or does a full bucket let you place water in the shaft and swim back up? Unclear. LOST.

### 3. Predictions
- The margin note is Hollis's handwriting (she "watches the paper change hands"). Hollis is Tilly's mother, and she's trying to swap Buh in to save her own daughter, Tilly, who is the true Named by the ledger's rule. Orlo, the grandfather, tore out the page because it names Tilly, and he's wavering.
- Fen is the go-between: he runs the trap, carries sacks down, and his allay delivered Ash's messages. Fen wrote the tallies out of guilt.
- The Below is fine. It may even be better (sun, grass, room), and the real reason for the exile is control, not space. Or it's worse in some way we'll see in ch3 (mobs at night? no way back up?).
- Ash will want Buh to stay below, or will want to go back up and expose Orlo. The brothers will disagree.
- Climax prediction: Drop Day, Buh jumps from "forty-one," which will be the roof of Forty (the block at the build limit is technically above Floor 40), or he jumps with Tilly.

### 4. Interest and next click
Interest 9/10. Yes, definitely. Revealing Ash alive in a green world under the fog flips the genre in a good way, and the mechanism (dispenser, piston, iron trapdoor, bell rope) is built from real vanilla parts, which is great. My list is growing, though. "It's me this year" is the first place where a character's conclusion doesn't follow from what they've just seen.

## Chapter 3: The Below

### 1. What I think the story is so far
The Named are dropped alive into the overworld under the fog, where thirty of them (minus the dead) live in a walled camp by a lake. The trap only fires from Forty: a slow signal line rolls down the tower and opens the Well just as a Forty jumper arrives, so from any lower floor you land before it opens. The rule behind it is that two children is the Count, a third needs the Cradle, the Cradle costs a name, and the house names one of its own, who must jump from Forty or the house names another. Nobody below goes back up, because when Jory did, his house named his brother. The cliffhanger is that Orlo doesn't pick: Mom named Ash, for Pip.

### 2. Confusion log
- **"Took me three years to get an allay to come when I call."** This answers my "why now," which is good. But Fen walks up and down that ladder with sacks. Why didn't Ash just hand Fen a written book in year one? An allay isn't needed for a courier when you have Fen. ANNOYED.
- **The allay geography.** In ch2 the allay is "asleep" in Fen's hut on the cap, and here it answers Ash's call from the lakeshore through a hundred-plus blocks of fog. Allays only answer note blocks within 16 blocks. The movie can fudge this, but the clip channels will not. ANNOYED.
- **"Thirty ones" at the camp, plus Cass's grave.** Fen's wall has thirty marks. Cass is dead, so there are at most 29 living ones, and Ash (one of the thirty) is standing there. Is the grave one of the thirty? Is Jory's brother at the camp? The count's off by at least one. ANNOYED.
- **Buh's memory: five steps in "one frame."** In ch1 he saw the square go dark "for one frame." Now he remembers the dispenser drinking, the stone sliding, Ash's shape dropping, the floor closing and the water pouring back. Either ch1 lied about the length, or he's reconstructing the memory from the model. As a reconstruction it's fine; as a "memory" it's the writer cheating. LOST for a moment.
- **"This time he remembers past the curtain."** I read it as Mom's hand already being on the curtain BEFORE the drop: she was waiting, so she knew. Nice if it's intended. HOOKED.
- **The Moss gravel bank.** It's vanilla-correct and clearly a Chekhov's gun (a torch under a falling column, as a way to destroy something tall in one hit). HOOKED.
- **Wren's rule, "...or the house names another."** This is THE rule of the movie, and it reads two ways. (a) If the named one won't jump from Forty, the house names another of its OWN: a hostage system where your sibling pays. (b) The house may name someone from ANOTHER house. Reading (b) is the only way Buh can be named this year (his house has no Cradle; Tilly's does), but it would break the society: every Cradle family would name somebody else's kid, and nobody would ever give up their own. Which is it? LOST: this is the load-bearing ambiguity.
- **"She says the rule as if he should already know it."** Who knows the rule? Mom says "Pip was a Cradle baby, we were lucky" in public, to a customer, so adults know. Wren learned it before 16. Then how is Buh, at 16 and about to jump, the one person who has never connected "Pip was a Cradle baby" to "Ash"? He's been looking at both facts for three years. The treatment needs him either kept ignorant on purpose (the rule is told to parents only, when they apply for the Cradle) or in denial. As written, it's a cheat. LOST.
- **Tilly.** If the rule is common knowledge, Tilly knows that her mom's Cradle means her house must name someone this year, and she's the only one of jump age. "I'm jumping from Floor 1. Imagine his face." That's not teenage rebellion, it's the one move the slow line can't catch. Is she dodging her own naming and knowingly letting it fall on someone else? If so, that's brilliant and dark. If not, the treatment hasn't checked what Tilly must know. HOOKED, but it has to be deliberate.
- **Jory: "Year nine, Jory climbed back up... That spring his house named his brother."** Why does Jory's return cause a naming? A naming is paid for a Cradle, not for a returning son. I can make it work: once his mother knew the Named live, giving up another child for another Cradle felt cheap. That's devastating, but it's my inference and the scene doesn't say it. Also, his mother now knows the lake exists. Thirty years of secrecy rests on one mother who already proved she'll trade a child for a baby? LOST.
- **"If the Highs ever find this lake, they'll fill the Needle."** So the Highs think the shaft is death. But Orlo's own Wellwright climbs a ladder down it with sacks. In thirty years, has no High ever asked Fen what's down there, or looked? Why is there a ladder in a death shaft at all? ANNOYED, close to LOST.
- **Bucket AND hole.** "A bucket and a hole. That's the whole trick." But each half seems to make the other redundant. If the floor opens under you, a full bucket doesn't stop you going down the shaft. If your bucket's empty and the Well is drained, you hit stone. The only case where both matter is a jumper who sees the hole and clutches on the cap stone beside it, which is exactly Buh's ch1 party trick ("places water on the stone beside the Puddle"). If that's the logic, it's the best setup in the script and nobody says it out loud. LOST until stated.
- **"Jump from anywhere lower and you've landed before it opens."** Great rule. It also means Mom wanting Ten isn't snobbery, it's her knowing Ten is safe. HOOKED.

### 3. Predictions
- Tilly's house is this year's Cradle house. Tilly is dodging by planning Floor 1, so "the house names another," and Orlo's house (Hollis's hand in the margin) has named Buh. Buh's ch2 "it's me" will turn out right for a reason he didn't know.
- Buh goes back up and confronts Mom, and she admits it. That's ch4's emotional peak.
- Ch4 or ch5: Buh refuses both options (being Named, or jumping low so Pip gets named later). He jumps from "forty-one," the roof above the Plank, maybe with a full bucket or no bucket, and uses the cap-clutch beside the hole to land UP TOP, in front of everyone, exposing the trap. Or he uses Moss's torch-and-gravity trick to collapse something.
- The Below comes up at the climax. Wren refuses, then comes anyway.
- Orlo is less the villain than the bookkeeper. Hollis or Brann may be worse.

### 4. Interest and next click
Interest 9/10. Yes. "Mom named me" is a gut-punch cliffhanger, and it reframes the ch1 hook ("Don't look," "We were lucky"). But this is the chapter where the rules get stated, and the most important sentence in the script ("or the house names another") can be read two ways, and the plot depends on which. I'm starting to suspect the writer needs both readings at once.

## Chapter 4: One In, One Out

### 1. What I think the story is so far
Ash made Mom name him so 13-year-old Buh wouldn't spend three years knowing. The Floor 12 bucket was Rook's clumsy scare, and Rook joins up. The plan is to let Hollis (Tilly's father, Orlo's son), who is pushing to have Buh rung twice in place of Tilly, spring the trap on Buh from Forty in front of a packed walkway, then have all thirty Named climb out of the hatch alive. Hollis finds the lake and fills the shaft with gravel and stone overnight, so tomorrow the floor opens onto rock, and he takes Fen and the hatch lever. Orlo, alone with Tilly's name, takes shears to cut his own tripwire. The Below signals with a torch that they're digging. Tilly finds the page: her grandfather named her.

### 2. Confusion log
- **"I made her. You were thirteen. You'd have spent three years knowing."** That line implies a name can be set years before the jump. Then "one a year, never two, never none" can't hold by itself. If Mom had named Buh, this year would carry two Named (Buh, and Tilly's house). So either Orlo hands out Cradles only to houses with a 16-year-old (never said), or the tally is wrong. LOST.
- **Mom's flashback.** Strong, and it fixes my "who picks" question. "Orlo looks only at Mom, because the house has to say it" is the best rule-in-action beat so far. No confusion.
- **Rook emptied the bucket "to scare you off Forty, not to kill you."** He emptied the bucket of a boy stepping off FLOOR TWELVE. That kills you. Every kid in this city has a bucket-clutch reflex; Rook knows. Then, two lines later, "What do you need?" A near-murder is forgiven in one beat, and his motive (why does Rook care whether Buh jumps from Forty?) is never given. ANNOYED, close to LOST.
- **"Buh tells him what Forty is paid with." / "My family's moving to Forty."** Is Rook's move to Forty itself paid with a name? It reads like a clue I'm supposed to get, and I don't. LOST.
- **Why does Buh need to jump at all?** Thirty "dead" people climbing out of the Needle in front of the packed Floor 1 walkway exposes everything on its own. Buh's "let the whole city watch it open" adds proof of the machine, sure, but the plan never says why both are needed. It reads as the hero needing a stunt. ANNOYED.
- **The ledger.** Orlo sits "with the ledger open at Tilly's name." But this year's page was torn out in ch2, and in scene 6 Tilly finds that very page in her father's room. Orlo can't have it open on his desk. Continuity break. LOST.
- **Iron trapdoor mechanics.** Hollis takes "the hatch's lever" with Fen, THEN "lifts the iron trapdoor." He can't open an iron trapdoor without power. Then "at dawn the Keepers shut the trapdoor": you can't close one by hand either. Removing power is what shuts it. The order is backwards. Vanilla-literate viewers will catch it, and the rest of the movie rewards vanilla literacy, so it matters. ANNOYED.
- **Hollis looks down, sees firelight, "says nothing."** Then he orders a whole night of Keepers pouring gravel down the hole, and every Keeper on that detail can see the firelight too. Thirty years of secrecy is now shared by a work crew. ANNOYED.
- **Where does a sky-city get stacks of gravel?** This tower has no ground, and its economy is mushrooms. Gravel and stone by the hundred, overnight? NITPICK, but it's the same question as "what do they eat," which the treatment never answers.
- **"Every tenth layer a layer of stone."** The writer is clearly pre-empting the Moss torch trick, and that's good. But Hollis has never seen the torch trick, so why does he think of stone layers? Fine if Keepers just do it for strength. NITPICK.
- **Ping the allay brings a single torch: "They're digging."** That's a lovely payoff of the gravel bank, and it lands. HOOKED.
- **Orlo with the shears.** If he cuts the line, the Well never drains and the floor never opens. A Named jumper with an empty bucket then lands in a FULL Well and lives, which also wrecks Buh's "let the city watch it open" plan. Is that the ch5 twist, or did the writer forget the plan relies on the trap firing? HOOKED, provisionally.
- **"Named: Tilly." Her "Floor 1. Imagine his face" was never a joke.** These two beats contradict each other. If Tilly only learns her name now, her ch1 Floor 1 plan was teenage spite, and the narration is putting a meaning in her mouth after the fact. If she knew all along, then on the ch2 roof she let Buh say "It's me this year" without a word, and she has been letting her father put Buh in her place for four chapters. That's a huge character fact, and the treatment can't leave it as narration. LOST.
- **Mom: "Floor 10, or you don't jump."** What happens to someone who doesn't jump? Do they stay on their parents' floor forever? The rule is never given, and it's now Mom's ultimatum. ANNOYED.
- **Timeline.** Six days from the ch1 market: two nights of allay, a practice day, a training night, a heist night, a night and day below, a day up and back down, the night before the Drop. It just about fits, but nothing on screen pins it. NITPICK.

### 3. Predictions
- Drop Day: Orlo has cut the line. Hollis, not knowing, hands Buh the empty bucket and pulls the second lever, and nothing happens. Or Hollis has re-strung it.
- Tilly, now knowing, jumps from Forty herself to take her own name back, and Buh has to choose whether to let her.
- Buh brings his own water against the rules (Rook smuggles it, or he scoops from the Well itself on the way: an EMPTY bucket can pick up water, which would make "the empty one" the hero's tool). He uses the beside-the-target clutch from ch1 to land on the cap next to a hole that opens onto gravel, in front of everyone.
- The thirty dig up through the gravel using torches and come out the hatch at the climax, so "nobody left to name."
- Mom finally plays the song.
- "Forty-one": Buh jumps from the roof above Forty, the block at 320, technically a floor that doesn't exist.

### 4. Interest and next click
Interest 9/10. Yes. All is lost is properly lost: the gravel makes the trap lethal, takes away the hatch, takes Fen, and Mom forbids the jump. But this chapter also produced the most continuity damage so far (the ledger page, the trapdoor order, Tilly's knowledge). Ch5 has a lot of rules to pay off, and it's the chapter where cheating is easiest.

## Chapter 5: Thirty-Three

### 1. What I think the story is so far
On Drop Day, Tilly dodges her name by jumping from Floor 1, so her house owes another. Hollis re-bridges the line Orlo cut, and Tilly lets him so the city can watch. At the Plank, Buh answers the census with "Thirty-three" and dares Orlo to "show them it's full." Orlo gives him water. The floor opens onto gravel, Buh clutches on the stone beside the hole in one tick, and Rook makes the walkway see it. Orlo, whose grandson was born that morning, pays the name himself and steps off wide into the fog. Buh powers the dead hatch with a redstone torch, and the thirty climb out to the city. Orlo surfaces alive in the lake Fen dug thirty years ago. The city builds a staircase down and starts building out. In the distance there are more towers, and a double toll that isn't an echo.

### 2. Confusion log
- **"Mom kneels at the note block for the first time in three years."** In ch4 she "sits at the note block and can't play it." It's a small slip, but it sits in the opening image. ANNOYED.
- **Tilly lets her father bridge the cut ("Do it, then. Everyone's watching.").** The plan Buh made was "let him, the lake'll catch me." Since then the shaft has been filled with gravel, so letting him now means "let him kill Buh unless Buh lands a one-tick clutch with a bucket he hasn't been given yet." Nobody restates the plan after the gravel. Tilly is gambling her friend's life on a plan whose premise is gone. LOST.
- **Buh walks up to the Plank with no backup.** He knows the floor opens onto gravel (he watched it fill). He has no bucket of his own (Brann pats jumpers down). His survival depends entirely on Orlo choosing water. "Show them it's full" is a good public squeeze, but the hero's life is the villain's call. And Rook is standing on the cap beside the Well, having just landed IN the Well from Forty, which means his own bucket is still full. One right-click on the cap stone and Buh has a safe landing. ANNOYED, and the comments will say it.
- **Orlo's offer: "Your mother on Forty. Your sister at a real window."** This is the first time Pip is a sister. Fine. But Ash fell "from Forty" and his family stayed on Floor 3, so does the jumper's family move or not? Rook's "My family's moving to Forty" says yes. Then it's worth one line on why a Named's family doesn't get Forty. ANNOYED.
- **"Don't take his bucket."** The ch1 cliffhanger line is never paid off. Buh takes his bucket, and that's what saves him. If the intent is an inversion (Orlo is the one who changes), someone needs to say it. LOST (a dropped thread, not a confusion).
- **"He lands, scoops the water back, and the empty bucket is in his hotbar."** This is the same error as ch1, now in the climax shot. Scooping water gives a WATER bucket. The ch1 hook ("a plain bucket, not a water bucket") proves the writer knows the icons, so this shot, the most-clipped one in the movie, shows the wrong one. A viewer reading the hotbar will think Orlo handed over an empty bucket after all. LOST for a beat.
- **Orlo steps off "wide of the Needle, into the fog," and surfaces in the lake.** In ch3, "Cass jumped wide" is the one death in the Below, and it has a grave. Jumping wide is now fatal or survivable depending on who the script needs alive. LOST: this reads like a mistake, not a mystery.
- **"Orlo, who believes the fog kills."** His Wellwright has climbed a ladder down that shaft with sacks for thirty years. And the night before, his own son and his own Keepers poured gravel down it all night, took his Wellwright away and locked him "down the hall" from Orlo. The Highkeeper noticed none of it? LOST.
- **"Thirty climb out... thirty people on the cap."** Cass is dead, there are kids born below (Moss), and "the empty camp" means everyone came up. The number thirty can't be the ones (29 alive) and the headcount (more than 30) at once. ANNOYED.
- **The geometry of the shaft.** If the thirty dug the same column the Well drops into, then by the time Ash reaches the trapdoor the gravel under the Well should be gone too (and the hole should open onto thirty people on a ladder). If they're two columns side by side, say so and show it. ANNOYED.
- **Hollis.** He knowingly armed a trap that opens onto gravel, under a boy, in front of the city: attempted public murder. He also rained gravel down onto a camp of people he'd seen alive. His ending is a wistful wide shot, "alone on Forty, looking down." HOOKED as a series thread, ANNOYED as justice.
- **"Weeks later... At Pip's fourth birthday."** Pip was a newborn at the census three years ago, days before Drop Day, and was "(3)" in ch1. Pip's fourth birthday is about a year after this Drop Day, not weeks. NITPICK.
- **The other towers.** From the ground they see "far away, the base of another tower rises into the fog." If these towers reach anywhere near build limit, their tops poke above the clouds, and Buh stood on "the top of the world" in ch2 and saw nothing. Render distance can explain it, but then the ground can't see them either. ANNOYED.
- **"Tilly: The echo." "That's not an echo."** That's a great button. It pays off Fen's "Echo" and the lone bell in the fog from ch1. HOOKED.
- **The redstone torch beside the iron trapdoor.** Correct vanilla, and set up all the way back in ch2 ("redstone dust and torches" in the hut). Best mechanical payoff in the script. But Hollis removed the lever and left a hut full of redstone torches three blocks away. NITPICK.
- **Orlo naming himself.** "Parents can't give themselves," and Orlo is the newborn's grandfather, not a parent, so he's the one person in his house who can. That's genuinely tight, if the audience works it out. It should be said in one line. HOOKED.

### 3. Predictions (end of story, so: what I think the series button means)
- There are many towers, each with its own Count, Cradle and bell. Someone built them all ("then who made the rest?"). The lone bell in ch1 was another tower's toll carried over the fog.
- Hollis stays up top, or goes to another tower, and becomes season two's antagonist.

### 4. Interest and next click
Interest 8/10. The climax hits emotionally: "Thirty-three" and "Show them it's full" are the two best lines in the treatment, and the redstone torch and the Mom/Ash "I named you." "I made you." exchange land. But the mechanics of the climax are carried by the antagonists doing the right thing at the right moment (Hollis re-arms, Orlo gives water), plus one geometry-defying lake. Would I click a season two? Yes. Was the cliffhanger strong? Yes, the "not an echo" button is strong.

# FINAL REPORT (written after reading bible.md)

## The whole story, as I'd tell a friend
There's a city in a single stone tower that reaches the build limit, above a fog everyone thinks is the void. At sixteen you jump once, from any floor you dare, into a one-block Well on a pillar called the Needle, and that floor becomes your family's forever. The Highkeeper hands you the only bucket you're allowed. Every house can raise two kids. A third needs the Cradle, and the Cradle costs a name: the house picks one of its kids, who must jump from Forty. That kid gets an empty bucket, and a slow redstone line drains the Well and opens a hole under it, timed so it only catches a fall from the very top. Buh's brother Ash was that kid three years ago, and Mom said his name so that Pip could be born. But the hole drops into a lake an old engineer, Fen, dug in secret, and the "dead" are living in a green world under the fog. Buh finds them. On Drop Day the Highkeeper's son rigs the trap for Buh over a shaft he's filled with gravel. The old Highkeeper hands Buh water instead, and Buh pulls off a one-tick clutch on the stone beside the open hole in front of the whole city. The thirty dig up and climb out of the hatch, the old man pays the name himself, and everyone walks down a staircase into the sun and starts building out instead of up. Then they hear another tower ring twice.

## Did the chapters deliver the bible?
The spine came across: the bucket, the toll, the hole, the lake, the Cradle's price, "Mom named me," "Thirty-three," "not an echo." These did NOT come across from the chapters alone:
1. **Wren is Fen's daughter.** Fen dug the lake the year his daughter was named, and Fen built the rig. None of this is on screen. It's the emotional key to Fen's thirty-year silence and to Wren's "I'm not climbing back up to say thank you," and to her ending "beside her father." As written, "Fen climbs down with Wren" means nothing.
2. **Cass is Jory's brother ("His brother is the grave").** The two lines sit next to each other, but I read them as two separate facts, and I asked in ch3 whether Jory's brother was at the camp.
3. **Why Jory's return cost his brother a name.** Neither the chapters nor the bible say.
4. **Tilly's secret (she's the one named).** The chapters stage it as a discovery in ch4, so the bible and the chapters disagree about what she knew and when.
5. **Rook as Buh's rival, Floor 12, "family of three."** I read ch1's "All three of us" as Rook, Juno and Kit. No rivalry is shown.
6. **Buh's want ("get Mom and Pip above the clouds").** Never said aloud. His ch1 goal reads as bravado ("forty-one").
7. **Brann reports to Hollis.** Never shown.
8. **The fog is believed to be the void.** Only implied (the cobblestone never lands).
9. **"The shortage is real only because the law against building out keeps it real."** "Half of Forty's empty" is set up as a big question and never answered. The thesis only lands at the very end ("We build out now").
10. **"Two to a house" means two CHILDREN.** That's only clear once Wren states the Count in ch3. The chant alone contradicts Mom's own household.
11. **Orlo can pay the name because he's a grandparent, not a parent.** It's elegant and never said.
12. **The 2x1 shaft (ladder column vs drop column), and that gravel only falls straight down.** Without these, "the floor opens onto gravel" after the diggers have cleared the shaft makes no sense.
13. **Shears disarm tripwire silently.** In the treatment that's narration. Nobody on screen tells the audience, so the cut and the bridge may not read.
14. **"Who cut the string?" is meant to be left open,** but the ch4 shears scene plus "For thirty years... Not this year" effectively closes it. It isn't ambiguous on screen.
15. **The reveal order.** The bible says the Cradle's price comes out in ch3, but the ch2 ledger ("+1 (Pip). Named: Ash") already gives most of it away.

## Unanswered questions

- Did Ash lie with "I'll hear it from the top"?
- Was Mom's hand already on the curtain before the drop? ("Remembers past the curtain.")
- Who built the towers ("Then who made the rest?"), and do the others have a Fen?
- Mom, Fen and Orlo: forgivable?

**CONFUSING (reads like a mistake):**
- Who in the city knows what the Cradle costs?
- When did Tilly know she was named?
- Why did Jory's return cost Cass a name?
- Why is half of Forty empty if the tower is "full"?
- How has Orlo never looked down his own shaft, or noticed his son filling it?
- Why did nobody ever dig down?
- Why does jumping wide kill Cass but not Orlo?
- What happens to someone who refuses to jump (Mom's ultimatum)?
- Does "...or the house names another" mean another of its own, or anyone's child?

## Plot holes and contradictions
My full numbered list, with severity and the smallest fix for each, is the persona section below. By severity:
- **BREAKS THE STORY:** #1.
- **CONFUSES:** #2 to #23.
- **NITPICK:** #24 to #40.

## Best 3 moments
1. **The cold open.** The moon catches the water in one bucket. "You fall once." "The floor I jump from is the floor I live on." The hotbar shows a plain bucket, and the toll rolls down the tower after him. It's a perfect standalone clip that the whole movie pays off.
2. **The census flashback (ch4).** "Take me." "Parents can't give themselves." "Write me." Orlo "looks only at Mom, because the house has to say it." She says "Ash." This is the rule shown in action, and it's devastating.
3. **The Plank (ch5).** "How many in your family?" "Thirty-three." "Show them it's full." The tip of the bucket: water. Then the Floor 6 clutch from Forty beside the open floor. (Close runner-up: the redstone torch beside the dead trapdoor, set up three chapters earlier.)

## Worst 3 moments
1. **Orlo surfaces alive after stepping off "wide of the Needle,"** in the same movie where "Cass jumped wide" is the one grave. That's plot armour for the antagonist's redemption.
2. **Rook's confession (ch4).** He emptied the bucket of a boy stepping off Floor 12, the motive doesn't parse, and he's forgiven and recruited in two lines.
3. **The ch4 cliffhanger, "Named: Tilly."** It's staged as her discovery and narrated as "never a joke" at the same time. Either way it rewrites her silence on the ch2 roof and never deals with it.

## Persona section: everything wrong with BUILD LIMIT

**Rule audit in one breath.** Most of the rules hold against every scene:
- the Drop at sixteen;
- one bucket from his hand, plus the pat-down;
- nothing past the Edge;
- no forty-one, with the vanilla 320 message;
- the slow line, so "always Forty";
- parents can't give themselves, and the grandparent loophole;
- the iron trapdoor needing power, and the redstone torch payoff.

The ones that DON'T hold:
- **The Below's secrecy.** Thirty years, a ladder, firelight, a returnee, a gravel night.
- **Who knows the Cradle's price.**
- **What jumping wide does.** It kills Cass and spares Orlo.
- **The hotbar icon after a scoop.**

**Is the climax earned?** Emotionally, yes. Every beat at the Plank and the cap is set up at least twice. Mechanically, it's half-earned. After the gravel, the hero walks up with no backup and survives because the antagonist chooses water. The other antagonist re-arms the trap at exactly the right moment. The diggers reach the hatch on the exact tick. A one-line re-plan at dawn (#12) would turn "Show them it's full" from a hope into a condition Buh sets, and that would earn it.

### The list

1. **BREAKS THE STORY.** *Ch3, the grave / ch5, Orlo's fall and "Fen's lake."* "Cass jumped wide" is the one death in the Below. Orlo "steps off, wide of the Needle, into the fog" and surfaces alive in the lake. The movie's only on-screen evidence that the Below can kill is contradicted at the antagonist's resolution. **Fix:** Wren: "Cass jumped wide. Wide of the lake, too." And Orlo steps off toward the lake side. Or Fen: "After Cass, I made it wider every year."

2. **CONFUSES (most important).** *Ch1, Mom's stall: "Pip was a Cradle baby. We were lucky," said to a customer. / Ch3, the wall: Wren says the rule "as if he should already know it."* Who knows what the Cradle costs? If adults know, how is Buh, sixteen and the textbook case, the only one who never connected Pip and Ash? If nobody knows, how has the city celebrated one Cradle a year for thirty years while that same house loses a child off Forty every single year, and never noticed? **Fix:** make it an open secret among parents. In ch4 Mom says: "Every mother in this tower knows what the Cradle costs. We don't say it in front of you." In ch1 the customer looks away.

3. **CONFUSES.** *Ch1, the ledge ("Floor 1. Imagine his face") / ch2, the roof (silent at "It's me this year") / ch4, the cliffhanger ("finds the torn page," then "was never a joke").* The bible says her secret is that she's named. The chapters play it as a discovery. If she knew, she let Buh believe it was him and let her father aim it at him. **Fix:** she knew from the census. On the ch4 lakeshore she confesses: "It's my name. Dad's trying to make it yours." The page she finds then proves her FATHER hid it. Cut the narrated "never a joke."

4. **CONFUSES.** *Ch3, the wall: "...or the house names another."* It can mean another of its own (a hostage system) or anyone's child. Buh being named depends on the second reading; the society only works on the first. **Fix:** Wren says "another of its own." Tilly in ch4: "Naming outside the house isn't allowed. That's why Dad tore the page out."

5. **CONFUSES.** *Ch5, the Plank: "Orlo, who believes the fog kills."* His own Wellwright climbs a ladder down it with sacks (ch2). The night before the Drop, his son and his Keepers pour gravel down it all night, and Fen is locked "down the hall" from the lever room (ch4, ch5). The Highkeeper misses all of it, and his whole final act depends on that belief. **Fix:** Hollis to Orlo: "The Needle's cracked. I'm sealing it till the Drop. Fen's sick." And Fen in ch2: "He's never once set foot on this cap."

6. **CONFUSES.** *Ch1, the establishing wide / bible: the Below's ground is about 30-40 blocks under Floor 1.* It's Minecraft. Everyone has a pickaxe (the thirty dig with them). No law forbids digging DOWN. A camp with campfires, nightly mob fights and a note block sits a few dozen blocks under the Lows. **Fix:** add "Nothing under the First" to the chant, enforced like the Edge. Show the tower's foot as the Highs' sealed footing, and put the camp's fires around the far side of the lake.

7. **CONFUSES.** *Ch3, Wren: "Nobody goes up."* "All of us" was available every year for thirty years, and Jory proved the ladder works. **Fix:** tie it to the hatch. It opens only with power from above, and Fen opened it only once, for Jory: "Never again." Buh's redstone torch in ch5 is then the first time it has ever opened from the outside.

8. **CONFUSES.** *Ch3, the grave: "Jory climbed back up... That spring his house named his brother."* A naming pays for a Cradle, not for a returning son. If the house reported Jory, Orlo would know the fog doesn't kill (#5). Fen's tally ("never two") forces the darkest reading: once his mother knew the Named live, the next Cradle cost her nothing. **Fix:** say it. Wren: "His mother knew we lived. So the next Cradle was cheap. She named Cass. Cass jumped wide so he wouldn't go down the hole."

9. **CONFUSES.** *Ch1, the Floor 6 ledge, and ch5, the fall ("scoops the water back, and the empty bucket is in his hotbar"); also the bible's production note.* Scooping water back gives a WATER bucket. The movie's own grammar ("a plain bucket, not a water bucket") makes the hotbar icon the tell. Correct the bible note too: the empty bucket is in the hotbar on the tick he lands, then it's full again.

One right-click on the cap stone gives Buh a guaranteed landing. **Fix:** Brann takes every bucket back on the cap: "One bucket, from his hand, and back to it."
