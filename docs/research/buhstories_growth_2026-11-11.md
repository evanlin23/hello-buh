# Buh Stories (@BuhStoriesMC): how to reach 100k subscribers fast on $0 (research, 2026-11-11)

This builds on `docs/story_arcs.md`, `docs/story_research_mystery.md`, `docs/research/narration_2026-11-10.md` (with its
`_data/`) and `docs/brand.md` "Buh Stories channel". Those already settle the story shapes, the narrator, the loop, the
titles, the pinned questions and the branding, so this page doesn't repeat them. It covers distribution, benchmarks,
conversion, launch, risk and money.

**How to read the labels.**
- Source type:
  - **[YT]**: YouTube's own words, from YouTube Help, the official blog, or Creator Insider (quoted directly).
  - **[YT-2nd]**: YouTube's words as reported by a trade outlet.
  - **[DATA]**: a third-party dataset.
  - **[OURS]**: our own measurement.
  - **[LORE]**: creator or vendor-blog consensus with no primary source.
- Confidence: **High**, **Med** or **Low**.

## Executive summary (10 lines)
1. YouTube has said the Shorts feed shows each Short to a small exploratory audience and widens or stops based on how that audience reacts ("the audience is the algorithm"). It names no thresholds. The only creator-facing signals are "stayed to watch" (viewed vs swiped away), percentage viewed, engaged views and subscribers per Short.
2. Since 2026-08-24 a view counts from the first frame. Engaged views (watched past the first seconds, loops excluded) are what YPP and most analytics use. **Judge everything on engaged views**, never the public count.
3. YouTube has said the following:
   - every Short, even a channel's first, can be recommended;
   - posting time doesn't matter to the system;
   - no posting volume triggers a penalty;
   - deleting and reposting "risks being seen as spam".
4. Niche benchmarks, from channels we measured:
   - Xenost (Roblox first-person story): created 2026-07-07, 1.32M subs in 4 months, about 2.5 Shorts a day, first Short 11M views.
   - Ashyyy: 818k in 4 months.
   - narrow: 2.36M in 22 months on only 70 videos.
   - Qubik: 78k in 11.5 months.
   - dum Dum gaming: 15M views but only 9.8k subs in 3 months.
5. Lifetime subscribers per 1,000 views in the genre is 0.6-2.0. Our runaway Short did 3.2 on 22k views. **At 1-3 subs per 1k engaged views, 100k subs costs 33-100M engaged views.**
6. **Realistic time to 100k:**
   - **2-4 months** if one of the first ~30 Shorts breaks 1M and we follow it up within days;
   - **6-12 months** (central case) if we land occasional 1M hits;
   - **never, without a format change,** if no Short passes 100k views by upload 30.
   The median new Shorts channel's best of its first ten Shorts gets about 8.8k views (vidIQ).
7. Policy risk is manageable but real. YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule names "templated storylines" and "characters put in the same situation over and over with the same outcome". Our fixed arcs and fixed narrator must keep producing genuinely different premises and outcomes.
8. **No AI disclosure is required** for a clearly unrealistic animated Minecraft fiction with a synthetic narrator who isn't a real person. Pip is Gemini TTS, which is SynthID-watermarked, so YouTube may auto-label. Its own Help says a label doesn't limit reach or earnings.
9. **Minecraft's usage guidelines allow ad revenue on gameplay videos** that add your own unique content. Never make "Minecraft" the dominant part of a name, and never imply Mojang endorses us.
10. **Money is not near-term.**
    - Fan funding unlocks at 500 subs plus 3M Shorts views in 90 days.
    - Ad revenue needs 1k subs plus 10M Shorts views in 90 days today, which becomes **20M from 2027-02-01** for new applicants.
    - From 2027-02-01, Shorts ad revenue also needs a rolling 10M per 90 days.

## Prioritised actions

### Do before launch
1. **Set the audience to "No, it's not made for kids" at channel level, and keep every word of copy general-audience.** YouTube weighs "characters that appeal to children", "stories for children" and the actual audience. A bubbly kid voice plus Minecraft is exactly the risk profile. Made-for-kids status turns off comments and notifications, so it would kill the pinned-question and subscribe loop. The eerie, mild-peril tone is our honest evidence of general audience. Never write "for kids". [YT] High.
2. **Lock the handle and name now.** Each can be changed only twice in 14 days, and an old handle is freed to others after 14 days. @BuhStoriesMC is compliant: "MC" is not the dominant "Minecraft" name. [YT] High.
3. **Verify the phone number to get "advanced features" on day 0**, and turn on 2-step verification (a YPP requirement). Without advanced features we lose the Related video link, Posts and custom thumbnails, and new channels otherwise wait for two months of history. [YT for the related link] High; [LORE for the posts gate] Med.
4. **Set the upload defaults once in Studio → Settings → Upload defaults:**
   - video and title language: English (needed for auto-captions and auto-dubbing);
   - category: Gaming;
   - "altered content: No";
   - comments: hold potentially inappropriate;
   - allow remixing.
   The category's effect on Shorts reach is unproven, so it's only for consistency. [LORE] Low.
5. **Build a backlog of 10-14 finished Shorts before the first upload.** Results are a lottery:
   - the median channel's first Short gets about 1.4k views in 30 days;
   - the best of its first 10 gets about 8.8k (6.5x);
   - only 21 % of individual Shorts reach 10k.
   The first ten must not be held hostage to production. [DATA vidIQ] Med.
6. **Pre-write the variation plan for the first 20 stories:**
   - no more than 2 in a row on the same arc (T1-T5);
   - no repeated outcome type (e.g. "the narrator was the mob" at most twice);
   - a different setting each time.
   This is our main defence against the inauthentic-content rule, and a check the story-critic can make. [YT] High.
7. **Use HelloBuh as a co-author.** YouTube says a collaboration video is recommended to the audiences of both channels. Put HelloBuh as collaborator on Light Sleeper, if the invite is offered (feature eligibility isn't stated on the Help page). This is a $0 seed audience that already likes our Minecraft look. [YT] Med (eligibility unknown).

### Week 1
1. **Day 1: Light Sleeper (the bar).** YouTube says even a first Short can be recommended and that early views are exploratory, so don't save the best story. A first hit compounds through subscriber conversion. [YT] Med.
2. **Post 1 Short a day, every day.** Go to 2 a day only while the buffer is 14 or more and quality holds; never more than 3 a day.
   - YouTube: no volume threshold triggers anything.
   - vidIQ (correlational): channels posting 52+ times in 360 days reached 1k subs 30.8 % of the time, vs 8.7 % for 12-51 uploads.
   [YT + DATA] Med.
3. **Posting time is low-stakes.** Keep the uploader's varied times, landing roughly in US afternoon or evening. YouTube says posting time "is not something we optimise for". [YT] Med.
4. **Do these by hand in Studio on every Short, because the API can't do them:**
   - **Related video:** Studio → Content → Short → Related video. It needs advanced features, and third-party tools report the Data API cannot set it. Point it at the channel's current best Short.
   - **Pinned question:** pin the story's question (brand.md list).
   [YT for the feature; LORE for the API gap] Med.
5. **Cross-promote from HelloBuh once 3-5 Buh Stories Shorts are live,** with a Post plus a pinned comment, so visitors land on a shelf and not on a single video. [LORE] Low.
6. **Write nothing into the description that gives the twist away** (brand.md rule 10). Add the Minecraft disclaimer line to the *channel* description: "Not an official Minecraft product. Not approved by or associated with Mojang or Microsoft." [Mojang] High.

### Ongoing: what to measure and the decision rules
Log every Short's numbers at 48 h and at 7 days, from Studio: shown in feed, stayed to watch %, engaged views, APV, subs gained, comments. Use `uploads/studio.json` (by hand, as now).

**Benchmarks:**
- Subs per 1k engaged views. Ours: runaway 3.2, spot_wrong 2.4, lapse_castle 0.8.
- Stayed to watch: blog lore says 70-80 % is good. YouTube publishes no benchmark, so use our own rolling median.
- APV above 100 % means it is looping.

| signal (per Short, after 1,000+ feed impressions) | threshold | action | basis |
|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Launch: week 1, and what's hard to change

**What's hard or impossible to change later:**
- **Handle and name:** two changes per 14 days; an old handle is freed after 14 days. [YT Help 2657964](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2657964) [YT] High.
- **Audience (made for kids):** the channel setting applies to existing and future videos. YouTube can lock a video's setting if it detects an error, and then you can only appeal. If we're wrong, comments, notifications and Posts go. [YT Help 9528076](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076), [9610989](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9610989) [YT] High.
- **Channel country:** sets YPP eligibility and the program terms. Set it truthfully at creation. [YT Help 13429240](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en) [YT] Med.
- **The first impression of the channel's catalogue:** the inauthentic-content review is channel-level and looks at "several videos in a row". A first page of near-identical stories is the hardest thing to undo. [YT Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) [YT] High.
- **Easy to change:** category, tags, upload defaults, captions and language. Set them once for consistency; they're low stakes.

The week 1 plan is in the action list above. Why each choice:
- Backlog of 10-14 (vidIQ lottery statistics).
- 1 a day (YouTube: no volume penalty; capacity limits us; correlational upside to more).
- Strongest story first (YouTube: any Short can be recommended).
- No time-of-day optimisation (YouTube).

## 5. Risks

### 5a. AI and TTS, and the altered-content disclosure
- Disclosure is required only for realistic content: a real person saying or doing something they didn't, altered
  footage of real events or places, or realistic scenes that didn't happen.
  - Not required: "clearly unrealistic" content (animation, fantasy), aesthetic edits, production help such as
    AI-written scripts, and cloning your own voice.
  - TTS isn't mentioned.
  - [YT Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en), [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content) [YT] High.
- **For Buh Stories:** a blocky Minecraft world, a fictional kid narrator, and no real person's voice or likeness.
  **No disclosure is required.** Toggle "altered content: No". [YT] High.
- **Auto-labels.** YouTube may auto-apply an AI label (its own GenAI tools, C2PA metadata, or detection). Detection
  labels can usually be removed by answering "No". Pip is **Gemini TTS**, which Google says carries an inaudible
  **SynthID** watermark on all audio output, so a detection label is plausible.

## Realistic timeline to 100k

The arithmetic: 100k subs at 1-3 subs per 1k engaged views needs **33-100M engaged views**. At 1 Short a day, that is:

Why it's this wide:
- Shorts outcomes are heavy-tailed. One hit is worth more than 100 median Shorts.
- Conversion falls as reach widens (dum Dum: 0.64 per 1k).
- Our only data is three HelloBuh Shorts at around 20k views.

The number to watch is **the best Short's engaged views after 10 and after 30 uploads**. It tells you which row we're in.
