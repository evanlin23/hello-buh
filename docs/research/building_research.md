# Research: how good Minecraft builds are made (2026-11-10)

Asked by the owner, 2026-11-10: "our builds are not particularly good looking or creative, are there guidelines
online about good minecraft building we could look at?" The rules distilled from this are in
`docs/building_guide.md`. Below: what each source says, and the licence check for building files.

Some pages could not be fetched from the session (minecraft.net articles returned 503, planetminecraft.com and
sportskeeda.com 403); for those the notes come from search-result summaries and are marked *(summary)*.

## 1. Sources and notes

### Minecraft Wiki, "Tutorial: Adding beauty to constructions"
https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Adding_beauty_to_constructions
- "Try not to make your build look like a box." Bay windows, framed (recessed) windows, arches for depth.
- Blend several shades of one main colour plus one accent colour. Match the palette to the biome (sandstone in
  deserts, dark oak in dark forests). Keep the roof in the house's palette (no acacia in an oak/spruce house).
- Vary floors with cracked/mossy variants: the more cracked, the older.
- Ceilings 3-4 (2 is too low), small rooms 4-5 high, medieval living rooms 5-6, halls 10-12.
- Landscaping: shrubs, flowers, a few trees, potted plants; hedges from leaves on wood; a focal point (fountain,
  tree house) in larger gardens. No grass or dirt as interior floors.
- Lighting: lanterns, campfires, chandeliers rather than torches.
- Shutters on 1x2 windows, paintings/item frames on bare walls, fewer doors and more open space.

### Minecraft Wiki, "Tutorial: Tips for your house" *(summary)*
https://minecraft.gamepedia.com/Tutorials/Tips_for_your_house
- Detail matters more than the chosen style. Credits Grian and MinecraftCinematics videos for the idea of depth.

### Minecraft.net (Mojang), "How do mega builds" (Grian's mega-base tutorial) *(summary)*
https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/how-do-mega-builds
- Plan the shapes first with coloured wool, then detail. Detailing "is by far the most lengthy bit": roof trims,
  balconies, windows, chimneys, pillars, arches, indents, and breaking up big shapes with friezes and strips of
  colour. Lighter colours and simple shapes at the bottom, bolder at the top to draw the eye up.

### Minecraft.net (Mojang), "Make your houses better" *(summary)*
https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/make-your-houses-better
- Fence posts, slabs, buttons and trapdoors for detail; bushes and trees and a kept landscape; mix materials
  (cobblestone with wood).

### Minecraft.net (Mojang), "Tutorial: tips for landscaping and terraforming" (BlueNerd) *(summary)*
https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/tutorial--tips-for-landscaping-and-terraforming
- "It's the terraforming that's hard": houses are easy, the land around them makes the build.
- No straight natural run over about 7 blocks (hillsides, paths, gardens, trees; not houses).
- Replace grey/bland terrain with brown dirt textured with granite and seagrass; river banks get erosion
  (coarse dirt, paths) and plants. Double-layer terrain so holes don't expose voids.

### WesterosCraft building docs (a large, long-running build server)
- Exteriors: https://westeroscraft.com/docs/building/fundamentals/exteriors
  - Depth should be structural: overhangs, buttresses, window reveals, sills. "Depth that exists purely to break
    up a flat wall without any structural logic tends to look arbitrary."
  - "Most roofs should include an eave or overhang ... A roof that cuts straight to the wall looks unfinished."
  - Mix two complementary woods (birch-oak, oak-jungle, jungle-spruce); wooden roofs use at least two woods (base
    plus weathering). Weathering at exposed ridges (bleach) and drainage points/eaves (water damage), never random.
  - Thatch at least 45 degrees; keep a defined roof shape.
- Layout and foundation: https://westeroscraft.com/docs/building/fundamentals/layout-foundation
  - Outline in wool first; avoid 2-high rooms; avoid symmetrical square footprints; L, T and offset plans; each
    wing should have a purpose; fit the layout to the land.
  - Every building has a stone base; no dirt or sand visible under walls; on slopes the foundation steps down.
- Gradients: https://westeroscraft.com/docs/building/fundamentals/gradients
  - Order a palette by shade first, then colour, then texture. Radiating clusters, brightest where the eye should
    go, darker at weathering points (openings, corners, bases). Avoid horizontal "sandwiching", "splattering"
    (skipped steps) and "smallpox" (patchy shallow gradients).

### Conquest Reforged, "Gradienting your builds"
https://conquestreforged.com/guides/gradients
- Transition step by step through value and hue; skip steps only for accents. Clusters of varied size, never in
  rows or columns. Pick blocks close in value, colour and shape. Random placement "rarely looks good". Squint to
  check values. Window drip stains: darken below windows. Lighter edge around arches.

### Craftdex, "The architecture of builds"
https://craftdex.net/articles/the-architecture-of-builds
- Massing: 3-4 volumes at different heights; secondary volumes 1/3-1/2 of the primary. Silhouette test: shape
  readable with brightness at zero.
- Focal element 25%+ taller or wider; odd wall lengths (5, 7, 9, 11) for a centre axis; detail densest at the
  focal point.
- Pillars 1 block proud; micro-depth from stairs/slabs (0.5), trapdoors/walls (<0.5), panes (0.4375 recess).
  "Any wall wider than 5 blocks should have at least one layer of depth."
- Odd roof widths end in one ridge block. Palette 3-4 materials, 60/30/10.

### verbina29, "minecraft building fundamentals"
https://verbina29.neocities.org/articles/mcBuilding
- Depth is the most important fundamental: interior-set doors, support pillars, fences, panes instead of glass
  blocks, roof overhangs. L/T footprints for large builds. Steep roofs for fantasy, shallow/asymmetric for modern;
  contrasting roof block; curve roof tips. Neutral main palette with colour in the details. Build into terrain
  (under cliffs, over ravines, into hills). Details: dormers, flower boxes, shutters, balconies, window hoods.
  Weathering with darker, cracked, mossy variants, vines and moss.

### Vibecraft "choosing materials" agent skill *(page only; licence not stated)*
https://skills.sh/amenti-labs/vibecraft/choosing-materials
- 60% primary (walls), 30% secondary (roof, trim), 10% detail. Weighted texture mixes such as
  80% stone_bricks / 15% cracked / 5% mossy.

### Stone texture mixes (community guides) *(summary)*
- https://craftdex.net/articles/stone-variant-palette-guide, https://gamertagmythras.com/blog/minecraft/minecraft-building-tips,
  https://www.minecraftmaps.com/tools/wall-pattern-generator
- One dominant block, never two equal primaries; e.g. stone_bricks 45 / stone 25 / andesite 20 / cobblestone 10.
  Mossy variants only near the ground and water.

### Roofs and depth (community guides) *(summary)*
- https://www.exitlag.com/blog/minecraft-roof-designs/, https://sportskeeda.com/minecraft/4-best-ways-add-depth-minecraft-build,
  https://ggservers.com/blog/improve-minecraft-building-skills-easily/
- Overhang 1-2 blocks with upside-down stairs under it; pillars pulled 1 block out cast a vertical shadow line;
  windows recessed and framed with trapdoors, stairs, fences. Order of work: shape, palette, depth, roof, details.

### Japanese style *(summary)*
- https://www.exitlag.com/blog/minecraft-japanese-house/, https://minecraft.how/blog/post/build-japanese-pagoda-minecraft
- Dark frame (dark oak/spruce) over white walls; roofs of deepslate tiles, blackstone or dark oak stairs, red
  terracotta for pagodas; stepped stairs make the roof read as curved; cherry and bamboo in small doses.

### Villages and towns *(summary)*
- https://minecraft.how/blog/post/build-medieval-town-minecraft, https://8bittoast.com/minecraft-build-a-village/,
  https://www.planetminecraft.com/blog/tips-for-building-a-medieval-town/
- Roads first; the biggest building (church, smithy, well, market) at the focal point and the rest around it;
  one shared palette, varied roof heights, overhangs, mixed oak/spruce beams, barrels, flower pots, lanterns;
  flatten roads, leave the land around them natural; organic layouts look better than grids.

### Vanilla structures and commands
- `/place`: https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/place (`feature`, `jigsaw <pool> <target> <max_depth 1-20>`,
  `structure`, `template ... [rotation] [mirror] [integrity] [seed] [strict]`; structures fail with no valid biome).
- Villages: https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village (IDs `village_plains`, `village_desert`, `village_savanna`,
  `village_snowy`, `village_taiga`; style set by the biome at the centre; palettes per type).
- Village pieces: https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village/Structure (town-centre pools `minecraft:village/<type>/town_centers`;
  pieces such as `village/plains/houses/plains_small_house_5`).
- Jigsaw example: https://www.sportskeeda.com/minecraft/how-generate-in-game-structures-using-place-command-minecraft-1-19
  *(summary: `/place jigsaw minecraft:village/desert/town_centers bottom 3`)*.

### On-camera presentation *(summary)*
- https://www.planetminecraft.com/blog/tips-to-improve-your-screenshots-4075113/,
  https://texture-packs.com/guides/how-to-take-better-minecraft-screenshots
- Something in the foreground adds depth without hiding the subject; don't centre the subject; low camera reads
  more cinematic. Silhouettes need a dark subject against a bright sky (general photography).

### Grian
- His "101 Minecraft Build Hacks" (2017) and 1.14 build-hacks videos are the best-known tips collections
  (https://www.sportskeeda.com/minecraft/grian-s-helpful-building-techniques-minecraft *(summary)*); the written
  version of his approach is the Mojang mega-build article above (plan in wool, then long detailing).
- Not found in text: BdoubleO100's or Mumbo's own written guides (their tips are in videos only); the Mojang
  terraforming article (BlueNerd) covers the same landscaping ground.

## 2. What we build today (lib/story.js)
- `house()`: a 6x6 hollow box of one plank type, cobble floor, 4 oak log corners, a flat spruce_planks ceiling
  with a spruce_slab lid, 2 glass blocks on two walls, glowstone in the ceiling, an oak door. Fails rules 2-10 of
  the guide (box, even width, flat roof, no overhang, no foundation, no landscaping).
- `clearSet()`: a 129x129 flat grass clearing with a terraced skirt: exactly the "superflat" look the owner called
  low effort. The guide replaces it with build-into-the-site and vanilla structures.

## 3. Renderer notes (our renderer, not vanilla)
`renderer/blockentities.js` draws signs, hanging signs, chests, beds, bells, banners, skulls and shulker boxes.
`docs/stories.md` still says block entities don't render; that predates the renderer work. Decorated pots,
lectern/enchanting books and conduits aren't in the list: render a still before relying on them.

## 4. Licences: building files we could legally use in a public repo

The question: can we commit someone else's schematic (`.schem`, `.litematic`, `.nbt`) to this public repo?

- **Vanilla structures (Mojang):** use them through `/place structure|jigsaw|template` on the server. That is
  ordinary use of the game, covered by Mojang's usage guidelines for videos (monetised videos are allowed; the
  guidelines are not a licence and can change: https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines, summarised at
  https://conductatlas.com/platform/minecraft/minecraft-usage-guidelines/video-content-monetization-carve-out/).
  Do **not** copy the `.nbt` files out of the jar into the repo: that redistributes Mojang's files.
- **Clearly permissive (CC0-1.0) repositories found** (licence from GitHub's licence detection; check the LICENSE
  file and that the author made the build before use):
  - `Greaby/minecraft-circles-schematics` (CC0-1.0): schematics of every circle 1x1 to 64x64. Useful as a geometry
    reference for towers and domes; trivial to generate ourselves anyway.
  - `DuskScorpio/Scorpio-File-Downloads` (CC0-1.0): world downloads and schematics from the author's videos.
    Contents not inspected (GitHub API access not enabled in this session); appears to be technical/redstone, not
    architecture. Treat as unverified.
  - No CC0, Unlicense or MIT repository of **architecture** (houses, villages, castles) was found.
- **Not usable / unclear:**
  - Planet Minecraft: licence is set per project by its author; many have none (all rights reserved), some allow
    use with credit, some forbid redistribution (https://www.planetminecraft.com/tickets/21051/,
    https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/medieval-windmill-schematic-3304524/). Not usable without a stated
    open licence on the specific project.
  - Free schematic sites (minecraft-schematics.com, mineschematic.com, shulkr.com, createmod.com,
    abfielder.com): "free to download", no licence stated. Not usable in a public repo.
  - BuiltByBit: paid, per-buyer EULA. Not usable.
  - `nate2014jatc/Schematics` on GitHub: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (no derivatives, no commercial use). Not usable.
- **Conclusion:** generate our builds in code from the rules in `docs/building_guide.md`, and use vanilla
  structures by ID as a base. Our own generator code and any schematics we export from our own builds are ours to
  commit.
