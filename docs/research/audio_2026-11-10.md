# Audio research: music, sound effects and the mix for story Shorts (2026-11-10)

Owner, 2026-11-10: "the best music to use? Are we rolling our own music? The best sound effects? ... the best
audio? ... everything should be the best and optimized in the process." Later the same day: "on screen sfx as
well, if needed/maybe". The standard that came out of this is `docs/audio.md`; the code is
`scripts/story_audio.py`, `scripts/onscreen_sfx.py` and `music/score.json`.

What was measured here and what comes from published norms or practitioner guides is kept apart. Where a
number is a judgement call, it says so.

## 1. What could and couldn't be measured
- **Our 8 published story Shorts** (`../hb-videos/shorts/story_*`): measured with `scripts/audio_measure.py`
  (ffmpeg ebur128: integrated loudness, LRA, true peak, 400 ms momentary loudness; the bed recovered as
  `short.mp4` minus `short_nosong.mp4`).
- **Top story Shorts' audio files: blocked.** yt-dlp got HTTP 403 on the media download for the top Xenost
  Short (`OZva-6vx4jk`). Getting past YouTube's bot check is off-limits, so no competitor file was measured. In
  their place: YouTube's own loudness reference (-14 LUFS), the AES/EBU streaming and dialogue norms, and the
  auto-caption data the narration study already had.
- **Auto-captions as a music signal.** YouTube's ASR writes `[Music]` when music is prominent enough to
  transcribe. From the 106 caption tracks the narration study fetched (`docs/research/narration_2026-11-10.md`):

  | Shorts | transcripts | with a `[Music]` tag |
  |---|---|---|
  | narrated (40+ words) | 71 | 21 (30%) |
  | wordless or nearly (under 40 words) | 35 | 22 (63%) |

  The big animated-storytime channels (Haminations 9.1M, CypherDen 2.3M, Infamous Swoosh 1.8M) had **0 of 20**
  narrated Shorts tagged. Their music, if any, never gets loud enough over the voice for the ASR to call it out.
  Wordless channels (AniMatters, Kamui, Hapico...) are the opposite: music carries the story. **So in narrated
  story Shorts the voice owns the track and the music sits well under it.** The bed is texture and pace, not a
  lead. That matches what we already do. Our beds were a fixed -16 dB under the voice and never moved, though.

What the numbers say:
- **Loudness is right already:** -14 LUFS integrated, true peak under -1 dBTP.
- **Digital silence between lines:** 8-12% of every story's nosong mix is below -70 LUFS (true digital silence).
  The NCS bed covered it in the uploads, but any Short without a bed, and every gap when the bed dips, drops to
  nothing. The pacing gate found the same thing.
- **The bed never moves.** It's 16-18 dB under the voice whether a line is spoken or not. It doesn't come up in
  the pauses, doesn't leave before the twist and doesn't change after it. The NCS phonk tracks were chosen for
  the stunt Shorts (drops and energy). They don't change mood with a story, and phonk under a serious narrated
  story is the opposite of the "cinematic, professional" feel the owner asked for.
- **Silent visible actions:** 34 visible sound-making events in story_torch (15 digs, 14 pickups...) had no
  sound at all (`scripts/onscreen_sfx.py story_torch`). story_sheep_stares had 6, story_nitwit 5 (falling
  anvils, a fence breaking).
- **The voice is raw TTS:** no EQ, de-esser or compression; only a loudness normalizer. Its 1-4 kHz presence
  share (the band that carries intelligibility) is 13-16% of the mix's energy. After the clarity chain it's 21%.

## 3. Music
### What story Shorts use
- Narrated story channels: the voice leads and the music is a low bed (section 1). Wordless story channels:
  music leads. Many Roblox storytime channels lay a trending or "sad" song under the narration, often added
  with the Shorts audio picker. That gives sound-page reach, but a picker song is claimed by its rights holder
  and can't be disputed (YouTube Help 13053317, in `research/music_and_length_2026-11-06.md`). It also can't be
  done from our automated upload.
- The cinematic register the owner wants ("serious, professional") is film-score grammar: sustained beds
  (drones, low strings, sparse piano), a build in the last third, **a drop-out right before the reveal and a hit
  on it**, then a change of colour after the twist (sad, eerie, triumphant). The trailer-editing and horror-
  Shorts conventions agree on this. It's practitioner knowledge, not something measured here.
- Tempo: under narration the bed should be slow (50-80 bpm) or unmetered, so it doesn't fight the narration's
  4.5 words/s rhythm (`docs/research/narration_2026-11-10.md`). Faster tracks (funny, heroic: 85-130 bpm) suit
  the light moods.
- Spectrum: a bed with a lead instrument in the voice band (1-4 kHz) masks words. Measured on the candidate
  tracks (share of energy at 1-4 kHz in the first 60 s): Ossuary 6 0.000, Stay the Course 0.003, Thinking Music
  0.003, Bittersweet 0.004, Crypto 0.008 (all clean); Heartbreaking (solo piano) 0.449, Unseen Horrors 0.181,
  Strength of the Titans 0.134. The clean ones are beds; the busy ones are payoff tracks, played after the
  narration is mostly done. `story_audio.pick` skips a bed above 0.15 automatically.

### Roll our own?
The cinematic branch's `dog_hill` used an in-house score: a note-block harp lullaby made from the game's own
samples (`scripts/story_short.py` on `claude/story-cine-2`). Compared on the same measurements as the licensed
tracks:

| | dog_hill score (ours) | licensed beds (range) |
|---|---|---|
| instruments | 1 sampled note-block harp + a sine pad | orchestras, choirs, synths, recorded and mixed by a professional |
| LRA (first 60 s) | 8.5 LU | 1.5-13.5 LU |
| L/R correlation | 0.20 | -0.23 to 0.91 |
| 1-4 kHz share | 0.039 | 0.000-0.18 |

Its loudness and spectrum fit a bed fine. Its sound doesn't: one plucked timbre and a static pad. It reads as
"Minecraft music box", which is charming for a cozy or sad story but has none of the weight of strings, choir
or brass under a horror reveal. The honest options for rolling our own:
1. **Procedural (numpy, what we have):** right for **texture**: room tone, drones, risers, sub hits and the
   drop-out logic. All of that is now ours (`story_audio.py`, `sfx.py`). It's wrong for melody or harmony
   that has to sound like a film score.
2. **MIDI + free sample libraries** (VSCO 2 Community Edition is CC0; Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra; GM
   soundfonts like GeneralUser GS): no licence risk at all. But unhumanised sampled orchestra sounds like a
   demo, and someone has to write music per mood. That's days of work to reach Kevin MacLeod's floor, and his
   catalogue is free, credited and tagged.
3. **The note-block motif** stays useful as an in-world colour (a story about a jukebox, a lullaby, a sad
   ending), not as the default.

**Recommendation:** use licensed cinematic tracks for the music (incompetech CC BY, mood-tagged, credited
automatically in the description at upload, which is what keeps its Content ID registration from claiming) and our own procedural layers for everything around them (room tone, risers, hits, ducking,
drop-outs, the switch at the twist). Revisit option 2 only if a claim problem shows up with CC BY tracks.

## 4. Sound effects
- **What the thing on screen does, it sounds like (vanilla first).** The game's own sound for every visible
  action: a dig's hits and break, a place, a door, a pickup, a mob's hurt and death, a bell, eating, a fuse,
  thunder. The files come from Mojang's asset CDN through `audio.py` (sha1-checked, cached in `work/sounds`,
  never committed). Minecraft's Usage Guidelines allow game audio in videos, including monetized ones.
- **Design sounds, sparingly.** Practitioner guides agree that a whoosh on every transition stops registering
  ("after ten minutes the effect has no impact left": overseeros.com/blog/youtube-sound-design). A story cuts
  on nearly every line, so whooshes go only on scene changes (a jump in the time of day, or a move of more than
  24 blocks), at most one per 6 s, peaking 40 ms before the cut, 20 dB under full scale.
- **A riser into the payoff, silence, then the hit.** That's the trailer grammar: the riser ends where the music
  drops out, 0.45 s of near-silence (voice and room tone only) builds anticipation, and the impact lands on the
  payoff frame. The drop-out now sits 19.8 dB under the hit on story_runaway (10.9 dB in the old mix) and 18.5 dB on story_nitwit (8.0 dB).
- **Room tone.** Real rooms are never silent, and digital silence reads as an edit error. Soft low-passed air at
  -47 LUFS runs under everything. That took the nosong mixes from 8-12% digital silence to 0%.
- **Sources** (all logged by URL, none committed): the Mixkit Sound Effects Free License ("sfxFree": monetized
  YouTube OK, no attribution, no standalone redistribution, which is why it's fetched and not committed) for
  the cinematic whoosh, riser and impact; Kenney CC0 (committed); our synths as the fallback for each.
  **Freesound CC0 is blocked from the container** (HTTP 403 on freesound.org and its CDN, and downloads need a
  login), so nothing from it is used. The owner can add Freesound CC0 clips by hand, with the URL in
  `sfx/SOURCES.md`.

## 5. Mix and loudness
- **Delivery: -14 LUFS integrated, true peak <= -1 dBTP.** YouTube normalises louder uploads down to about
  -14 LUFS (it doesn't raise quieter ones), and -14 / -1 dBTP is the common platform target for YouTube,
  Spotify and TIDAL (forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026). AES
  streaming guidance puts -1 dBTP as the minimum safety margin for lossy codecs (AES TD1004; EBU R128 s2 is
  stricter for broadcast distribution). Our old master already hit -14 LUFS; the new one gets there with a
  static gain and a limiter run at 4x oversampling, so the ceiling is a true-peak ceiling. AAC encoding adds 0.3-1 dB
  of overshoot after the limiter (the first try measured -0.7 dBTP), so the master measures the encoded file and
  lowers the ceiling until it lands. The demos measured -1.6 and -1.0 dBTP at -14.2 / -14.1 LUFS.
- **Voice-to-music: about 18 dB under speech, about 11 dB in pauses.** Guides for voice-over beds give 15-24 dB
  under the voice for storytelling, ducking 6-12 dB with a 30-80 ms attack and a 250-700 ms release, and
  over-ducking as the most common mistake (nemovideo.com, vidpros.com, openclip.app; search notes in this
  doc's history). Phone speakers and captions-on viewing favour the clear end. We use a bed at -26 LUFS, ducked
  7 dB while a line is spoken (80 ms attack, 350 ms release, starting 80 ms before the line), so it sits ~18 dB
  under the -15 LUFS voice and rises to ~11 dB under it in the pauses. Line timing comes from voice.json, so
  the duck is exact rather than detected.
- **Game under the voice:** -27 LUFS, ducked 4 dB during lines, so on-screen sounds land in the gaps and still
  read.
- **Voice clarity chain** (ffmpeg, before the level): high-pass 80 Hz (rumble, plosives), -2 dB at 250 Hz (TTS
  boxiness), +2.5 dB at 3.5 kHz (consonant presence), de-esser, 3:1 compression (threshold -18 dBFS, 5 ms /
  60 ms). These are standard dialogue-mastering moves, kept gentle because Gemini and Kokoro takes are already
  clean.
- **Don't flatten the payoff.** The old chain ran ffmpeg's single-pass `loudnorm` (a dynamic compressor) twice.
  The new chain uses static gains and one limiter. Measured, the payoff's peak momentary loudness sits 5.3 / 5.4 dB
  over the median on runaway / nitwit (5.2 / 3.8 before), and the 0.45 s before it is 9 dB quieter than before. The
  loudness range stays small (1-3 LU) because the narration is continuous, so LRA is not the measure to watch here.
