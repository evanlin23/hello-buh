# Batch 4: blocky-stories premises, adapted to Buh Stories (2026-11-11)

Owner, 2026-11-11: "the blocky-stories we can use their premises right?" Yes: evanlin23/blocky-stories is the
owner's own channel (Buhtown). This page takes its premises, keeps each one's engine (the rule, the two-way clues, the
reveal), and sets it in our world with a naive kid narrator (Pip's voice, `narrator`). **Premises only**, for the owner
and the lead to pick from. Nothing here is canon until it is made.

Mid-run brief change (the lead, from the owner, main 18bc037c / e9c00513):
- **STANDARDS 0x**: the premise doesn't have to be a vanilla mechanic. "Minecraft is just the look." The blocky twist
  is kept as it is. Where a mechanic is used, it is still shown and true.
- **STANDARDS 0w**: line 1 opens with the genre frame: "In my village...", "In my world..." or "My [grandma/aunt]...".

## The process
1. **Read** blocky's RULES.md (the hit study), stories/premise-bank.md (30 ranked + 13 lane premises),
   stories/ideas.md (~110 made, scripted, killed and claimed ideas), lore.md, its story.json premises and twists, and
   uploads.json (22 published). On our side: story_format.md, STANDARDS 0w/0x/0y/0z/0a/5a, canon.md, map.json, and our
   batch 2 and batch 3 pitches (claude/batch3-premises, -round2, -round3) and claude/script-* branches.
2. **Translate.** First pass: the strange rule as a real vanilla mechanic, checked on minecraft.wiki. A web-fetch
   agent read 12 pages; the copper golem, recovery compass, phantoms, zombie-villager cure and wooden-sword repair
   all hold. Then the brief changed (0x), so the second pass keeps blocky's own twist and only dresses it in our world.
3. **Gate: two fresh cold readers per premise, a CASUAL viewer and a Java GAMER.**
   - Each reader got line 1 and the clues, and wrote a midpoint guess and how sure.
   - Only then did it get the spoken reveal line. It answered: right, partly or wrong; surprised; a one-sentence read;
     and a share score out of 10.
   - **Kill rule:** a reader right at 70 % or more, or both readers right at 60 % or more. A casual reader who can't
     explain it in one sentence also kills it.
   - **Round 1:** 15 premises, 8 readers (4 casual, 4 gamer). 3 passed.
   - **Round 2:** 8 round-1 kills with their giveaway clues rewritten as decoys, plus 4 new blocky premises, read by
     6 fresh readers (3 casual, 3 gamer). 6 passed.
4. **Critic.** The story-critic (pitch mode) scored the 9 survivors against the format's blockers (below).

## The honest headline
- **9 of 27 premises passed the gate.** Round 1: 3 of 15. Round 2: 6 of 12.
- **Blocky's own clues give its twists away.** Its scripts are 100-150 s and plant hard, so in a 30 s cut, one clue
  each ("Mom's gloves", "the golden apple", "Mom's nose") was read at 80-90 %. Every round-2 save came from swapping
  that clue for a decoy that points somewhere else plausible. The decoys are listed per premise.
- **What the gamer readers caught:** "zombies can't open doors" draws "actually, on Hard..." comments; zombies don't
  eat carrots. Both clues were cut or reworded below.
- **The one that read wrong in a dangerous way:** R11, Dad and the neighbour faking a feud to share coffee. Both
  round-2 readers read the two men as a secret couple (75 % and 60 %). Blocky's own rule on ep38 ("reads like they are
  not a straight couple even though it should be") applies, so it is dropped.

### A. The Statue Gets Younger (R12)
- **Source:** blocky `ep99-statue-years` ("The Statue That Grows OLD For Us", scripted, never built or published).
- **Line 1:** "In my village, everyone gives the big stone statue one year on their birthday, so nobody gets old. And the
  statue is getting YOUNGER. And nobody will tell me why."
  - Why it hooks: a countable wrongness (a wrinkle a day, gone) on a giant face. The rule is visible: a village with no
    old people, one grey head.
- **Clues (with the kid's misreading):**
  1. "Every morning it has one wrinkle fewer." The rain, Dad says.
  2. "Mom slips out before sunrise every day." Morning walks, she says.
  3. "Dad's the only one in the village with grey hair. He never gave the statue a single year." Stubborn, Mom says.
  4. "This morning Mom had a grey hair. She hid it from me." (This is the decoy: the kid reads it as Mom being sick or
     scared. Readers read it as Mom paying Dad's share.)
- **Reveal (spoken):** "Mom was taking her years back, one every morning, so she could grow old next to Dad."
- **Sting / loop:** Mom and Dad on the bench, both grey. The kid: "And the statue's getting younger."
- **Someone loved at stake:** Mom (the kid thinks she's ill).
- **The rule turned against itself:** the gift can be taken back.
- **Place / day / link:** the High Meadow's top, west of Larkfield (unclaimed land), a small new hamlet around a
  player-built stone statue, day 3900. Larkfield's roofs and bell are in sight to the east, before its torches
  (day 4500). It leaves the statue, which later High Meadow stories can show.
- **Engine needs:** a block-built statue face with a wrinkle line removed per shot (a set change per day, no new
  feature). Grey-haired villager variants: a skin or texture tint on Mom and Dad (new, about 1 h).
- **Cold reads:**
  - casual: guessed "Mom is GIVING her years", 70 %, **wrong**. Surprised: **yes**. Read: "Mom is quietly taking back
    the years she gave so she can grow old with stubborn Dad." Share 9.
  - gamer: guessed she covers Dad's share, 35 %, confused. **Wrong**, surprised: **yes**. Read: "Mom has been taking
    her donated years back so she can age alongside Dad." Share 8.
  - Round 1 version (clue "she LAUGHED at her first grey hair") was guessed at 60 % and 70 %. The fix was the decoy.

### B. The Bell Under the Village (I02)
- **Source:** blocky `ep98-town-whistle` ("Our Town Hides When The WHISTLE Blows", scripted, not built or published).
  The whistle becomes a village bell.
- **Line 1:** "In my village, when the bell rings, everyone hides in their cellar. And every time, our cellar gets one
  step DEEPER. And nobody will tell me why."
  - Why it hooks: a safety ritual plus a countable wrongness (13 stairs, then 26).
- **Clues:**
  1. "Nobody's ever seen who rings it." Breakfast, midnight, any time.
  2. "We had 13 stairs. Now we have 26." The house is settling, Dad says.
  3. "Grandpa's house is the only one with no cellar. When the bell rings, he just makes tea." (Decoy: readers suspect
     Grandpa rings it.)
  4. "Last time, at the bottom, I heard something breathing." Dad snoring, I think.
- **Reveal (spoken):** "Nobody rings the bell. The thing under the village does. Every time we go down, it pulls our
  stairs one step closer."
- **The rule turned against itself (blocky's climax):** the kid climbs the tower and rings it from the top while
  everyone stays upstairs: its own call drags the stairs the other way. One beat, then cut on the bell.
- **Fix from the reads:** pay off Grandpa's tea in one line ("Grandpa built his house on stone. It can't call him.").
  Show the stair count on screen (a sign or chalk marks) so "pulls the stairs" is a picture, not a phrase.
- **Someone loved at stake:** Dad, who goes down first every time.
- **Place / day / link:** a new snowy village in the White at (-1998, -994) (unclaimed in canon), "Coldhollow", day
  2400. It leaves the bell, which rings by itself from then on: a stamp later White stories can hear. Nearest place:
  Ravenford, about 500 blocks south.
- **Engine needs:** a cellar staircase rebuilt longer per scene (set change). A bell that swings with no one at it:
  the bell block's ring animation, triggered by a stamp (check that `renderer/` draws the bell swing). Dark cellar
  lighting.
- **Cold reads:**
  - casual: "something lives under the village, they dig closer", 35 %. **Partly**, surprised a little. Read: "the
    monster rings its own alarm so families climb down to it." Share 6, with notes that "it pulls our stairs" is hard to
    picture and Grandpa's tea isn't paid off (both fixed above).
  - gamer: "Grandpa rings it, or a warden below", 35 %. **Partly**, surprised: **yes**. Read: "a creature under the
    village rings the bell itself and pulls them one step closer each time." Share 8.

### C. My Brother's Bell (I06)
- **Source:** blocky `ep49-car-wash` ("the bell on your collar rings before every mistake", scripted, not built or
  published).
- **Line 1:** "In my village, a little bell on your collar rings right before every mistake. My big brother's rings ALL
  DAY. Mine has NEVER rung. And nobody will tell me why."
  - Why it hooks: a visible marker on every character (blocky's rule 2) and a count (all day vs. never).
- **Clues:**
  1. "He says it's because I'm perfect." I think it's because I never try anything.
  2. "Every time I'm about to jump the creek, HIS bell rings." He's such a mess.
  3. "He keeps one hand in his pocket all the time. Even when he swims."
  4. "Tomorrow he leaves for the city, and I'm scared to do anything without him."
- **Reveal (spoken):** "In his pocket was a tiny clapper. From MY bell. Every ring I blamed on him was mine."
- **Loop:** the kid's own bell, clapper back in, jangles as the kid jumps the creek anyway. "Mine has never rung."
  becomes the opposite.
- **Fix from the reads:** the gamer asked why the clapper rings for the kid's mistakes. Plant it once: "Your bell
  knows you, Grandma says. Not the bell, the clapper."
- **Someone loved at stake:** the big brother, leaving.
- **Place / day / link:** Tidewater, the fishing hamlet, day 2800. The creek runs to the sea under the lighthouse on
  the rise, after One Block Higher (2600, in progress).
- **Engine needs:** a tiny brass bell on every collar: a small item model on the villager/player chest (new, about
  2 h, docs/engine_features.md). The bell's ring SFX, pitched up for the kid's.
- **Cold reads:**
  - casual: "brother has the clapper from my bell", 60 %. **Right**, not surprised. Read: "my brother secretly took
    on all my mistakes so I'd feel brave, and now he's leaving." Share 7.
  - gamer: "the bells were swapped", 65 %. **Partly**, surprised a little. Read: "big brother took the clapper out of
    the kid's bell, so every 'mess' the kid blamed on him was the kid's own." Share **9**.
