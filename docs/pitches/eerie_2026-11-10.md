# Eerie mysteries: 15 pitches, 2026-11-10 (writers' room, lane A)

Owner's brief, 2026-11-10: "create more stories, the more creative the better. it must have that 'why tf?' and
curiosity pulling people in, and a good buildup (with foreshadowing, not too obvious) and clues and a good payoff".

**This lane:** quiet dread, fair-play clues, a twist that recontextualises line 1, and a vanilla mechanic as the key
to the mystery. No gore. None of these reuses a made, claimed or pitched premise (checked against `stories/*`, the
`claude/story-*` branches, the premise bank in `docs/stories.md` and `docs/story_pitches_2026-11-10.md`); none uses
"the narrator is the mob".

**Scoring.** The premise test (`docs/story_craft.md` section 1), 0-2 each: **Img** one image, **Rule** breaks a rule the
viewer knows, **Q** the question asks itself, **#** countable, **Ans** the answer changes the picture. Then 1-10:
**Tw** twist, **Orig** originality, **Bld** buildability (checked against `renderer/entity_models.json` and
`docs/lessons.md`: witches, pillagers, ravagers, phantoms, cats, item frames, chests, lightning, name tags render;
mob armor, map contents, the Glowing outline and the creaking don't), **Fit** owner fit. Verdict per the
`story-critic` rule: develop at 8+ on the test with nothing under 6. No critic agent could be spawned in this run, so
the writer scored strictly against runaway (image 10, twist 10); the next run should hand this table to a fresh
`story-critic`.

## The pitches

Each: the one-image hook, the "why tf?" question, the clues, the twist, why a Minecraft player re-watches.

**1. Monsters Nearby** (developed). *Hook:* an empty, lantern-lit bedroom and the action bar: "You may not rest now;
there are monsters nearby." Nine nights running. *Why tf:* "where tf is the monster? the room is empty." *Rule:* a bed
refuses you if a hostile mob is within 8 blocks; lightning turns a villager into a witch. *Clues:* a torch ring
marking the 8 blocks he searched (floor, attic, well, nothing); Grandma "hasn't been the same since the storm" (burned
roof, new lightning rod); she giggles at her cauldron (the witch's sound); a black cat; she never sleeps; she drank
something red and her burn healed; "I'll sit right here, where I can watch you." *Twist:* he sends her to the
neighbors to keep her safe; as she walks past eight blocks the bed lets him sleep, and lightning shows her at the gate:
a witch. *Re-watch:* the empty chair beside the bed in shot 1, "Monsters? In MY house, dear?", the giggle.

**2. Thirty-One Raids** (developed). *Hook:* villagers throwing bread at an old man crossing the square at dawn: he
has saved them from 31 raids. *Why tf:* "why tf does every raid start the night grandpa gets home?" *Rule:* an
ominous bottle gives Bad Omen, and walking into a village with it starts a raid; heroes get thrown gifts. *Clues:* "he
says they follow him"; a wall of 31 totems with one empty frame; a table set for two since Grandma died; he hunts at
the pillager tower; a chest of grey bottles he calls medicine; he drinks it at the gate; "Lock the door, kid. They're
coming." *Twist:* too tired to fight, Grandpa can't; the boy drinks the medicine to be brave like him; the raid horn
sounds; Grandpa holds out his crossbow: "Now they'll love YOU too, kid." *Re-watch:* L01 becomes a confession; the
empty 32nd frame; "They know who BEATS them."

**3. The Pale Tree.** *Hook:* a pale, dead-looking tree standing in the middle of the village road, a little closer
to our door every morning; old Mrs. Hale has sat at her window facing it every night for 40 years. *Why tf:* "why tf
does that tree move, and why won't she stop staring at it?" *Rule:* the creaking (1.21.4+) only moves when no player
is looking at it. *Clues:* she sleeps by day; the tree has a face-like knot; dropped tools by the road; she asks me to
"keep her company" on long nights; one night her lamp goes out. *Twist:* she's been holding a creaking frozen with her
eyes for 40 years; tonight she fell asleep, and it's at our door; now it's my turn to watch. *Re-watch:* every shot
she's in, she's looking out the window. **Engine first:** creaking model, pale oak and pale moss textures.

**4. Boots on the Lake.** *Hook:* a pair of glinting diamond boots walking by themselves across a black lake at night,
ice forming under each step. *Why tf:* "why tf are boots walking on the lake?" *Rule:* invisibility hides your body but
not your armor; Frost Walker freezes water; golems attack players who've hurt villagers. *Clues:* the golem stands at
the dock facing the lake every night; Mom leaves a bottle on the dock; Dad "had to leave" after the fight with the
butcher. *Twist:* it's Dad, invisible so the golem can't see him, visiting the house every night; the potion runs out
on the dock. *Re-watch:* the golem's stare, Mom's bottle. **Engine first:** armor on mobs and players.

**5. Sit.** *Hook:* Grandpa's dog sitting on our porch, collar faded, for twelve years. *Why tf:* "why tf did a dog
that's sat for twelve years just stand up?" *Rule:* a sitting tamed wolf only stands for its owner. *Clues:* the dog
growls at no one; Grandpa "went to sea"; the porch boards worn under its paws. *Twist (weak):* Grandpa is back; nobody
has found a turn better than that. *Re-watch:* not yet. Keep the hook; find a twist.

**6. The Name in the Dark.** *Hook:* at night a name, GRANDPA, floats down the village street by itself, two llamas
on leads beneath it. *Why tf:* "why tf is a name walking down our street?" *Rule:* wandering traders drink
invisibility at dusk and milk at dawn; a name tag still shows on an invisible mob; name-tagged mobs never despawn.
*Clues:* Grandpa only trades, never talks; he sells me sticks for emeralds; he vanishes at sunset, drinks milk at
dawn; Grandma's anvil. *Twist:* when the real Grandpa died, Grandma name-tagged a wandering trader "Grandpa" so he'd
never leave. *Re-watch:* every "grandpa" habit is a trader mechanic. Too funny for this lane; good for a lighter one.

**7. The Zombies Go to the Beach.** *Hook:* a line of zombies walking straight through the village at night, past
every door, down to the beach. *Why tf:* "why tf do the zombies ignore us?" *Rule:* zombies seek out and trample turtle
eggs. *Clues:* Grandma breeds turtles in the cove; eggshells on the sand every morning. *Twist:* she lays a line of
eggs every evening as a lure; tonight there are no eggs left. An answer, not a turn.

**8. The Cat on the Chest.** *Hook:* a black cat that has sat on the old mason's chest for twelve years; nobody can open
it. *Why tf:* "why tf won't that cat move?" *Rule:* a cat on a chest stops it opening; a sitting tamed cat only moves
for its owner. *Clues:* the chest's latch is red (a trapped chest); a hum under the floor. *Twist:* the chest is wired
to TNT under the square; the mason sat his cat on it before he died. The payoff is a blast: runaway's ending.

**9. Green Sparks Sink.** *Hook:* when I kill a zombie, the green experience sparks don't fly to me; they sink into the
ground under my feet. *Why tf:* "why tf does my XP go into the ground?" *Rule:* experience orbs fly to the nearest
player. *Clues:* scraping at night; my chests reorganised. *Twist:* Dad, "lost in the mine", has lived in a tunnel
under every path I walk. An answer.

**10. Two Arrows.** *Hook:* my map shows two arrows. I live alone. *Why tf:* "who tf is the other arrow?" *Rule:* a
map shows every player carrying a copy of it, flat, with no height. *Clues:* the second arrow sits on mine when I'm
home and lags a step when I walk. *Twist:* someone is directly beneath me. **Engine first:** map contents and markers.

**11. The Loot at the Cliff.** *Hook:* every morning a pile of armor and tools spins at the foot of the cliff, gone by
noon. *Why tf:* "who tf dies there every night?" *Rule:* death drops despawn in five minutes; players respawn at their
bed. *Clues:* Dad comes home every dawn without his armor; his bed is the only one that's warm at sunrise. *Twist:*
Dad dies there every night. Killed: whatever the reason, it reads as self-harm.

**12. The Furnace in the Empty House.** *Hook:* a lit furnace glowing in a house nobody has lived in for nine years.
*Why tf:* "who tf keeps the furnace going?" *Rule:* a furnace only burns while it smelts. *Twist:* a hopper feeds it
from below (a forgotten auto-cooker, or someone under the house). Every version deflates or repeats #9.

**13. The Quiet Village.** *Hook:* every path and floor in the village is wool, and everyone whispers when I'm around.
*Why tf:* "why tf does everyone shush only me?" *Rule:* sculk shriekers answer only players; the fourth shriek
summons the warden. *Twist:* only my noise can wake it; at the festival I laugh a fourth time. Runaway's spine (my joy
dooms everyone).

**14. Fish in the Sky.** *Hook:* fish swimming across the village's sky. *Rule:* a player-built glass dome on the
seabed. *Twist:* we live under the sea; I break the "sky". A player reads it from frame 1.

**15. Every Clock Spins.** *Hook:* every clock and compass in the village spins; beds are forbidden. *Rule:* clocks
and compasses spin, and beds explode, outside the Overworld. *Twist:* we're in the End. Obvious from the first frame.

## Notes for the next writer
- The strongest pattern in this lane is **a sleep, a sound or an item the player already knows, used as evidence**:
  the bed's refusal message (#1), the witch's giggle (#1), the ominous bottle (#2). They give fair-play clues a
  Minecraft player can solve and a non-player can still follow.
- #3 (The Pale Tree) is the best premise here on paper; it waits on the engine. #4 too.
- #5 (Sit) has the cleanest hook in the table and no twist yet. Worth a room of its own.
