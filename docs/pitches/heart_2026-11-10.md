# Pitches, 2026-11-10: mysteries with an emotional twist (writers' room, heart lane)

Owner's brief: "create more stories, the more creative the better. it must have that 'why tf?' and curiosity pulling
people in, and a good buildup (with foreshadowing, not too obvious) and clues and a good payoff". This lane: a strange
image and a real mystery whose answer hits the heart (love, loss, loyalty, sacrifice), earned by planted clues, never
sappy. Fifteen original premises, scored with the premise test (`docs/story_craft.md` section 1) and the `story-critic`
pitch rubric; the top two are developed to full scripts in `stories/bed_in_the_square/` and `stories/stand_up/`.

**How this was scored.** No critic agent could be spawned in this run, so the writer applied the rubric strictly, with
runaway as the bar (image 10, twist 10). The next step for both scripts is the table read (`/story-write` step 6: a cold
reader, `story-critic` in script mode, `story-continuity` in pre-build mode). Originality was checked against everything
on main and the `claude/story-*` / `agent/*` branches (runaway, respawn_tax, put_back, same_spot, sign_by_bed, nitwit,
sheep_stares, chunk_next_door, more_golems, one_block_higher, one_more_eye, respawn_voice, sun_wont_set), the premise
bank in `docs/stories.md`, and the ten in `docs/story_pitches_2026-11-10.md` (none duplicated; one of their parked
pitches gets a suggested fix at the end, not counted here). Mechanics were checked against 1.21.11's `en_us.json`
(the world-border death message is "%1$s left the confines of this world") and buildability against
`renderer/entity_models.json` and `docs/lessons.md`.

## The fifteen

Each: the image (line 1's picture), the question, the clues, the twist, why a Minecraft player re-watches.

**1. The Bed in the Square** *(develop: `stories/bed_in_the_square/`)*
- **Image:** a single red bed alone in the middle of a cobbled village square, villagers curving round it. "It's been
  there forty years."
- **Clues:** nobody may sleep in it or build near it (they moved the well instead); Grandma makes it every morning;
  "If it's BLOCKED, he wakes up somewhere ELSE"; Grandpa Walt walked east to see where the world ends, along a line of
  torches that runs to the fog; "He never slept in another bed. Not once."; Dad: "There IS no end, Ma. He's gone."
- **Twist:** the bed is his respawn point and he never moved it. At dawn, as Dad comes with an axe and the narrator
  blocks him, the chat says "Walt left the confines of this world" (the world border's own death message): he reached
  the edge, and the edge sent him home. He appears beside the bed with nothing in his hands. Grandma: "You're late."
- **Rewatch:** two rules every player has lived (your bed is your respawn; the border kills) become a love story where
  dying is the fastest way home; L04, L06 and Dad's "there is no end" all flip.

**2. Stand Up** *(develop: `stories/stand_up/`)*
- **Image:** a queue of thirty players snaking across a town square to a little roofed plot where a grey wolf sits.
  "Four hundred players have tried to make this dog stand up."
- **Clues:** ten years sitting; only the owner can stand a sitting dog; the narrator is back after ten years to find
  his first house, a lime-roofed dirt hut on the hill the dog faces; the collar is lime (only an owner dyes a collar);
  the name tag says BESTFREND ("some kid couldn't spell"); Pia, the kid who runs the line: "Somebody told him SIT. And
  never came BACK."
- **Twist:** it's his dog. He clicks, it stands. "I told him I'd be back tomorrow."
- **Rewatch:** every player has gone back to an old world and found their dog still sitting there; this makes you the
  one who left. L08 and L11 ("I almost didn't BOTHER") are brutal the second time.

**3. Three Hundred Lightning Rods**
- **Image:** a village bristling with copper lightning rods, twenty on every roof, glinting in a storm.
- **Clues:** Dad climbs up to check them in every storm; Mom "left during the big storm"; a witch lives in the swamp
  hut at the edge of the village and stands at the treeline watching our window; she never throws a potion at me.
- **Twist:** lightning turns a villager into a witch (vanilla). The witch is Mom; Dad built the rods so it never
  happens to anyone else.
- **Why park:** a 10 on the premise test, but it's the third "the monster is family" (chunk_next_door, golem_inside,
  more_golems), and a witch-mom who attacks players is dark. Revive it only with a twist that isn't about who the
  witch is (and there's no vanilla cure for a witch, so there's no hopeful ending to aim for).

**4. The Golem Walks Into the Sea**
- **Image:** an iron golem wading into the waves at sunset while the village watches; 300 nights.
- **Clues:** it comes back each dawn more cracked (cracks show its health); I repair it with my iron every morning;
  no drowned have come ashore in three years; the old village is under the bay.
- **Twist:** it walks the sea floor every night to hold the drowned back at the only path up to the beach.
- **Why park:** the "secret protector" answer explains more than it flips, and it's the same shape as the phantom-roof
  pitch (Dad keeping watch). The best image in the batch for a night shot; it needs a different answer.

**5. The Fox With the Totem**
- **Image:** a fox asleep on our doorstep with a golden totem of undying in its mouth.
- **Clues:** foxes steal things (Dad thinks it stole it off Grandpa); Grandpa had exactly one totem and didn't come
  back from the last raid; when I was little he had me feed sweet berries to two foxes (bred foxes trust the breeder).
- **Twist:** in the next raid the fox throws itself at a vindicator to defend me, dies, and the totem pops: it lives.
  Grandpa gave his only totem to the fox, for me, and went into the raid without it.
- **Why park:** two steps (the fox defends me → so Grandpa gave it the totem on purpose); the "gave, not stole" needs
  its own game-shown proof (vanilla foxes drop a held item for food: this one never does?).

**6. The Trader Who Can't Vanish**
- **Image:** a wandering trader at the well, twelve years of moss on his stall. "Wandering traders stay two days."
- **Clues:** his llamas vanished on schedule; on his first day a girl tapped his shoulder and ran; village kids look a
  little *above* his head when they talk to him; her parents logged off one day and never came back.
- **Twist:** the first third-person shot shows his name tag: DAD. She named him so he could never despawn.
- **Why park:** the best heart twist in the batch, but Java's wandering trader despawns on its own timer, and a name
  tag very likely doesn't stop it (Bedrock differs). If a Java check shows named traders stay, this goes to the top.

**7. Two Beacons**
- **Image:** at nine every night a beacon on our hill turns green, and far across the sea another one turns green back.
- **Clues:** Grandma climbs up with a green pane every night; she and her sister were split by the ocean monument
  between the islands; Grandma died in the winter; I've been changing the glass since.
- **Twist:** I row across at last and find a boy my age at the other beacon: his grandmother died years ago too. We've
  each been keeping the other's grandmother alive.
- **Why park:** two steps and an O. Henry shape; the beacon beam needs engine work.

**8. Turtles in Grandma's Kitchen**
- **Image:** forty sea turtles crawling up the village street and through grandma's kitchen door, every spring.
- **Clues:** turtles return to the beach where they hatched; zombies trample turtle eggs; grandma's kitchen floor is sand.
- **Twist:** she carried the eggs inside the night the zombies came. **Why park:** it explains, it doesn't flip.

**9. The Ice Path**
- **Image:** at night a pair of diamond boots walks across the black lake, freezing a path of ice under them (Frost
  Walker on an invisible player: armor still shows).
- **Twist search:** someone who can't be seen visiting every night; no answer yet that's one step and kind.
- **Why park:** the most original image here; it needs its twist.

**10. The Iron Farm Zombie**
- **Image:** a zombie in a glass box in an abandoned iron farm, three villagers screaming at it, a golem born every few
  minutes and dropping into lava. Narrated by the zombie: "I've made 1.2 million golems. None of them got to live."
- **Why park:** narrator-is-the-mob against runaway, and sad rather than warm; a strong world-abandoned image.

**11. The Death Pile on My Doorstep** *(kill)* Every morning, a player's death drop (stone sword, leather armor, bread,
XP orbs) on my doorstep. Answer: Dad dies holding the door against zombies every night: the phantom-roof answer again.

**12. Goodnight in the Chat** *(kill)* A hardcore world; every night "Mom: goodnight" appears in chat; Mom died two
years ago (hardcore death = spectator: she can watch and type, never touch). The hook is text, and it's grief, not
warmth.

**13. The Golem's Poppy** *(kill)* The golem holds out a poppy to an empty patch of grass every morning. Every answer
needs a memory vanilla golems don't have.

**14. Grandma's Rainbow Sheep** *(kill)* A sheep that cycles every colour (name tag `jeb_`); grandpa named it for her.
Cute, renders already, but the answer is an easter egg, not a turn.

**15. The Cat on the Chest** *(kill)* A cat has sat on grandpa's chest for eleven years (a cat on a chest stops it
opening). The answer is whatever's inside: a told reveal.

## A fix for a parked pitch (not counted above)

`docs/story_pitches_2026-11-10.md` #6, **The Allay's Deliveries**, was parked for want of "a twist that the mechanics
give for free". The mechanic: an allay that hears a note block delivers its items to that note block, not to its
player. So the cookies on my windowsill every morning aren't for me: they're for the note block under my window, played
at sunrise by a daylight sensor Grandma wired before she died, so her allay would keep bringing me cookies forever.
The reveal is the redstone (break the dirt under the windowsill: a daylight sensor, a wire, the note block). Offered to
whoever picks that pitch up.
