# Absurd "why tf" mysteries: pitches, 2026-11-10 (writer B)

Owner's brief, verbatim: "create more stories, the more creative the better. it must have that 'why tf?' and curiosity
pulling people in, and a good buildup (with foreshadowing, not too obvious) and clues and a good payoff".

This lane: the most bizarre single Minecraft image we can invent, played straight and serious, explained by a rule a
Minecraft player already knows, with a payoff that gets a gasp or a laugh. Fifteen premises, scored with the premise
test (`docs/story_craft.md` section 1) and the `story-critic` pitch rubric, then the top two written out in full:
`stories/light_sleeper/` and `stories/spawn_room/`.

**How this was scored.** This run could not spawn a separate `story-critic`, so the writer applied its rubric and its
calibration (runaway = image 10, twist 10; sheep_stares twist 5; respawn_tax image 4) and scored down when in doubt.
Originality was checked against every story on main and on the `claude/story-*`, `agent/*` branches, the premise bank in
`docs/stories.md`, and the ten pitches in `docs/story_pitches_2026-11-10.md` (wall_grows, golem_inside, phantom_roof,
the sky bridge, the librarian's bed, the allay, the kitchen diamond, the desert snow golem, the groaning parrot, the
empty field block). Buildability was checked against `renderer/entity_models.json` (warden with its roar, player,
villager, sheep, wolf, wither, piglin, iron golem, minecart, boat, egg, lightning all render), `render.js` (the Darkness
effect, name plates, mobs' held items, lightning, chat via `edit.py --chat`) and `docs/lessons.md` (item frames with
their item, bells, signs, beds, chests, cake and heads render; mob head-wear and lead ropes are not drawn).

Not taken from the brief's examples: a cow in a pumpkin needs head-wear on animals (vanilla animals can't wear one, and
the renderer draws no head-wear), a chicken laying books has no vanilla rule behind it and chickens still draw badly,
and "a village that only builds upward" had no Minecraft rule we could make the answer from (the carpet village below
is what that idea became: a village whose strangeness is *how* it's built, explained by the game).

## The fifteen

Premise test, 0-2 each: **Img** one image, **Rule** breaks a rule the viewer knows, **Q** the question asks itself, **#**
countable, **Ans** the answer changes the picture. Then 1-10: **Tw** twist, **Orig** originality, **Bld** buildability,
**Fit** owner fit. Verdict per the critic: develop = test 8+ and nothing under 6.

**Critic notes on the field.** The lane's strength is that the *answer* is a rule players learned by playing (wool
and the warden, world spawn, boats, Wither roses), so the payoff feels earned rather than invented. Its weakness is
that the same knowledge solves some of them on frame 1 (the AFK saint, the grandpa boat, the lectern): those are
sketches. The two picked hide their rule behind an image that non-players also find strange, and stage the rule
twice before the reveal, so the player gets ahead in the middle and everyone else at the end.

## Pick 1: Light Sleeper (`stories/light_sleeper/`)

**Logline.** A boy in a village where every inch is carpeted and the bell is wrapped in wool wants to hear one song,
and puts a jukebox on the well.

**The twist.** The carpet is silence: the village sits on an ancient city, and the "glowing moss" at the bottom of the
well is a sculk shrieker. The first song anyone there has ever played calls the warden. The reveal is the game's: the
shrieks, the Darkness pulsing, the warden rising out of the square, "was obliterated by a sonically-charged shriek".

**Why it hooks.** The opening frame (a rainbow carpet village) is strange to everybody; the "why" is answered by a
rule most players know (wool, sculk, the warden), planted in pictures (the wool-boxed bell, the glow down the well)
and in lines that are misread on the first watch: Mom's "The ground's a light sleeper" (a saying), "She said it was a
fox" (foxes do scream), "The music made the lights go dark, like a real party" (the Darkness effect). The player gets
ahead at the scream (~24 s) and spends the second half dreading the jukebox: runaway's bomb under the table, with a
whispered delivery that makes the first loud sound in the Short the reveal. Re-watch: line 1, the bell, Mom's two
lines and "I'd never danced before" all mean something else. 124 words, ~55 s with the reveal.

## Pick 2: Spawn Room (`stories/spawn_room/`)

**Logline.** Forty-one strangers have appeared next to a boy's bed; each walks outside and punches a tree, so the
boy follows a compass to find where they come from, and it leads him home.

**The twist.** His bedroom is world spawn. Every new player in the world starts beside his bed, respawns there after
their first night's death, and punches the nearest tree. The reveal is the game's: the compass needle points at the
bed, a stranger appears on that exact block, and the chat fills with "joined the game" as twelve more arrive at once.

**Why it hooks.** The image is a home-invasion horror frame (a blank stranger by a child's bed) that turns into a
laugh, and it's built from the most universal Minecraft memories there are: your first spawn, punching a tree,
dying the first night, the floating trees every new player leaves behind, going back to visit spawn. Grandpa's lines
("Everybody starts SOMEWHERE", "Compasses point HOME, kid") are literally true. A player gets ahead at the floating
trees and the compasses; the reveal still lands because of its scale (the flood) and the sting ("We're gonna need more
TREES"). 110 words, ~48 s with the reveal.
