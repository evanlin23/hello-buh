# Story arcs: the shapes the best story Shorts use

Owner, 2026-11-10: *"this one also has a loop. can we look at the story arcs for the best stories in this area? we can
try to make our stories based on those then"*.

This page breaks down the model Short the owner sent, and the others we could read (`docs/story_research_mystery.md`
section 1). It then gives the recurring arc templates, with beat timing for ~35 s, the loop mechanics, and why each
works. The mystery test (STANDARDS 1.0) still applies to every template.

**We describe other people's scripts by structure only.** We never commit their text.

## 1. The model: "I don't have a twin" (the owner's pick, a top Roblox story Short)

**The numbers:**
- 196 words in 35.6 s, about **5.5 words/s** overall;
- one narrator voices every quote;
- captions are 2-3 words with one green key word, and quotes keep their marks.

| t (s) | words | beat | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0-2.4 | 13 | **paradox hook** | Line 1 contradicts itself ("I don't have a twin", said at the twin's wedding). The question is "how?". It also secretly states the ending: it's the line he says at the climax. |
| 2.4-5.3 | 16 | **inciting accident** | "It started when..." flashes back to one small, embarrassing, physical moment in front of the person he likes. |
| 5.3-7.8 | 14 | **the lie (the engine)** | He blames an invented twin, and "So I kept doing it" turns one lie into a rule. |
| 7.8-12.5 | 26 | **refrain escalation** | Three or four mishaps, each answered by the same one-word refrain (the twin's name), then a capper line. It's comic rhythm: setup, refrain; setup, refrain. Each item is under 8 words. |
| 12.5-14.2 | 9 | **turn 1: backfire** | The crush prefers the invented twin. The lie now costs him. |
| 14.2-19.8 | 31 | **commit deeper** | A disguise and a date as the twin. A mishap again, and she loves it. "She never laughed like that with me." |
| 19.8-20.7 | 5 | **the irony line** | Five words that name the paradox ("jealous of myself"). It's the quotable one. |
| 20.7-24.0 | 18 | **turn 2: the plan backfires** | He tries to end it, and the attempt escalates it (a proposal meant to scare her; she says yes). |
| 24.0-27.1 | 17 | **the peak** | The whole world is watching. He's in two roles at once. |
| 27.1-31.6 | 25 | **reversal** | A character's line reveals the truth was always the other way: she liked *him*, from the very first accident (the inciting beat pays off). |
| 31.6-35.6 | 22 | **climax, cut mid-sentence** | The moment of decision ("speak now"). He takes off the disguise, breathes, "and said," and the video ends. On replay, the first words ARE what he said. |

**Why it works:**
1. The question is set by a contradiction, which a viewer can't scroll past.
2. Every beat is a *consequence* of the last ("So I...", "Then she..."). Nothing is a list of facts.
3. The refrain gives rhythm and a laugh every ~1.2 s.
4. Two backfires escalate the stakes.
5. The reversal re-reads the inciting beat (the first accident was the moment she fell for him).
6. The cut mid-sentence on the climax forces the replay, and the replay *answers* the question line 1 asked.

The mystery test holds:
- the question comes by 3 s;
- at the midpoint you can guess (he'll have to confess) but not be sure how;
- the reversal is one sentence;
- the cut is on the climax.

**The loop mechanics:** the last line is an unfinished speech verb ("...took a deep breath, and said,"). Line 1 is the
object of that verb. Played in a loop it reads as one sentence. Line 1 has to be the climax's dialogue, so write the
ending first, then make its last spoken words the hook.

## 3. The templates

Every template gets the same five fixed parts:
- a **line 1** that asks a question;
- **consequence links** ("So...", "Then...", "But...") between beats, never "and also";
- **one character line** of six words or fewer, literally true;
- the **turn at 65-85 %**;
- the **cut ≤1 s after the reveal**, ideally mid-sentence into line 1.

Target **150-190 words for 30-35 s**, in short sentences. The speed comes from the writing, not from stretching the
take (STANDARDS 2.3).

### T1. The Paradox Loop (the owner's model)
- **Hook:** a contradiction that is literally the ending's line. "I've never ___. That's what I said when ___."
- **Beats (35 s):**
  - 0-2.5 the paradox;
  - 2.5-5 "It started when..." (one small physical moment);
  - 5-8 the rule or lie the narrator invents;
  - 8-13 a refrain escalation of 3-4 items;
  - 13-15 backfire 1;
  - 15-20 commit deeper;
  - 20-21 the irony line;
  - 21-24 backfire 2;
  - 24-27 the peak, the whole world watching;
  - 27-32 the reversal (a character's line re-reads the inciting moment);
  - 32-35 the climax, cut mid-sentence.
- **Loop:** the final speech verb, then line 1.
- **Why:** a contradiction is the strongest curiosity gap; the refrain is rhythm; two backfires escalate; the replay
  answers the question.
- **Minecraft fit:** a rule the narrator invents and has to keep up (blaming the creeper, a name tag, a fake twin
  skin).

### T2. The Town Rule (Light Sleeper, Gneef's "In this town")
- **Hook:** "In my village, ___" (a rule or a custom, with a number).
- **Beats:**
  - 0-3 the rule;
  - 3-8 how far it goes (2-3 examples that escalate);
  - 8-11 the plant: a character's literal line;
  - 11-16 the one time it was broken, and what happened (the bomb);
  - 16-22 the want: the narrator needs to break it;
  - 22-28 he breaks it, and the signs mount;
  - 28-32 the game reveals the reason;
  - cut.
- **Loop:** cause and effect. The reveal is why line 1's rule exists. Or end mid-action: "So I..." into line 1.
- **Why:** the rule is the question; the plant is literally true; the viewer dreads the breaking.

### T3. The Sinister Reread (Tyler's 7.45M)
- **Hook:** a damning fact told flatly ("He adopted ten kids. None came out alive." / "Grandpa has broken the same
  lectern 3,000 times.").
- **Beats:** each clue reads two ways (sinister, and literally kind); the turn at ~75 % is the kind truth; one beat;
  out.
- **Loop:** line 1 re-read with the truth in mind.
- **Why:** the viewer judges, then is made to re-judge. The emotional flip is the payoff.

### T4. Who Am I (runaway)
- **Hook:** "Everyone ___ when they see ME" (how the world treats the narrator).
- **Beats:**
  - an escalating list (a refrain of how each one reacts);
  - a loving, misread line from family;
  - a picture clue misread as affection;
  - the bomb (a sensation he describes and doesn't understand);
  - the want;
  - the game reveals what he is.
- **Loop:** a question back to line 1 ("Why does that ALWAYS happen?").
- **Why:** the answer is about the narrator; POV hides it in plain sight. Use at most twice (the narrator-is-the-mob
  cap).

### T5. The Countdown Cliffhanger (Ryex's ending, The Walk)
- **Hook:** a long, strange effort with a number (3,000 lecterns; 22 years).
- **Beats:**
  - the effort escalates;
  - the reason, in a character's literal line;
  - the moment it finally works;
  - cut *on the first second of the payoff*, before any reaction.
- **Loop:** the payoff is the first step of line 1's effort ending ("...and Grandpa stepped off the dock. My
  grandpa..."). Or end on a new open question.
- **Why:** years of effort make one second of payoff feel huge, and the cut leaves the viewer finishing it.

## 4. How we use them
- **Pick a template before the premise test.** Pitch tables get an `arc` column.
- **Write the ending first.**
  - In T1, the climax's spoken words are line 1.
  - In T2, the reveal is the rule's reason.
- **Count words per beat against the template's clock.** The take must still be continuous and natural
  (STANDARDS 2b.4). If 190 words won't fit at a natural pace, cut beats, never squeeze.
- **The cold reader checks the loop:** read the last line and line 1 together. Do they make one sentence, or cause
  and effect?
